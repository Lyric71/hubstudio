# How to run a social media client approval workflow without the email chain

Client approval on social content breaks on two questions: which version the
client approved, and whether the post changed after they said yes.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What does a social media client approval workflow need?

One thread per post, with every version and every decision on it, and a post
that cannot move while the client decides. In hubStudio, the client signs in
to see only the work made for their company, approves it or sends it back
with a comment, and nothing on the thread can be deleted.

Here is where an email chain and a single approval thread part ways.

| Question that starts the dispute | Email chain | One approval thread |
|---|---|---|
| Which version did they approve? | The attachment above the reply, if nobody renamed or forwarded it | A numbered version tab, v1 or v2, marked Validated with the date |
| Did it change while they decided? | Nothing stops an edit | The post is locked: no change, no delete, no publish |
| Who saw what? | Everyone copied, and anyone they forwarded to | The client's people see only their company's work |
| Where is the record? | Spread across inboxes, and deletable | One page per piece; no version, comment or decision can be deleted |

<!-- SECTION: Where email breaks -->

## Where does client approval by email break down?

At those two questions, and nobody asks them until something has gone wrong.

The first is which version was approved. Files get renamed, forwarded and
replaced over a week of replies. A "looks good" sits under whichever
attachment happened to be above it. Reconstructing the answer means reading
the thread backward.

The second is whether anything changed after the yes. An email reply approves
a file at a moment. Nothing in the inbox stops someone fixing a typo, swapping
a picture or editing the caption after that moment, and nothing records it.

One benchmark puts a number on how many marketers feel it.

> In an annual benchmark survey of 980 B2B marketers, mostly in North
> America, 33 percent named managing workflow issues or the content
> approvals process as a challenge outside content creation, down from 41
> percent the year before. The survey does not print the base of the
> percentage beside the figure.
> Source: annual B2B content marketing benchmark survey, published October
> 2024, online questionnaire fielded June 25 to August 16, 2024.

The fix is a record that cannot be edited and a post that cannot move while
it waits. The rest of this guide sets that loop up in the hubStudio app.

<!-- SECTION: Set up the client -->

## How do you give a client a login that shows only their work?

An admin sets up each client company once, on the Team page.

- On the Clients card, type the company's name under New client and click
  Add the client. Two clients of one team can't share a name.
- Under the client's name, type a person's first name, last name and email,
  then click Give them a login. They get an invitation by email with the
  role Client, valid for a week.
- Tag the work. Pick the client in Made for: in the image or video studio
  before the run, on a History card at any time, or on a post in a network
  module.

![The Clients card: the New client field, a client with its Rename and Delete the client buttons, one login with Pause login, and the form to give its people a login](/Images/help/your-team-clients.webp "Each client gets its own small form for logins.")

Nothing reaches a client until it is tagged for them. Your team keeps seeing
everything it made, whoever it was made for. Pause login stops one person
signing in; Let back in restores them.

The app charges no subscription and no seat fees, so a login for every
approver on the client's side costs nothing.

<!-- SECTION: What the client sees -->

## What does the client see when they sign in?

Only what you made for their company. The page is titled Made for and the
company's name, newest first and grouped by day, with tabs for All, Images,
Videos, Posts and Files.

An image or a video shows as a card with Download. A post takes a whole row:
the network, its title and text, its pictures, and where it stands, whether
Published, Planned for a date or last Updated.

![A client login on its Client space: the menu holds Client space and Validation, the page is titled Made for and the company name, with the All, Images, Videos, Posts and Files tabs and nothing shared yet](/Images/help/client-space-home.webp "A client login lands on its Client space, with two entries in the menu.")

What the client never sees matters as much. The prompts, the engines and the
costs stay with your team. A client login holds no balance and sees no
prices. When it downloads a file, the download is charged against your team
balance, like any download. Its menu holds two entries, Client space and
Validation.

When you need a decision, a card at the top of their space lists what waits,
and they get an email with a link.

<!-- SECTION: Send for approval -->

## How do you send a post to the client for approval?

From wherever you made it. Send for validation sits on a ready tab of the
image studio, under each picture, on a ready tab of the video studio, and on
the publishing step of a post in each network module.

- Check the Title. Name the network and the day it should go out.
- Pick the Validator: a teammate, or one of the client's people, listed under
  Client: and the company's name.
- Add a Message to the validator: what to look at, the context, the deadline.
- Click Send for validation. The thread opens in a new tab, and the validator
  is emailed.

![The Send for validation dialog: Title, Type, Validator, the asset to upload, paste or link, and an optional message](/Images/help/validation-send-dialog.webp "A title, a validator, the piece, and a message if you want one.")

The message is free text, and it does the work a cover email used to do.

```prompt Message to the validator, an example
First version of Thursday's launch post for LinkedIn: the copy and one
picture. Please check the product name in the first line and the link at
the end. We need your decision by Wednesday noon to schedule it for
Thursday morning.
```

A deadline written there is a sentence for the validator to read, not a date
the app tracks. Something made outside the app (a caption in a document, a
link to a microsite) goes through Send an asset for validation on the
Validation page.

A piece made for one client can only go to that client's people or to a
teammate. Name someone from another client and the app refuses: "This piece
was made for another client. Ask one of that client's people, or a
colleague."

<!-- SECTION: The thread -->

## What happens on the approval thread?

Everything about one piece stays on one page. Tabs at the top hold each
version, v1, v2 and so on, with (current) beside the latest. Each says when
it was submitted and by whom, then when it was Validated or Sent back. On the
right, the Thread lists every event and comment in order.

The client decides there. Validate approves the version. Send back asks for
changes, and it won't go without a comment, so "not quite" always comes with
a reason. Each version gets one decision.

You answer a send-back with Submit a new version, on the same page, and say
What changed. The status returns to Waiting for validation. Sending the same
piece again never opens a second thread; it adds a version.

![The Validation page titled Assets to validate, with the Waiting for me, My requests and All assets tabs and the Send an asset for validation button](/Images/help/validation-page.webp "Three tabs: what waits for you, what you sent, and everything your team sent.")

Every step sends an email: the validator on each new version, you on each
decision, everyone on the thread on each comment. A client's colleague can
comment on their company's threads even when someone else is the validator.

<!-- SECTION: The lock -->

## How do you stop a post changing while the client decides?

You don't have to. A post sent for validation shows Waiting for validation in
its network module, and the band reads: "Waiting for validation: this post is
locked until the validator decides."

Until then, nobody on your team can change it, delete it or publish it. That
includes the colleague who wants to fix the caption while the client is still
reading.

Approved, the post can go out. Sent back, it returns to draft for another
round, and the next version goes on the same thread.

<!-- SECTION: Roles -->

## Who can do what in the approval loop?

hubStudio has four roles. Settle this table before you invite the client,
because one cell trips teams up.

| Action | Admin | Creator | Viewer | Client |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Send a piece, submit a new version | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Comment on a thread | Yes | Yes | No | Own company's threads |
| Validate or send back | Yes, in the validator's place | When named validator | No, even when named | When named validator |
| Change the validator | Yes | When requester or validator | No | No |

That cell is the Viewer's. A Viewer can be named validator but can't record a
decision, so the piece sits until an admin steps in. Name a creator, an admin
or one of the client's people instead.

<!-- SECTION: The record -->

## Can anyone delete an approval or rewrite the record?

No. Nobody can withdraw a request or delete a thread, a version or a
comment. The history is kept on purpose. If a request is no longer needed,
say so in the thread.

There are two safety valves. An admin can decide in the validator's place
when the validator is away. The requester, the validator or
an admin can name another validator; on a client's thread, the list offers
your teammates and that client's people.

<!-- SECTION: Cost -->

## What does client approval cost in hubStudio?

Nothing for the round trip itself, and no AI is involved. Two things around
it are billed against the prepaid balance as usual: a file you upload to a
thread is stored with your team's files and billed as storage, and opening or
downloading a file from the thread is billed like any download.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do agencies get client approval on social media posts?

Give the client's people their own logins and send each post to one of them
on a single thread. In hubStudio, an admin adds the client under Clients,
gives its people the Client role, and tags work Made for that client. Send
for validation on the post's publishing step names the client's person, who
validates it or sends it back with a comment.

### How do I stop a post being changed after the client approved it?

Approve a numbered version, not a file in an inbox. Each decision in hubStudio
is tied to a version tab, v1, v2 and so on, with its date, so the approval
points at the version the client opened. While the post waits, it is locked.
To change it after the yes, send it again: it lands on the same thread as a
new version for the client to decide on.

### Can a client approve posts without seeing our other clients' work?

Yes. A client login sees only what was made for its own company, in its
Client space, and only its own company's threads in Validation. A piece made
for one client can't be sent to another client's people: the app refuses it
and asks for one of that client's people or a colleague.

### What happens when a client sends a post back?

They must write a comment to send it back, so the reason is on the record.
The post returns to draft, you are emailed, and Submit a new version opens on
the same page. You add the new version and say what changed. The status goes
back to Waiting for validation, and the client is emailed again.

### Can the client see what the content cost or how it was made?

No. The prompts, the engines and the costs stay with your team. A client login
holds no balance, sees no prices and spends nothing. It sees the finished
work, can download it, comment on it and decide on it when it is the named
validator. Its downloads are charged against your team balance, like any
download.

### Can I delete an approval thread?

No. In hubStudio, nobody can withdraw a request or delete a thread, a version
or a comment; the history is kept on purpose. If a request no longer
applies, say so in the thread. An admin can still decide in the validator's
place, and the validator can be changed.
