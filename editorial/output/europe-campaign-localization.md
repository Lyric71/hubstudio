# Localizing one campaign for Europe: what changes in France, Germany, Spain and Italy beyond the words

Translation is the smallest job. Take one campaign into France, Germany, Spain
and Italy and the discount, the green line, the image labels and the sale dates
all get rechecked, market by market. The formats stay exactly as they are.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What changes in campaign localization for Europe?

Campaign localization in Europe changes four things before it touches the
copy: how a discount shows its old price, whether a sustainability line
survives, which retouching or AI labels the images carry, and when a sale may
run. The formats do not change. hubStudio describes production practice here,
not legal advice.

Take one campaign with four parts: a hero image with a model, a 9:16 video, a
"30 percent off" promotion and a line that says "eco-friendly packaging." Here
is what each market asks of it.

| Asset | France | Germany | Spain | Italy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Headline and copy | French required in all advertising; any translation no more legible than the French | Copy in the language the ad set targets | Copy in the language the ad set targets; pack labels at least in Castilian | Consumer information at least in Italian, no less visible than another language |
| "30 percent off" | Old price is the lowest of the last 30 days; "soldes" only inside the two legal periods | Old price is the lowest of the last 30 days; unit price beside any price by weight or volume | Old price is the lowest of the last 30 days | Old price is the lowest of the last 30 days |
| "Eco-friendly packaging" | Cut it: banned by the EU directive from Sept. 27, 2026; French law still in parliament | Cut it: banned in German law since Sept. 27, 2026 | Cut it anyway: Spanish law not passed yet, and one master serves all four | Cut it: banned in Italian law since Sept. 27, 2026 |
| Hero image with a model | "Photographie retouchée" if the silhouette was reshaped | Casting is the brand's call | Casting is the brand's call | Casting is the brand's call |
| 9:16 video | Same file, French supers and voice | Same file, German supers | Same file, Spanish supers | Same file, Italian supers |
| Sale date | Winter sales from Jan. 6, 2027 (derived from the rule) | No legal sale periods since 2004 | Each retailer picks | Each region sets them; Jan. 5, 2027, on the common rule |

"Brand's call" means no statute read for this page adds a label to that image;
casting and setting there are production decisions, covered further down.
Every cell was checked against the published texts on Oct. 10, 2026.

## Which rules apply in all four markets?

Two EU directives sit under every version of the campaign: one for the price,
one for the green line.

The price rule is the older one. It has applied since May 2022.

> Any announcement of a price reduction must show the prior price, the lowest
> price the trader charged in a period of at least 30 days before the
> reduction.
> Source: Directive (EU) 2019/2161, Article 6a of Directive 98/6/EC, applied
> from May 28, 2022. Instrument text, read October 10, 2026.

All four markets wrote the 30 days into their own law: France in Article
L112-1-1 of its Consumer Code, Germany in section 11 of its Price Indication
Ordinance, Spain in Article 20 of its Retail Trade Act and Italy in Article
17-bis of its Consumer Code. For production, that turns the "was" price into a
data question. The artwork can be finished early. The number in it waits for
whoever owns the price history, in each market separately.

The green rule is newer, and it bites harder.

> From Sept. 27, 2026, a generic environmental claim the trader cannot back
> with recognized excellent environmental performance is banned outright, along
> with sustainability labels that rest on no certification scheme or public
> authority and climate-neutral claims based on offsetting.
> Source: Directive (EU) 2024/825, Article 4 and the points it adds to Annex I
> of Directive 2005/29/EC, February 28, 2024. Instrument text, read October
> 10, 2026.

The directive spells out what "generic" means.

> The directive's own examples of generic claims include "eco-friendly,"
> "green," "nature's friend," "climate friendly" and "biodegradable." A claim
> stops being generic when its specification sits, clear and prominent, on the
> same medium, such as the same ad spot.
> Source: Directive (EU) 2024/825, recital 9, February 28, 2024. Instrument
> text, read October 10, 2026.

So "eco-friendly packaging" goes. A line that says something true and
provable, on the same frame, can stay in. The recital gives its own pair:
"climate-friendly packaging" is generic, while a statement that all the energy
used to make the packaging came from renewable sources is specific.

A directive binds governments, not shops. The four markets are not at the same
point.

| Market | National law carrying the 2024 green-claim rules | Status on Oct. 10, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | Third Act amending the Unfair Competition Act, Feb. 12, 2026 | In force since Sept. 27, 2026 |
| Italy | Legislative Decree 30 of Feb. 20, 2026 | Applies since Sept. 27, 2026 |
| France | The DDADUE bill adapting French law to EU law, articles 20 and 21 | Passed by the Senate Feb. 18, 2026; before the National Assembly |
| Spain | Draft Sustainable Consumption Act | Approved by the cabinet at first reading July 1, 2025; not in the Official State Gazette |

One master serves all four, so build it to the strictest of them, which today
means the German and Italian texts.

Don't confuse this text with the Green Claims Directive. That is a separate
proposal from 2023, and the European Parliament's legislative tracker listed it
as blocked in September 2026.

<!-- SECTION: France -->

## What does France add?

Three rules, each touching something the viewer sees.

**Language.** The Toubon law of 1994 makes French obligatory in the offer and
description of goods and services. Its Article 2 adds: "Les mêmes dispositions
s'appliquent à toute publicité écrite, parlée ou audiovisuelle." English can
run, but a French version has to sit beside it, and Article 4 requires the
French to be as legible, audible or intelligible as the foreign text. Putting
an English slogan into a registered trademark doesn't get around it. The same
Article 2 says so.

**Retouched photos.** A commercial photo of a model whose body was reshaped
with software carries a label.

> "Photographie retouchée" must accompany a commercial photo of a model whose
> silhouette was slimmed or thickened with image software, on posters, online,
> in the press and in printed advertising. The fine is 37,500 euros, or up to
> 30 percent of the advertising spend.
> Source: French Public Health Code, Article L2133-2, and Decree 2017-738, in
> force since October 1, 2017. Statute text on Legifrance, read October 10,
> 2026.

The decree puts the check on the advertiser, which must find out whether the
photos it buys were altered. If the master was retouched for shape, the French
file gets the label, legible and set apart from the message.

**Influencer posts.** Content that paid influencers publish has its own
labels, and they are plural.

> Paid influencers must label images with a reshaped silhouette or face
> "Images retouchées" and images made by AI to depict a face or a silhouette
> "Images virtuelles." A breach carries up to a year in prison and a 4,500
> euro fine.
> Source: French Law 2023-451 of June 9, 2023, Article 5, as rewritten by
> Ordinance 2024-978 of November 6, 2024. Statute text on Legifrance, read
> October 10, 2026.

That reaches the creator cut of the campaign, not just the brand's own ads. A
generated face in a sponsored post is "Images virtuelles" in France, whatever
the rest of Europe asks.

<!-- SECTION: Germany -->

## What does Germany ask of the price card?

Mostly, how the price is shown.

> Every announced price reduction in Germany must state the lowest total price
> charged to consumers in the 30 days before it, and any price per weight,
> volume, length or area needs the unit price beside the total.
> Source: German Price Indication Ordinance (PAngV) of November 12, 2021,
> sections 4, 5 and 11, as amended May 12, 2026. Statute text, read October
> 10, 2026.

The unit is one kilogram, one liter, one meter, one square meter or one cubic
meter. A price card built for another market, showing a 400 ml bottle at its
total price alone, fails here.

The Unfair Competition Act adds a duty not to leave things out. Section 5a
treats withholding essential information as misleading, and section 5b lists
what counts in an offer: the main characteristics of the product, the trader's
identity and address, and the total price. A short cut that drops the price
line to save a second is an easy way to break it.

Germany wrote the green rules into national law by an act of February 2026.
It has no legal sale season at all. The 2004 rewrite of that act abolished the
old end-of-season sales rules, calendar and product range alike.

<!-- SECTION: Spain and Italy -->

## What do Spain and Italy add?

Spain's General Advertising Act of 1988 makes misleading, unfair and aggressive
advertising unlawful and treats it as unfair competition. Its consumer law
wants mandatory label information at least in Castilian. That is a labeling
rule, not an advertising one, but it shapes the shot: if a Spanish-market pack
exists, it is the pack to show. Sales are the trader's call.

> Spanish retailers choose their own sale seasons and decide how long each
> runs.
> Source: Spain's Retail Trade Act, Ley 7/1996, Article 25, as amended in
> 2012. Consolidated text on the Official State Gazette, read October 10, 2026.

Italy's Consumer Code carries its own language rule. Article 9 requires all
consumer information at least in Italian, and where other languages appear,
the Italian can't be less visible or legible. Foreign words
in common use are allowed. Article 21 now names environmental characteristics
among the things an ad can mislead about.

Italian sale dates are set region by region, on a shared rule.

> Italy's regions start winter sales on the first working day before Epiphany
> and summer sales on the first Saturday of July, under a common rule agreed
> in 2011.
> Source: Tuscany regional resolution 607 of May 18, 2026, and Lombardy's sales
> page, updated May 27, 2026, both read October 10, 2026. Regional authorities
> restating the rule.

## When can the sale run in each market?

| Market | Who sets the dates | Winter 2027 | Summer 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| France | The economy minister's order, two periods of four weeks | Wed. Jan. 6 to Tue. Feb. 2, derived from the rule; four eastern departments start on the first working day of January | Wed. June 23, derived from the rule |
| Germany | Nobody: legal sale periods abolished in 2004 | Retailer's choice | Retailer's choice |
| Spain | Each retailer, dates and length | Retailer's choice | Retailer's choice |
| Italy | Each region, on the 2011 common rule | Tue. Jan. 5 on the rule; Sicily has published Jan. 5 to March 15 | Sat. July 3 on the rule; Sicily has published July 3 to Sept. 15 |

> French winter sales start the second Wednesday of January at 8 a.m., moved
> to the first Wednesday when the second falls after the 12th, and run four
> weeks.
> Source: French ministerial order of May 27, 2019, under Commercial Code
> Article L310-3. Statute text on Legifrance, read October 10, 2026.

France had not published its official 2027 notice on Oct. 10, 2026, so the
French dates above are the rule applied to the calendar. Two more French
details shape the brief. Goods sold as "soldes" must have been on sale and
paid for at least a month before the period opens. And the 30-day rule still
applies: for a Jan. 6 sale, the reference price is the lowest one charged
since early December, holiday promotions included.

Some Italian areas run their own dates. The Bolzano chamber of commerce listed
Jan. 8 for most of Alto Adige in 2026, and March 7 in its tourist towns.

## What gets reshot, what gets regenerated and what stays?

The law decides some of it. Copy, price blocks, the green line and the labels
change because a text says so. Casting, setting and props are left to the
brand. They are production decisions.

Split the master into layers before anyone shoots or generates. The product,
the light and the framing are shared: they are the hardest part to get right,
and nothing in the four markets' rules asks for them to change. The people,
the setting, the props and anything with words on it are candidates for a
market version.

Then decide layer by layer. Would a buyer in that market recognize the street
or the kitchen in the frame? Is there a legal reason to change it, a pack or
a label? Is it cheaper to regenerate a background than to reshoot a scene? A
generated setting behind the real product is often the cheapest local version
there is.

A generated person is not a free swap. In France it can trigger "Images
virtuelles" in a creator's post, and in all four markets a realistic synthetic
person can be a deep fake.

The campaign adaptation cost per market piece prices these levels of change.

## Do formats change from one country to another?

No. On the same network, a French, German, Spanish and Italian ad use the same
shapes.

> Meta publishes one set of ad specs with no country selector: a feed image at
> 4:5 and 1440 x 1800 pixels, and a Reels video at 9:16 and 1440 x 2560, with
> 14 percent of the top, 35 percent of the bottom and 6 percent of each side
> kept free of text and logos.
> Source: Meta Ads Guide, Facebook Feed image and Instagram Reels video pages,
> read October 10, 2026. Platform specification pages.

TikTok's only regional split in its in-feed specs is a separate safe-zone file
for right-to-left scripts. LinkedIn's single-image sizes carry no country
variant either. The master stays one set of shapes, and nobody should be
rebuilding formats per market.

What does change on the platform is language matching. LinkedIn's help center
says "Ad creative and landing page language should match the audience language
selected in ad set audience settings," and the language can't be changed once
the ad set is live. TikTok's review checklist wants captions, spoken words and
subtitles to match a language accepted in every country an ad group targets.
In practice: one ad set or ad group per market language.

<!-- SECTION: AI -->

## Do AI-generated visuals need a label in these markets?

Since Aug. 2, 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act asks the brand or agency that
deploys a deep fake to disclose it. The Commission's guidelines count a
realistic synthetic person in an ad as a deep fake, and an AI image that
misleads about how a product looks or works. A real product against a generated background is not
one, as long as the ad doesn't mislead. The EU AI Act labeling piece covers who
applies the label and how the platform labels line up with it. For creator
content, France adds its own label on top.

## How do you keep one master and four versions in order?

Keep every version of the launch in one place. Send each market's cut to its
own reviewer.

In the hubStudio app, Campaigns keep one launch's files under one name with a
brief: the renders, the edited videos, the posts and the documents. A campaign
points at files in the Assets Library and copies nothing, so the French cut and
the master stay side by side, and a new version stays in. Validation then sends
each market's version to its reviewer, a teammate or a client, who approves it
or sends it back with a comment. Versions stay on one thread. A reviewer in
Paris can run the app in French.

**A change list for the brief.** It can be pasted as it stands.

- Copy: transcreate per market; in France, French beside any English, at the
  same legibility.
- Price: the lowest price of the last 30 days per market, from the price owner,
  not from design; in Germany, the unit price beside any price by quantity.
- Green line: cut every generic claim; keep only a specific, provable claim
  stated on the same frame.
- Images: log which files were reshaped; "Photographie retouchée" on French
  ads; "Images retouchées" or "Images virtuelles" on French creator posts.
- People and settings: no rule decides these, so the brief has to.
- Formats stay as they are. One set of shapes covers all four markets.
- Dates: French soldes from Jan. 6, 2027; Italy by region; Germany and Spain
  at the retailer's call.

The transcreation as a production line piece covers the copy side of this
list, and the ad creative design service builds the per-market batch. The Meta
platform page covers the network itself.

## Questions buyers ask about localizing a campaign for Europe

### What changes when you localize an ad campaign for Europe?

More than the words. In France, Germany, Spain and Italy a discount must show
the lowest price of the last 30 days, generic green claims are banned under an
EU directive applied from Sept. 27, 2026, France adds language and retouching
rules, and sale dates follow national or regional rules. Formats stay the same
on each network.

### Is it legal to run an English-only ad in France?

Not under the Toubon law. Its Article 2 makes French obligatory in all written,
spoken and audiovisual advertising for goods and services. English can appear
alongside, but the French must be as legible, audible or intelligible.
Products with a foreign name widely known to the public are exempt, and a
registered trademark does not exempt a slogan.

### What is the 30-day prior price rule for discounts in the EU?

When a shop announces a price reduction, it must show its prior price, defined
as the lowest price it charged in at least the 30 days before the reduction.
The rule comes from a 2019 directive applied since May 28, 2022. France,
Germany, Spain and Italy all use the 30-day period in their own laws.

### Can I still say a product is eco-friendly in EU ads in 2026?

Not as a bare claim. From Sept. 27, 2026, "eco-friendly," "green" and similar
generic claims are banned unless the trader can show recognized excellent
environmental performance. A specific claim stated clearly on the same medium
can run. Germany and Italy apply the rule in national law; France and Spain had
not passed theirs on Oct. 10, 2026.

### Do I need a retouched photo label in France?

Yes, if a model's silhouette in a commercial photo was slimmed or thickened
with software. The label reads "Photographie retouchée" and applies to posters,
online ads, the press and printed advertising. Paid influencers have a separate
duty: "Images retouchées" for a reshaped body or face, and "Images virtuelles"
for an AI-generated face or silhouette.

### When are the official sales periods in France, Spain and Italy?

France fixes two four-week periods by ministerial order; the rule puts winter
2027 at Jan. 6 to Feb. 2, pending the official notice. Spain lets each retailer
choose its seasons and their length. Italy's regions set the dates on a common
rule, which gives Jan. 5, 2027, for winter and July 3 for summer. Germany has
no legal sale periods.

### Do social media formats change from one European country to another?

No. Meta, TikTok and LinkedIn publish one set of ad specs for every market,
with no country variant, so a French and a German ad use the same shapes. What
changes is language: LinkedIn and TikTok want an ad's language to match the
audience language of the ad set, so plan one ad set per market language.
