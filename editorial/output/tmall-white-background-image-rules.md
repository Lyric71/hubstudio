# The white-background image: Tmall's separate rule set, counted

Most English guidance treats the fifth Tmall image as a product shot on white.
The Chinese sources treat it as something else: a deliverable with its own
checks, briefed and retouched on its own terms. No English page explains that it
exists.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What is a white-background image on Tmall?

It is a main image with a rule set of its own, most often published as the fifth
image in a general-category listing. Fourteen of fifteen sources that address it
treat it as a separate deliverable rather than as a styled main image dropped
onto white.

These specs are not read from a seller backend. Both platform rule centers
gate their rule text behind a seller login, so no open source reaches the
official value.

across 15 independent sources in Chinese and English on 10 September 2026, with
the number of sources that agreed shown per row. Rows marked consensus had broad

## Does the white background have to be pure white?

In words, every source that describes it says yes. As a number, the record is
younger and thinner than it looks.

> The most commonly published background for the white-background image is pure
> white. Eight of fifteen sources call for pure white, and every source that
> prints a number prints RGB 255 255 255. The number is recent: every source
> dated 2017 to 2024 describes pure white in words and prints no value, and all
> three that print the triplet are dated 2026.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language first,
> collected 10 September 2026, counted by domain and grouped by publication
> date. Modal value with its agreement count, not a reading from the platform.

The harder question for a retoucher is how close to white is close enough, and
there the sources split.

> Thirteen of fifteen sources state no tolerance at all. One publishes a
> tolerance of plus or minus 3, counting 252 to 255 on each channel as passing,
> on a page whose own publisher gives 255 flat on its specification page. One
> says any near-white fails and describes a background at 254 254 255 as
> detectable. No source describes any value below 252 as acceptable.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, each published tolerance position counted separately. Contested
> modal position, reported as the spread rather than a single value, not a
> reading from the platform.

Put those two positions together and one choice survives both.

> RGB 255 255 255 is the only background value that no published reading
> rejects. A background at 252 passes the one published tolerance and fails the
> one published zero-tolerance reading. Clipping the background to pure 255 is
> therefore the one choice the published record does not contradict. That is a
> conclusion drawn from the spread, not a platform rule.
> Source: derived from the published tolerance positions above, fifteen
> independent sources collected 10 September 2026, reasoning stated openly and
> resting on modal values rather than primary readings.

Clip to 255. It costs a retoucher nothing, and it is the only value on the
published record that nobody says fails.

## Can a model appear in a white-background image?

No, on every source that addresses it, and the prohibitions are the most
consistent part of the whole record.

> The prohibitions are the most consistent part of the published record. Nine
> of fifteen sources rule out text, watermarks, logos and promotional overlay.
> Seven rule out a model, six rule out shadows, six rule out spliced composites,
> and five rule out hangers, mannequins and tags. One extends the model ban to
> any body part at all, hands, feet, legs or a head.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language first,
> collected 10 September 2026, counted by domain for each prohibited element.
> Modal positions with their agreement counts, not a reading from the platform.

| Prohibited element | Sources agreeing | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Text, watermarks, logos and promotional overlay | 9 of 15 | consensus |
| A model | 7 of 15 | consensus |
| Shadows | 6 of 15 | consensus, with the contact shadow contested |
| Spliced composites | 6 of 15 | consensus |
| Hangers, mannequins and tags | 5 of 15 | consensus |
| Any body part, including hands | 1 of 15 | contested |
| Reflections and glare | 1 of 15 | contested |

The shadow row carries a caveat worth reading before a retoucher removes one.

> Whether a faint shadow directly under the product survives is not settled.
> Six of fifteen sources prohibit shadows with no exception. Two, both published
> in 2026, describe a light contact shadow as tolerated, and one of those two
> says the opposite on another of its own pages. Reflections and glare are
> named as prohibited on one source only.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, counted by domain with self-contradicting publishers noted.
> Contested modal position, not a reading from the platform.

Six sources against two, with one of the two contradicting itself. Remove the
shadow.

## How much of the frame should the product fill?

Here the published guidance has changed shape over time, and the two shapes
point in different directions.

> Published fill guidance for the white-background image comes in two
> generations. Five pages dated 2017 to 2024 say the product should fill the
> frame and leave no white margin. Three pages dated 2026 give a number instead,
> 70 to 80 percent of the frame, which leaves 20 to 30 percent white. The full
> numeric spread runs from 60 to 80 percent, and no source states where either
> version came from.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, grouped by publication date and reported as the spread rather
> than averaged. Contested for the figure, not a reading from the platform.

No margin against a fifth of the frame left white is not a small disagreement.
The newer numeric guidance is the more cautious working choice, but neither
version has a stated origin, so this page publishes the band and not a cap.

<!-- SECTION: file weight -->

## A file weight that looks confirmed and is not

> The 38KB to 300KB file weight that circulates for the white-background image
> traces to a single origin. It appears on three of fifteen sources. The oldest
> is dated September 2017, and the two later pages repeat that page's size,
> format and prohibition list almost line for line. Other sources give a 3MB
> ceiling instead. Three pages restating one page are one data point, not three.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, with each figure traced to its earliest dated appearance.
> Contested, originating once, not a reading from the platform.

A figure copied twice from a 2017 page looks like agreement in a count and is
really one voice. Staying under 300KB satisfies both readings anyway.

## Where does Tmall use the white-background image?

In recommendation, according to the sources. Not in search, according to none of
them.

> The white-background image is described as feeding recommendation and content
> channels on the Taobao app homepage. Eight of fifteen sources say so, with the
> channel names changing as the pages get newer. Six of fifteen say a listing
> without a compliant white image loses eligibility for those channels. No
> source in the collection says the image is used in search results, and none
> puts a number on the exposure lost.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese and English, collected
> 10 September 2026, counted by domain. Modal position with its agreement count,
> the absence stated with its count, not a reading from the platform.

The slot moves by category, and on JD it moves to the front.

> Published category standards move the white-background image. A reproduction
> of the food standard puts it second. A 2017 tutorial puts it second for
> luggage, and apparel is published both as the fifth image (2017) and as the
> first (2026). On JD, every page naming a slot puts the white image first,
> against Tmall's fifth, five of five pages per platform with none
> contradicting.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources collected 10 September 2026,
> one source per category map, and a separate twenty-four-page JD and Tmall
> collection from the same date. Contested for each category map, consensus for
> the JD against Tmall slot, not a reading from either platform.

## Why white-background images fail

No rejection notice was obtained for this page, and none is reconstructed. No
source ranks the causes either, so what follows is an unranked list.

> No published source ranks why white-background images fail. Three of fifteen
> describe causes, as an unranked list: a background that is not pure white, a
> shadow, a model, text or a logo, a product too small in the frame, a
> duplicated product, and rough cutout edges. None says how often any of them
> occurs.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026. Stated as an unranked list with its count, not as a platform
> rejection taxonomy.

Each listed cause has a corresponding fix. The table below is an illustration of
that checklist, built from the causes above, and not a record of any rejected
listing.

| Near miss | Compliant version |
|---|---|
| Background at 250 or 252 on a channel | Background clipped to RGB 255 255 255 |
| Faint contact shadow under the product | Shadow removed entirely |
| Product small in a sea of white | Product scaled into the 60 to 80 percent band |
| Brand logo or promotional text on the image | Image carries the product only |
| Soft or jagged cutout edge | Clean edge at full resolution |
| Product shown twice in one frame | One product, one view |

The Tmall product image requirements article covers the other four main images
and the detail page. The article comparing JD image requirements with Tmall
covers the slot difference between the two platforms. The AI image production

## Questions sellers ask about the white-background image

**What is a white-background image on Tmall?**

A main image with its own rule set, most often published as the fifth image in a
general-category listing. Fourteen of fifteen sources collected on 10 September
2026 treat it as a separate deliverable. Category standards move it, with food
published at the second slot, and on JD every source naming a slot puts it first.

**Does the white background have to be pure white?**

Every source describing it says pure white, and every source printing a number
gives RGB 255 255 255. Tolerance is contested: one source allows 252 to 255,
another says any near-white fails. Clipping to 255 is the only value no
published reading rejects, which is a conclusion from the sources, not a rule.

**Can a model appear in a white-background image?**

No. Seven of fifteen sources rule out a model, and one extends the ban to any
body part, including hands. The same record rules out text, watermarks, logos,
spliced composites, hangers, mannequins and tags, and six sources prohibit
shadows, with a faint contact shadow described as tolerated on only two.

**Where does Tmall use the white-background image?**

Eight of fifteen sources describe it feeding recommendation and content
channels on the Taobao app homepage, and six say a listing without a compliant
one loses eligibility for them. No source says it is used in search results, and
none puts a number on the exposure lost.
