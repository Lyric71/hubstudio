# What it costs to localize a global campaign for China

Search this and you get translation platforms quoting cents per word. That
answer is not wrong so much as aimed at a different problem. The expensive part
of a China adaptation is not the words. It is that the picture was composed for
a frame China does not use, carrying text that is a legal object here, featuring
a person whose release does not cover this territory.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## How much does it cost to adapt a campaign for China?

There is no single number, and anyone printing one is guessing. What can be
done honestly is to itemize the stack, put a sourced band on the lines that
have one, and mark the lines that have no published benchmark at all as exactly
that.

Seven lines carry the cost: layout adaptation, re-shoot or recasting, platform
variants, copy transcreation, compliance review, AI labeling, and upload QA. A
translation quote covers part of one of them.

One framing point before the detail. This work comes out of the production
budget, which is the line that has been shrinking.

> Production averaged 19 percent of total marketing budgets, against 24
> percent before the pandemic.
> Source: global advertiser trade body content production research, March
> 2025. Survey of senior marketing procurement respondents at 50 global brands
> across more than ten sectors.

## Is China localization just translation?

No, and the reason is structural rather than cultural. Three things about a
Western master stop being true the moment it enters a Chinese surface, and none
of them is fixed by changing the language.

The frame is different. The on-image text becomes a regulated object. And the
person in the picture is licensed for somewhere else.

Translation vendors are not hiding this. They are answering the question they
were asked, and they price it clearly.

> Published rate cards in the translation category put professional English to
> Simplified Chinese at roughly 0.08 to 0.18 USD per source word, and price
> transcreation per hour or per project instead, because source word count
> does not track the creative work.
> Source: published pricing pages collected across the category, September
> 2026, no company named. Rate cards only, no survey and no method published
> behind the band.

Read the second half of that. The category itself stops pricing per word as
soon as the work becomes creative, which is the category conceding the point
this page is making.

## Why can a Western master not be re-cropped for China?

Four reasons, strongest first. The first is arithmetic you can check yourself.

**The typography has nowhere to live.** Take a 16:9 master and cut it to a tall
frame at full height. A 3:4 frame keeps about 42 percent of the original width.
A 4:5 frame keeps about 45 percent. A 9:16 frame keeps about 32 percent. A
2.39:1 cinema master cut to 9:16 keeps about 24 percent.

Those are ratios divided by ratios, not measurements: 0.75, 0.8 and 0.5625
divided by 1.778, and 0.5625 divided by 2.39. Whatever the exact frame a given
surface wants, a wide master surrenders half to three quarters of its width to
reach a tall one. The subject sat on a third that no longer exists, the negative
space that carried the headline is gone, and the product has moved relative to
the edge. That is a rebuild, not a crop.

**The on-image text is a legal object here.**

> China's Advertising Law bans "national level," "highest grade," "best" and
> any term of the same or similar meaning in advertising, and sets a fine of
> 200,000 to 1,000,000 RMB on the advertiser for a breach, with the same band
> on the agency and the publisher.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China, Articles 9(3) and
> 57, 2021 amendment. Statutory text, no sample.

Superlatives are the ordinary grammar of Western advertising headlines, and a
good share of them are baked into artwork rather than sitting in a caption
field. Taking them off means re-typesetting and re-exporting every size, which
is a production job, not a copy edit. Note also that the penalty band lands on
the agency and the publisher too, not only the advertiser.

The rule is narrower than its reputation, and it is worth knowing where the
edges are.

> China's market regulator published Enforcement Guidelines on Absolute Terms
> in Advertising as Announcement 2023 No. 6, adopted 24 February 2023 and
> published 20 March 2023, setting out where absolute wording is not caught
> and where a first, promptly corrected breach draws no penalty.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation announcement, 20 March
> 2023. The regulator's own published guideline, twelve articles.

> Beijing's market regulator issued an implementing work guideline on 20 March
> 2024 stating that absolute wording is caught only where it points at the
> goods being sold and has the effect of misleading consumers or disparaging
> other traders.
> Source: Beijing Municipal Market Supervision Administration work guideline
> and official Q&A, 20 March 2024. The regulator's own text.

So a superlative about the brand's ambition reads differently from a
superlative about the product's rank. That distinction is a review decision on
every line of on-image copy, and it is work whether the answer is yes or no.

**The ad has to declare itself.**

> Under China's Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising, in
> force 1 May 2023, an advertisement must be identifiable as one, and any post
> that promotes goods through knowledge sharing, experience sharing or
> consumer review formats while carrying a purchase link must be conspicuously
> marked "advertisement."
> Source: Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising, Article 9,
> State Administration for Market Regulation Order No. 72, issued 25 February
> 2023. Statutory text.

That mark is a layout element the master never had. It changes the safe area on
every frame it appears in, which means it has to be designed rather than
stamped on at upload.

**The likeness rights do not travel.**

> Under China's Civil Code, a likeness may not be made, used or published
> without the portrayed person's consent; where a licence clause is disputed
> it is construed in favor of the person portrayed; and where the licence term
> is unagreed or unclear either party may end it on reasonable notice.
> Source: Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, Articles 1019, 1021
> and 1022, in force 1 January 2021. Statutory text.

Read the middle clause. Ambiguity is resolved against the party using the
image. A release written for a European campaign that does not name Chinese
platforms, Chinese territory or a defined term is not a document you want to
rely on, and the third clause means an unclear term can be ended on notice
after the campaign is live.

The choice is re-clearance or recasting, and re-clearance is often the slower
of the two. Worth saying plainly: this is a rights and logistics argument, not
a claim about what audiences prefer. No source supports the idea that locally
cast talent performs better, so this page does not make it.

## How many assets does a China adaptation produce?

More than the master count, because each surface wants a structurally different
object rather than a resized one.

| Surface | What it structurally demands | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace product pages | A square catalog frame plus a long scrolling detail page, authored separately | The detail page is a document, not a resized ad |
| Short video | A tall frame with a moving safe area, plus an ad mark where a purchase path exists | Frame arithmetic and the Internet Advertising Measures |
| Social discovery | A cover plus a card sequence, so one hero does not fill the unit | Format-inherent |
| Messaging and owned | An article layout with its own cover crop, distinct from every ad frame | Format-inherent |
| Broadcast social | A feed card crop, plus the ad mark where a purchase path is attached | Internet Advertising Measures |
| Livestream | Scripted claims and on-screen supers become compliance objects | Advertiser and endorser duties attach by role |

The livestream row is the one that surprises budget-holders.

> The same Measures put advertiser duties on a seller who promotes by
> livestream, and agency, publisher or endorser duties on the room operator
> and the on-camera talent depending on their role.
> Source: Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising, Article 19,
> in force 1 May 2023. Statutory text.

Duties attach by what someone actually does in the room. So the script, the
supers and the product claims need the same review the artwork got, and that
review is a line item.

## The labeling line nobody budgeted for

If any part of the adaptation is generated, a second regime applies, and it
lands on the file itself.

> China's Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content, issued 14
> March 2025 by four central bodies and effective 1 September 2025, require a
> user-perceivable label on generated images and video and a second, machine
> label written into the file metadata carrying the synthetic attribute, the
> provider identifier and a content number, and require that exported and
> downloaded copies still carry the visible label.
> Source: Cyberspace Administration of China announcement, 14 March 2025. The
> regulator's own published instrument, effective 1 September 2025.

> A mandatory national standard on labeling methods for AI-generated synthetic
> content took effect alongside those Measures on 1 September 2025.
> Source: national mandatory standard GB 45438-2025, in force 1 September
> 2025. Standards body approval, confirmed in state wire coverage the same
> day.

Two labels, one visible and one in the metadata, and the visible one has to
survive export and download. Most adaptation pipelines flatten and re-export
files repeatedly, which is precisely where a visible label gets lost and a
metadata label gets stripped. Budget the QA pass, because the requirement is
about what arrives at the other end.

None of this is theoretical enforcement.

> China's market regulator handled 46,900 illegal advertising cases in 2024
> with 349 million RMB in fines and forfeitures, of which more than 30,000
> cases and 187 million RMB were internet advertising.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation full-year enforcement
> totals, released 14 March 2025. National case counts published by the
> regulator at a press briefing.

More than 30,000 internet advertising cases in one year. Whatever the odds on
any single asset, this is an actively policed area rather than a dormant one.

## What does a translation quote leave out?

Itemized, with the honest state of the evidence on each line.

| Line | In a translation quote | Published benchmark available |
|---|---|---|
| Copy rendered into Chinese | Yes | Yes, per source word |
| Transcreation of headlines | Sometimes, priced separately | Priced per hour or project, no per-unit band |
| Re-typesetting every size | No | No published benchmark |
| Layout rebuild for tall frames | No | Conflicting published cards, see below |
| Re-shoot or recasting | No | One published regional rate card |
| Talent re-clearance | No | No published benchmark |
| Compliance review of on-image claims | No | No published benchmark |
| AI label application and survival QA | No | No published benchmark |
| Upload QA per surface | No | No published benchmark |

Six of those nine lines have no published benchmark anywhere. That is not this
page being coy. It is the actual state of the category, and it is why a
translation quote is the only number most buyers ever see: it is the only line
with a public price.

## Two worked stacks

Built from published cards only, each line carrying its own attribution. They
deliberately do not total, because the lines come from different sources with
different units and a headline sum would imply a precision none of them has.

**A catalog-led adaptation.** A brand bringing an existing product range onto
Chinese marketplace pages, no film, no talent.

> A Chinese commercial photography studio's published 2026 rate card lists
> flat-lay shots at 80 to 150 RMB an image, in-studio model shots at 200 to
> 300 RMB, retouching at 50 to 200 RMB an image and short video at 3,000 to
> 15,000 RMB a piece, on a five-style minimum.
> Source: a published Chinese ecommerce photography rate card, dated 20 July
> 2026, described by its publisher as its own executing rates for the
> Guangzhou and Pearl River Delta region. One studio's book, no survey behind
> it.

That is one studio's book in one region, published on one date, and it is
quoted here as exactly that rather than as a market average. Against it sits
the layout line, which is where the published evidence falls apart.

> Published Chinese rate cards for ecommerce detail-page design disagree by
> roughly an order of magnitude, from 200 to 300 RMB at the low end of a 2023
> page to 1,500 to 5,000 RMB a set on a 2026 page. None of them states what a
> set contains, and none states a sample or a method.
> Source: published Chinese design rate cards collected September 2026, dated
> between June 2023 and April 2026, no company named. Published prices only,
> conflict stated rather than averaged.

An order of magnitude, and nobody defines the unit. That disagreement is not a
research failure to apologize for. It is precisely what a buyer walks into when
they ask three suppliers for a detail-page quote and get three numbers that
cannot be compared, because none of them says what a set contains.

So the catalog stack reads: capture per image against a dated regional card,
retouch per image against the same card, layout against a range that must be
pinned to a defined set before it means anything, plus compliance review and
upload QA with no benchmark at all.

**A film-led adaptation.** A brand with an existing hero film and a Western
cast.

Here the first question is not a price, it is the release. If the talent
agreement does not name Chinese territory, Chinese platforms and a defined
term, the Civil Code position above makes reliance on it a poor bet, and the
stack forks: re-clearance, which is a legal cost and a calendar risk, or a
recast and re-shoot, which is a production cost with a known shape. The film
also needs the tall-frame rebuild described earlier, and short video sits at
3,000 to 15,000 RMB a piece on that same published card.

Both stacks then carry the labeling line if anything in them is generated, and
both carry a QA pass that verifies the visible label survived export.

## How to scope this before anyone quotes

Four things to settle, because they are what makes the quotes comparable.

**Define the set.** Decide what one delivered unit is, per surface, before you
ask for a price. The order-of-magnitude spread above exists mostly because
nobody defines it.

**Audit the releases first.** Not after the creative is chosen. Whether the
talent travels decides whether this is an adaptation or a re-shoot, and that is
the largest fork in the whole stack.

**Read your own headlines against Article 9(3).** Mark every superlative and
decide, line by line, whether it points at the goods. Do it before layout,
because the answer changes the artwork.

**Count the surfaces, not the assets.** Each surface wants a structurally
different object. Counting assets from the master will undercount, every time.

The brands solutions page sets out how this work is scoped, the Tmall platform
page covers the marketplace half of the surface list, and the premium SUV case
study shows a campaign adaptation running end to end.

## Questions buyers ask about China localization

**How much does it cost to adapt a campaign for China?**

There is no single published figure, and six of the nine cost lines have no
public benchmark at all. What is published: translation at roughly 0.08 to 0.18
USD per source word, capture from 80 to 300 RMB an image on one dated regional
card, and detail-page layout spanning 200 to 5,000 RMB depending on which card
you read.

**Is China localization just translation?**

No. Translation covers the words, which is roughly one line of a seven-line
stack. The frame changes, the on-image text becomes a regulated object under
the Advertising Law, an ad mark has to be designed into the layout, and Western
talent releases rarely cover Chinese territory or platforms.

**Why can a Western master not be re-cropped for China?**

Cutting a 16:9 master to a tall frame keeps about 32 to 45 percent of its
width, which is ratio arithmetic rather than a measurement. The headline's
negative space and the subject's placement both go. Add re-typesetting to
remove superlatives, a required ad mark, and talent rights that do not travel.

**What does a translation quote leave out?**

Re-typesetting every size, the layout rebuild for tall frames, re-shoot or
recasting, talent re-clearance, compliance review of on-image claims, AI label
application and survival QA, and upload QA per surface. Only the words and
sometimes transcreation are inside a translation quote.

**How many assets does a China adaptation produce?**

More than the master count, because each surface demands a structurally
different object rather than a resize: a square catalog frame plus a separate
long detail page, a tall video frame with a moving safe area, a cover plus card
sequence, an article layout with its own cover crop. Count surfaces first, then
assets.
