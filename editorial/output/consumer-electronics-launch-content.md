# Consumer electronics launch content: the build, asset by asset

Consumer electronics product launch content is an asset list with an accuracy
layer on top. That layer covers screens, certification marks, claimed specs and
energy labels, and it decides which assets can be generated and which have to be
shot on the real device.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What does consumer electronics product launch content include?

A consumer electronics launch needs a hero film and its cut-downs, a marketplace
listing set, product and packaging stills, interface renders, and the
certification mark and energy label images the rules call for. Each one shows a
device making claims. hubStudio builds the list against those rules first,
because they decide what can be generated.

The pages that rank for this topic list assets and stop there.

> Across four buyer phrasings and 32 search results, one page came from a
> regulator, and it was a 2018 proposal to repeal a 1966 rule on stating TV
> screen size. None of the six results read in full addressed certification
> marks, energy labels or a ruling on product imagery.
> Source: search-results audit run 10 September 2026 across four buyer
> phrasings, publisher type recorded for every result.

Four things on a launch asset can be held to a rule. The table ties each one to
the code, ruling or instrument read for this page, market by market. It
describes production practice, not legal advice. A claim or a mark that matters

## What have regulators ruled against?

No marketplace rejection notice was available to this research. The reasons
below are regulator findings, not platform rejections. Each one is a mistake a
launch asset can repeat.

| Asset ruled on                  | What was wrong                                                                        | Ruled by and when                  |
|---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Dash cam web page               | A stock night photo with number plates added, read as footage from the cameras sold   | UK advertising regulator, 5 June 2024 |
| Portable cooler display ad      | "Cool Any Room in Seconds," with no evidence supplied                                  | UK advertising regulator, 26 November 2025 |
| Robot toy ad                    | Footage appearing to show real or AI-generated puppies implied a highly realistic toy | UK advertising regulator, 25 March 2026 |
| Televised shaving demonstration | Plexiglass in place of sandpaper, undisclosed, presented as proof viewers could see   | US Supreme Court, 5 April 1965     |

The pattern is old and it is consistent. An image offered as evidence has to be
evidence.

## Can product screens be simulated in ads?

Not in a way that passes a render off as what the device shows. The rulings read
for this page do not turn on the words "screen images simulated." They turn on a
narrower question: does the ad present the image as the device's own output?

> A UK advertising regulator upheld a complaint against a dash cam brand's web
> page after finding that a night image of readable number plates was a stock
> photo with the plates added, not footage from the cameras advertised.
> Consumers, it ruled, would read the image as a screen grab from the product,
> and it told the advertiser not to use images that exaggerate product
> performance.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 5 June 2024, an upheld investigation
> under the code's misleading advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules.

The same regulator's guidance deals with small print. It names a gameplay
disclaimer, not a screen disclaimer, so applying it to a "screen images
simulated" line is an analogy. It is a close one.

> The same regulator's guidance says images should not be altered in a way that
> exaggerates what the product can do, and that a qualification or small print
> such as "Not representative of actual gameplay" is generally unlikely to stop
> an ad from misleading.
> Source: UK advertising regulator guidance on misleading imagery, 11 September
> 2025, published advice drawing on six earlier rulings.

US law drew the same line long before.

> The US Supreme Court held that an undisclosed mock-up in a TV demonstration was
> a material deceptive practice because it told viewers they were seeing proof
> of the claim with their own eyes. Where an ad rests on the seller's word rather
> than the viewer's own perception, the Court said, an undisclosed prop is not
> prohibited.
> Source: FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive Co., 380 U.S. 374, decided 5 April 1965, the
> Court's opinion on a televised shaving demonstration using plexiglass in place
> of sandpaper.

> The US Federal Trade Commission judges an ad by its overall impression,
> including its visual imagery, and says written disclosures or fine print may
> be insufficient to correct a misleading representation.
> Source: FTC Policy Statement on Deception, 14 October 1983, the Commission's
> published enforcement policy, read in full text.

For the build, that sorts interface work into two kinds. This is production
practice, not a rule. A render shown as interface design asks the viewer to take
the seller's word. A frame shown as a screen grab, a camera sample or a live demo
asks viewers to trust their own eyes, and it has to come off the device.

## What evidence do claimed specs need?

Evidence in hand before the ad runs, in each of the three markets read.

> The UK non-broadcast code requires marketers to hold documentary evidence for
> objective claims before an ad runs, and bars ads that mislead by exaggerating
> the capability or performance of a product. The broadcast code applies the
> same two rules to TV.
> Source: UK advertising codes, non-broadcast rules 3.7 and 3.11 and broadcast
> rules 3.9 and 3.12, code text read on the regulator's site 10 September 2026.

> A UK advertising regulator upheld a complaint against a portable cooler ad
> that claimed it could "Cool Any Room in Seconds," finding the advertiser
> supplied no evidence and the claim was unsubstantiated and misleading.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 26 November 2025, an upheld
> investigation of a paid online display ad under the code's misleading
> advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules.

> The US Federal Trade Commission treats an objective claim made without a
> reasonable basis as deceptive, and where an ad says "tests prove," it expects
> the advertiser to hold at least the level of proof the ad claims.
> Source: FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation, 23 November
> 1984, the Commission's published enforcement policy.

China writes the rule into statute, and puts a price on breaking it.

> China's Advertising Law requires information about a product's performance,
> function, quality, ingredients and price to be accurate, clear and
> unambiguous, and requires cited data, statistics and survey results to be true,
> accurate and sourced. An ad counts as false where performance, function,
> specification or quality information does not match reality and materially
> affects the purchase, or where it relies on fabricated or unverifiable data.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China (Guanggao Fa), 2021
> amendment, Articles 8, 11 and 28, statute text read in Chinese on 10 September
> 2026.

> Publishing a false ad in China draws a fine of three to five times the
> advertising fee, or 200,000 to 1,000,000 yuan where the fee cannot be
> calculated or is clearly low, rising to five to ten times, or 1,000,000 to
> 2,000,000 yuan, for three or more violations within two years or serious
> circumstances.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China (Guanggao Fa), 2021
> amendment, Article 55, statute text read in Chinese on 10 September 2026.

The production practice that follows is simple to state. The test report is an
input to the storyboard, not a check at the end. Every figure on a spec callout,
a detail page or a voiceover line traces back to it, in every language version.

## How should certification marks and energy labels appear?

As the product actually holds them, and not before. China's certification rules
name advertising outright.

> Products on China's compulsory certification catalog must be certified and
> carry the CCC mark before they leave the factory, are sold, imported or used
> in business. The certification holder must use the mark correctly on the
> product, its packaging, and in advertising and product introductions, and no
> one may forge, alter, misuse, buy or transfer a certification mark.
> Source: Provisions on Compulsory Product Certification (Qiangzhixing Chanpin
> Renzheng Guanli Guiding), quality inspection authority Order 117 as amended by
> market regulator Order 61 of 29 September 2022, Articles 2, 32 and 33, read on
> a Chinese market regulator's site 10 September 2026.

The mark requirements were revised for 2024, so a mark file from an older
campaign needs checking before it is reused.

> China's certification regulator revised its compulsory certification mark
> requirements in an announcement dated 8 August 2023, in force from 1 January
> 2024.
> Source: national certification and accreditation administration, Announcement
> 2023 No. 12, announcement page read on a Chinese market regulator's site 10
> September 2026, dates only.

US rules bite earlier in the calendar, at the teaser stage.

> Under US rules, marketing a radio frequency device includes advertising it. A
> device advertised or displayed before FCC authorization must carry a statement
> that it has not been authorized and may not be offered for sale or lease, or
> sold or leased, until authorization is obtained.
> Source: 47 CFR 2.803, as amended 25 November 2025, eCFR text read on the Legal
> Information Institute mirror 10 September 2026.

> US rules let a radio device with a built-in display show its required labeling
> electronically, reachable in no more than three steps from the settings menu,
> with the FCC identifier or other information also on the packaging or a
> removable label. Devices authorized by supplier's declaration may carry the FCC
> logo, on a voluntary basis.
> Source: 47 CFR 2.935 and 2.1074(b), 82 FR 50827 and 50829, 2 November 2017,
> eCFR text read on the Legal Information Institute mirror 10 September 2026.

The EU mark carries a narrow meaning, and a caption should not stretch it.

> The CE marking is the manufacturer's declaration that a product meets EU
> requirements, affixed only after the correct conformity assessment. It does not
> mean the EU or any authority approved the product as safe.
> Source: European Commission CE marking page, dated 25 October 2021, and the EU
> business portal page last checked 17 July 2026, both read 10 September 2026.

Energy labels split by market.

> Where a product on China's energy label catalog is sold online, the energy
> label must be shown in a conspicuous position on the main product information
> page. A label used in advertising may be scaled in proportion, and no one may
> forge or misuse an energy label or use one for false publicity.
> Source: Energy Efficiency Label Measures (Nengyuan Xiaolv Biaoshi Guanli
> Banfa), Order No. 35 of the national development and quality inspection
> authorities, published 29 February 2016, in force 1 June 2016, Articles 6 and
> 17, read on China's market regulator site.

> Since 1 March 2021, TVs, monitors and signage displays sold in the EU carry an
> energy label on an A to G scale. Any visual advertisement for a specific
> display model, online included, must show its energy efficiency class and the
> range of classes on the label, and technical promotional material that
> describes the model's parameters must show its class.
> Source: European Commission product page for electronic displays, read 10
> September 2026, summarizing Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2013 under framework
> Regulation (EU) 2017/1369.

The two rules are not the same. China mandates the label on the online listing
and allows a scaled version in advertising, without requiring one in every ad.
The EU puts the class and the range into any visual ad for a specific display
model. A display launching in both markets needs its label asset built for both
uses.

Marketplaces also place these images in particular listing slots. The Tmall
product image requirements article covers the published slot maps and how far
they can be trusted.

## Which parts of a tech launch can be generated, and which must be captured?

One test sorts every asset. Is it offered as evidence of what the device is and
does, or as the world around it? The table applies that test. The split is
production practice, and the last column names the rule that decides each row.

| Asset                                        | Generate                                    | Capture                                   | The rule that decides                                   |
|----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Footage or stills offered as device output   | No                                          | Yes, from the device itself               | UK dash cam ruling, 2024; Colgate-Palmolive, 1965       |
| Performance demonstrations                   | No                                          | Yes, backed by test evidence              | UK cooler ruling, 2025; FTC substantiation statement, 1984 |
| Hero product stills, macro and packaging     | Only from a model trained on the product    | Yes, and the reference for any generation | Marketplace AI image rule, reported 2025                |
| White-background image                       | A clean cutout from a capture               | Yes                                       | Production practice                                     |
| Interface animation                          | Yes, as CGI shown as interface design       | Where the ad offers the screen as proof   | Colgate-Palmolive, 1965; FTC deception statement, 1983  |
| Lifestyle scenes and environments            | Yes                                         | Optional                                  | UK AI guidance, 2026: the advertiser stays responsible  |
| People, pets or objects reacting to the device | Context only, never the product's behavior | The product's behavior                  | UK robot toy ruling, 2026                               |
| Certification mark and energy label images   | No                                          | The issued mark or label, reproduced      | CCC provisions, Articles 32 and 33; energy label Measures, Article 17 |
| Spec callouts                                | Layout only                                 | Figures from the test report              | Non-broadcast rule 3.7; Advertising Law, Article 11     |

One delivered launch shows the split inside a single film.

> One launch film for an AI wearable became 22 cuts: a 90-second hero film, six
> use-case vignettes of 15 to 30 seconds, vertical, square and horizontal
> versions of every cut, edits for five platforms, and voiceover and on-screen
> text in three languages. Real product footage came from a two-day shoot, CGI
> carried the interface, and generated scenes carried the world around the
> device.
> Source: hubStudio's iFlytek AnyPin case study page, read 10 September 2026,
> figures as the case page publishes them, one engagement, no method statement.

The iFlytek AnyPin case study sets out the full build. Generated footage can
still overstate a product, and the tool does not take the blame for it.

> A UK advertising regulator upheld 22 complaints against an ad for a robot toy,
> finding that footage which appeared to show real or AI-generated puppies gave
> the impression the product was highly realistic, and ruled that the advertiser
> must not misleadingly represent or exaggerate the functionality and appearance
> of its products.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 25 March 2026, an upheld investigation
> under the code's misleading advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules.

> The same regulator's guidance holds that the advertising code is
> media-neutral: the rules apply regardless of how the content was created, and
> the advertiser remains responsible for any AI output used in its ads.
> Source: UK advertising regulator guidance, 11 June 2026, published guidance
> for advertisers on AI-generated content.

At least one Chinese marketplace polices the same failure in product stills.

> A Chinese marketplace became the first to publish a rule aimed specifically
> at AI fake product images, naming four failure categories: product material
> or style that does not match the real item, distorted or exaggerated product
> effects, an obvious cutout and paste look, and bodies or scenes that break
> physical laws.
> Source: Economic Information Daily (Jingji Cankao Bao), 27 March 2025,
> reporting the platform's own rule announcement and its detection-model
> enforcement.

At volume, production practice moves the product accuracy into the model, trained
on the product line itself.

> A self-serve content platform with models trained per product line went live in
> eight weeks for a consumer electronics brand's North American teams and outside
> agencies, and produced thousands of brand assets a week after launch.
> Source: hubStudio's HiSense case study page, read 10 September 2026, figures as
> the case page publishes them, one engagement, no method statement.

That eight weeks measures a platform going live, not a launch content build. The
HiSense case study covers the deployment.

The manufacturers solutions page describes launch imagery made before physical
units exist. Pair any such image with two rules from this page. An image offered
as device output has to be device output, and a radio device advertised in the
US before FCC authorization carries the not-authorized statement. The video
production service covers the capture side of the split.

## In what order does a launch content build run?

By dependency, not by calendar. The plan below is production practice, not a
benchmark. It carries no durations, because nothing read for this page measures
one.

| Step                                   | Must be true before it starts                                             | What it unlocks                                          | Rule behind the gate                                      |
|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Lock specs and claim evidence          | A test report behind every objective claim the assets will make           | Spec callouts, detail pages, voiceover lines             | Non-broadcast rule 3.7; FTC substantiation statement; Advertising Law, Article 11 |
| Confirm certification and authorization | CCC certification before the mark appears; FCC authorization, or the not-authorized statement on US ads | Mark images, packaging stills, teasers | CCC provisions, Article 2; 47 CFR 2.803 |
| Confirm the energy class               | The class on the issued label                                             | China listing page, EU display ads, technical material   | Energy label Measures, Article 6; Regulation (EU) 2019/2013 |
| Storyboard for modular cuts            | Every ratio, platform edit and language planned from the first board      | Hero film, vignettes, platform edits                     | iFlytek AnyPin case page                                  |
| Capture                                | The real device on set                                                    | Product footage, macro, packaging, in-hand, white background | UK dash cam ruling, 2024                              |
| CGI and generation                     | Captured product reference and approved interface designs                 | Interface renders, environments, lifestyle scenes        | UK robot toy ruling, 2026; marketplace AI image rule, 2025 |
| Cut-downs, ratios and localization     | Claims and on-screen text checked in each language                        | Platform edits, localized versions                       | Advertising Law, Article 8                                |
| Compliance review                      | Every asset checked against the accuracy table                            | Release                                                  | The accuracy table on this page                           |

The first three rows are gates, not tasks. Capture, generation and cut-downs
start once they clear. A claim that fails review sends the build back to the
first row, not the last.

## Questions about consumer electronics launch content

**What content does a consumer electronics launch need?**

A hero film with use-case cut-downs in every ratio and language, a marketplace
listing set with main images, a white-background image, detail pages, a
specification chart, banners and short video, plus packaging and in-hand stills,
interface renders, spec callouts, and certification mark and energy label images.
Every asset shows a device making claims, so each has to match the real product.

**Can product screens be simulated in ads?**

No rule read for this page addresses the "screen images simulated" line itself.
The rulings turn on presentation. A UK regulator upheld a complaint in 2024 over
a night image offered as footage from a dash cam when it was a stock photo. In
the US, an undisclosed mock-up presented as proof viewers can see is deceptive,
and fine print may not fix a misleading visual.

**How should certification marks appear in product images?**

Only as the product holds them. In China, products on the compulsory
certification catalog must be certified and marked before sale, and the mark
must be used correctly in advertising and product introductions. In the US, the
FCC logo is voluntary for devices authorized by supplier's declaration. The CE
marking is a manufacturer's declaration, not an approval. This is production
practice, not legal advice.

**Which parts of a tech launch can be AI-generated?**

Context, not proof. Lifestyle scenes, environments and backdrops can be
generated. Footage offered as what the device does, performance demonstrations,
and the marks and labels it holds come from the real product. A UK regulator
upheld 22 complaints in 2026 over footage that appeared to show real or
AI-generated puppies, and the advertiser stays responsible for AI output.

**How long does a launch content build take?**

No benchmark turned up in the sources checked for this page. The calendar
depends on the gates in the build plan: test evidence, certification and
authorization, and the energy class, then capture, generation and cut-downs. The
iFlytek AnyPin case page reports "weeks, not months" for one hybrid build. That
describes one engagement, not a standard.
