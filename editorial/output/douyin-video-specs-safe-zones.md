# Douyin video specs and safe zones, and the TikTok numbers standing in for them

Ask for Douyin video specifications in English and every answer you get is about
TikTok. The two apps share a parent company and a vertical frame. Whether they
share an interface is something nobody has published a comparison of, and the
safe-zone figures in circulation are almost all TikTok's.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What aspect ratio does Douyin use?

A 9:16 vertical frame at 1080 by 1920 pixels. All four sources that name a native
ratio give 9:16, and all four give that size. That is the most settled row on
this page. The safe zone is the least settled, and it is where the TikTok figures
have filled the gap.

<!-- SECTION: How to read these numbers -->

## How to read these numbers

These specs are not read from a creator backend. Douyin's creator surfaces serve
no specification text to anyone not logged in, so no open source reaches the
official value for the app.

What follows is the most commonly published figure for each spec, collected
across 12 independent sources in Chinese and English on 10 September 2026, with
the number of sources that agreed shown per row. Rows marked consensus had broad
agreement. Rows marked contested did not, and those carry the full spread
rather than a single number.

Treat this as the best available public reading, not as the rule. Platform
specs change without announcement and often without an English notice at all.
Before you build a batch to any number on this page, open your own creator
account and confirm it there. Reviewed 10 September 2026.

One exception runs through the page. Douyin's developer documentation for its
upload API is publicly readable, and where a figure comes from it, the row says
so and says what it covers.

<!-- SECTION: the readable doc -->

## The one platform text anyone can read

> Douyin's Open Platform documentation for its upload API, read on 10 September
> 2026, states that a video uploaded through that API may total up to 4GB, must
> be sent in chunks above 300MB, is best sent as MP4 or WebM, and may not run
> past 15 minutes. That documentation governs apps publishing through the API.
> It does not say the phone app or the web creator platform share those limits,
> and no source found says they do.
> Source: two Douyin Open Platform documentation pages fetched unauthenticated
> on 10 September 2026, text read directly. Primary reading for the API route
> only, not a statement of in-app limits.

That is a real platform statement, and it is narrower than it looks. It tells a
developer what the upload API accepts. It says nothing about what a creator
posting from the phone can upload, and treating it as app guidance is exactly how
one published page ended up repeating API limits as creator advice.

## Resolution, duration, codec and file size

> The most commonly published Douyin post format is a 9:16 vertical frame at
> 1080x1920 pixels. Four of four independent sources that name a native ratio
> give 9:16, and all four give 1080x1920.
> Source: 12 independent published references, 11 Chinese-language and 1
> English-language, collected 10 September 2026, counted per domain and reported
> as the most commonly published figure. Modal value, not a primary reading.

| Spec | Most published value | Spread across sources | Sources agreeing | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical | No conflicting value found | 4 of 4 | consensus |
| Resolution | 1080 by 1920 | No conflicting value found | 4 of 4 | consensus |
| Format | MP4 | WebM also named in the API documentation | 4 | consensus |
| File size cap | 4GB on 2026 pages | 1GB and 2GB on 2024 pages | 3, one copying the API doc | contested |
| Duration ceiling | 15 minutes | 30, 60 and 5 minutes, and a 1-minute figure, once each | 4, one copying the API doc | contested |
| Bitrate | No usable mode | About 1 to 15Mbps | 2 | contested |
| Cover frame | 9:16 at 1080 by 1920 | None published against it | 2 of 2 | contested, too thin for consensus |
| Maximum resolution, frame rate, codec | 4K, 30fps, H.264 with AAC | One source each | 1 each | contested |

File size and duration are the two rows most likely to be quoted to you with
confidence, and both split by when the page was written.

> Published Douyin upload limits split by when the page was written. Pages from
> 2024 give a 1GB or 2GB file cap. Pages from 2026 give 4GB. The most common
> duration ceiling is 15 minutes, on four sources, but one of them restates
> Douyin's API documentation as app advice, and a 2024 page still describes a
> one-minute limit for ordinary users that matches a 2019 rollout.
> Source: 7 independent published references carrying a file cap or duration,
> Chinese-language, collected 10 September 2026, grouped by publication date
> before counting. Modal values, contested, not primary readings.

A one-minute limit from a 2019 rollout, restated on a 2024 page, is the kind of
figure that survives in production briefs for years. Treat any duration you are
given without a date as suspect.

> Published Douyin bitrate advice runs from about 1Mbps to 15Mbps for the same
> 1080x1920 frame. The two pages calling 1Mbps "officially recommended" date
> from 2024 and link no official page.
> Source: 3 independent published references, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, every figure recorded and the spread reported rather than
> averaged. Contested, not a primary reading.

A fifteenfold spread on bitrate is not a spec. Pick a bitrate that keeps the file
comfortably under the smallest cap on the table, and do not trust a page that
calls one figure official without linking the official page.

## What is the Douyin safe zone?

There is no agreed figure, and the reason is that almost nobody publishes one for
Douyin.

> Only two Chinese-language sources publish Douyin-specific safe-zone insets,
> and they do not agree. The top band appears as 220 pixels and as 15 percent
> of frame height, about 288 pixels, on two pages of the same site. The bottom
> band appears as 200, 288 and 380 pixels. No Chinese source publishes a width
> for the right-side button column at all.
> Source: 5 independent published references addressing a Douyin safe zone,
> Chinese-language, collected 10 September 2026, every inset converted to a
> 1080x1920 canvas before comparison. Range published, contested, not read from
> the platform.

One site gives two top figures across two of its own pages. The bottom band has
three values. The right-side column, where the like and comment buttons sit, has
no Douyin figure anywhere in the Chinese record.

The surfaces are not treated consistently either.

> Of five sources addressing where Douyin's interface covers a video, one
> separates the in-feed view from the profile grid, where it says the cover
> shows as a 3:4 crop. The rest treat the feed as the only surface.
> Source: 5 independent published references, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, classified by whether they distinguish surfaces. The grid crop
> is found once and is contested.

## Where the TikTok figures come from

> Four English searches for Douyin video specifications returned roughly forty
> results and not one page carrying a Douyin spec table. TikTok guides filled
> the space. Every right-side button-rail width in circulation, between 100 and
> 140 pixels, comes from a TikTok page.
> Source: 4 English queries run 10 September 2026, every result classified by
> platform and page type, and every rail figure traced to its page. Stated as an
> observation about published sources.

So the rail width you have seen quoted for Douyin is TikTok's. That does not mean
the two apps differ. It means only one of them has had its interface measured in
public, and nobody has published a side-by-side of both.

## The safe box that clears both

With no agreed Douyin figure, the useful move is to find the region every
published figure leaves clear.

> Whichever published account of the Douyin overlay is correct, a layout that
> keeps text, faces and calls to action between 108 and 972 pixels across and
> between 288 and 1540 pixels down a 1080x1920 frame clears every Douyin inset
> in circulation. To clear the published TikTok insets as well, narrow it to 108
> to 940 across and 288 to 1436 down.
> Source: derived 10 September 2026 by taking the largest inset on each side
> from every published Douyin figure, and for the two-app box every published
> TikTok figure, across 5 Douyin and 11 TikTok sources. A safe intersection, not
> a platform rule.

| Box | Across | Down | Size | Clears |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Douyin box | 108 to 972 | 288 to 1540 | 864 by 1252 | Every published Douyin inset |
| Two-app box | 108 to 940 | 288 to 1436 | 832 by 1148 | Every published Douyin and TikTok inset |

Both boxes are derivations from published numbers, not measurements of either
app. If one master has to run on both platforms, design to the two-app box and
you are clear of every figure anyone has printed.

## Where do Douyin captions and end cards get covered?

On the published numbers, lower than a TikTok layout assumes.

> On the published figures, a caption placed just above a 270-pixel TikTok
> bottom inset sits about 110 pixels inside the 380-pixel bottom band that one
> Chinese source gives for Douyin. That is a comparison of two sets of published
> numbers, not a measurement of either app.
> Source: arithmetic on published TikTok and Douyin inset figures collected 10
> September 2026. Derived, contested on both inputs.

End cards follow the same logic. A call to action placed in the lower fifth of a
TikTok cut sits inside the largest published Douyin bottom band. Lift it into the
two-app box before re-exporting.

On sound, none of the twelve Douyin sources publishes an assumption about whether
viewers watch with sound on, so this page has no figure to give. Burned-in
captions inside the box cover both cases, which is a production choice rather
than a platform rule.

## Re-exporting a TikTok master for Douyin

A checklist built from the figures above. Every line is a derivation from
published numbers or a production choice, not a platform instruction.

| Check | What to do |
|---|---|
| Frame | 9:16 at 1080 by 1920 |
| Captions and on-screen text | Inside the two-app box, 108 to 940 across and 288 to 1436 down |

| Right side of frame | Keep faces and text left of 940 pixels, since no Douyin rail width is published |

## Questions about Douyin video specs

**What is the Douyin safe zone?**

No agreed figure exists. Two Chinese sources publish Douyin insets and disagree:
top bands of 220 and about 288 pixels, bottom bands of 200, 288 and 380 pixels,
and no right-rail width at all. Keeping text between 108 and 972 pixels across
and 288 and 1540 down clears every published Douyin figure, as a derivation.

**Can I upload a TikTok video to Douyin?**

The frame carries over: published Douyin figures give 9:16 at 1080 by 1920. What
needs moving is text. On published numbers, a caption just above TikTok's
270-pixel bottom inset sits inside the largest Douyin bottom band. Lift captions
and end cards into the two-app safe box before re-exporting.

**What aspect ratio does Douyin use?**

A 9:16 vertical frame at 1080 by 1920 pixels, given by four of four sources that
name a native ratio, collected on 10 September 2026. It is a counted published
value rather than a figure read from the app, so confirm it in your own creator
account before exporting a batch.

**How long can a Douyin video be?**

Fifteen minutes is the most common published ceiling, on four sources, but one
of them restates Douyin's upload API documentation as app advice, and older
pages still carry a one-minute limit from a 2019 rollout. The API documentation's
15 minutes applies to the API route only. Confirm the ceiling in your own account.

**Where do Douyin captions and end cards get covered?**

Published Douyin bottom bands run from 200 to 380 pixels and top bands from 220
to about 288 pixels, with no right-rail width published for Douyin. Captions
placed for TikTok can fall inside the largest Douyin bottom band. Keep text and
calls to action inside the derived two-app box.
