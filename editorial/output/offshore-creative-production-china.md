# Offshore creative production in China: what it involves in 2026

Search for offshore creative production and China appears as a name on a country
list. Search for anything China-specific and the results are about software, factory
sourcing or marine engineering. Nobody describes what running creative production
there actually involves for a foreign brand, so this page does, from the laws and
notices that govern it.

A note before the detail: the sections on data, rights, contracts and payment
describe production practice drawn from the published text of each instrument. They
are not legal advice.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What is offshore creative production in China?

It is producing campaign and commerce assets through a studio based in China, whether
shooting there, generating there or both. The creative part is familiar. What is
different is the operational layer around it: getting product samples in and out
through customs, handling files that contain people under Chinese data law, making
sure a commissioning contract actually transfers ownership, and working across a time
zone that barely overlaps Europe and never overlaps New York.

The reason brands look at China at all is proximity to the product.

> India held 2.8 percent of global manufacturing and China 28.8 percent.
> Source: India's Economic Survey 2024-25, tabled 31 January 2025, a
> share-of-global-output figure published by the government of the country it
> flatters less.

hubStudio has a stake here, and says so.

> hubStudio runs studios in Shanghai, Changsha, Hong Kong and the Philippines,
> and the company is registered in Hong Kong.
> Source: hubStudio's published company information page, read 10 September
> 2026.

The article comparing China and India for creative production covers where India wins
and where China does. This page is the next step: once China is on the list, what the
work involves.

## What engagement models are available?

Three, and they differ in what the buyer ends up managing rather than in price. No
published figure attaches to any of them.

| Model | What you buy | What you manage | Where it strains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated team | People's time, directed by you | The queue, the briefs, day-to-day direction | Your own review hours, across the time difference |
| Project | A scoped set of deliverables | Scope sign-off and acceptance | Changes after scope is agreed |
| Platform | A tool you operate yourself | Output quality and the whole workflow | Anything the tool cannot do, including capture |

Choosing between them is a question about where your own team's attention goes. A
dedicated team needs someone on your side awake during Chinese working hours. A
project needs a brief tight enough to quote. A platform needs someone who can run it.

The way brands buy production has not settled, which is part of why this question
keeps coming up.

> Two thirds of brands changed their agency model in the previous four years.
> Source: global advertiser trade body content production research, March 2025,
> 50 global brands across more than ten sectors, senior marketing procurement
> respondents.

## Does offshore production really save 40 percent?

The figure is real in the sense that it circulates. It is not real in the sense that
anyone can check it.

> A savings figure of about 40 percent circulates in this category, sometimes as
> a 40 to 60 percent band and as up to 80 percent on individual services. It is
> published as a percentage drawn from respondent answers, with no respondent
> count on the public pages, no absolute cost, no baseline, no currency and no
> unit of work.
> Source: a figure circulating in this category, September 2026, published as a
> percentage with no absolute figures and no sample size.

A percentage with no baseline cannot tell you what it saved against, and one with no
unit of work cannot tell you what was produced. The more useful exercise is to list
what a savings figure of that kind does not say it counts, because each item is a real
cost with a real instrument or calendar behind it.

| Cost the claim does not say it counts | Why it is real |
|---|---|
| Coordination and overnight review cycles | China's working day barely overlaps Europe and never overlaps New York |
| Onboarding and rework rounds | Every new supplier needs briefing, and early rounds rarely land first time |
| Sample shipping and a customs guarantee | Samples entering on temporary terms need a guarantee or a carnet |
| Rights clearance | Likeness use needs consent, and some edits need separate consent |
| Data paperwork | Files containing people can need a standard contract and an impact assessment |
| Travel | Anyone attending a shoot in person |

Wages are the obvious place to look for a savings ratio, and they will not support one.

> China's average annual wage in culture, sport and entertainment was 126,040
> yuan in urban non-private units in 2024 and 61,669 yuan in urban private
> units.
> Source: China's National Bureau of Statistics, 2024 average wages of employed
> persons in urban units, published 16 May 2025, gross pre-tax wage bill divided
> by average employment, by industry. The figure is not comparable with any
> foreign wage series and supports no savings ratio.

That figure is a national industry average, not a studio rate, and it measures a
different thing from any wage series in another country. Any savings ratio built by
dividing one country's wages by another's is an invention, however confidently it is
printed.

## Getting product samples in and out

This is the part of offshore production most buyers have never thought about, and it
is governed by a specific customs instrument.

> Product samples can enter China on temporary terms and leave again. The
> customs measures list samples among thirteen categories of temporary goods.
> They must be re-exported within six months of entry, in their original
> condition apart from normal wear, and customs may extend that period no more
> than three times, by no more than six months each time.
> Source: China's customs measures on temporary entry and exit goods, Order No.
> 233, issued 7 December 2017 and effective 1 February 2018, Articles 3, 6 and
> 10, read in the State Council Gazette text, September 2026.

Six months, in original condition. A sample that gets cut open for a product shot is
a sample that has changed condition, which is worth settling before it ships rather
than at re-export.

> Goods entering on temporary terms without an ATA carnet need a guarantee
> lodged with the local customs office. Under a carnet, China's trade promotion
> council stands as the national guarantor.
> Source: China's customs measures on temporary entry and exit goods, Order No.
> 233, Article 9, read in the State Council Gazette text, September 2026.

> China's customs accept inbound ATA carnets for three uses: exhibitions and
> fairs, professional equipment, and commercial samples. Professional equipment
> covers the photography, audiovisual and broadcast gear used for reporting,
> recording programs and filming. Carnets are not accepted for containers or
> repair parts.
> Source: China's national carnet issuing and guarantee body and a customs
> question-and-answer column in the state press, January 2023, read September
> 2026.

A carnet covers both samples and filming equipment, which makes it the cleaner route
for a shoot that brings gear in as well as product.

> Advertising materials and samples of no commercial value are exempt from
> import tariff, and certain temporarily imported goods may defer tariff if they
> leave again within six months. The law's own text sets no value threshold for
> "no commercial value," and the exemption covers tariff only.
> Source: the Tariff Law of the People's Republic of China, adopted 26 April
> 2024 and in force 1 December 2024, Articles 32 and 37, read on the national
> tax administration's law database, September 2026.

Tariff only. The exemption says nothing about other import taxes, so a sample is not
simply duty and tax free because it is a sample.

> No public source checked publishes a typical customs clearance time for
> inbound product samples.
> Source: Chinese-language searches of the customs instruments and national
> clearance statistics, September 2026. The only national clearance figure found
> covers all import cargo in 2021, not samples.

Plan for clearance time you cannot look up, which in practice means shipping samples
earlier than feels necessary.

## How does data and IP work with a China studio?

Most product work contains no people and none of what follows applies to it. The
moment a shoot includes a model, a creator or a customer, it does. This section
describes production practice, not legal advice.

> China's Personal Information Protection Law, in force since 1 November 2021,
> treats biometric identification, religious belief, specific identity, medical
> health, financial accounts and whereabouts as sensitive personal information,
> along with all personal information of children under fourteen. Processing it
> requires the person's separate consent.
> Source: the Personal Information Protection Law, Articles 28, 29 and 74, read
> on the Cyberspace Administration of China's site, September 2026.

> Before personal information leaves China, the law requires one of the routes
> it names, chiefly a regulator security assessment, a certification, or a
> standard contract with the overseas recipient. The person must be told who
> receives the data and why, and must give separate consent. The company must
> run a personal information protection impact assessment first and keep a
> record of it.
> Source: the Personal Information Protection Law, Articles 38, 39 and 55, read
> on the Cyberspace Administration of China's site, September 2026.

The 2024 provisions then set volume tiers for when each route is needed.

> Under China's 2024 cross-border data provisions, a company that is not a
> critical information infrastructure operator needs no export mechanism for
> non-sensitive personal information on fewer than 100,000 people a year,
> counted from 1 January. Non-sensitive information on 100,000 to under one
> million people, or sensitive information on fewer than 10,000, needs a
> standard contract or certification. At one million non-sensitive, or 10,000
> sensitive, a regulator security assessment is required, and a clearance lasts
> three years.
> Source: the Cyberspace Administration of China's Provisions on Promoting and
> Regulating Cross-border Data Flows, Order No. 16, issued and effective 22 March
> 2024, Articles 5, 7, 8 and 9, read on the regulator's site, September 2026.

| Personal information leaving China, per year | Route under the 2024 provisions |
|---|---|
| Non-sensitive, fewer than 100,000 people | No export mechanism needed |
| Non-sensitive, 100,000 to under one million | Standard contract or certification |
| Sensitive, fewer than 10,000 people | Standard contract or certification |
| Non-sensitive one million or more, or sensitive 10,000 or more | Regulator security assessment, valid three years |

The table hides the detail that catches production teams.

> The volume exemption covers non-sensitive information only. A talent file
> that counts as sensitive personal information has no volume floor, so even a
> handful of people puts it in the standard contract or certification band,
> unless another exemption in the provisions applies.
> Source: a reading of Articles 5 and 8 of the 2024 cross-border data
> provisions, September 2026. This describes production practice, not legal
> advice.

Whether a given file counts as sensitive is not something this page can decide for
you. What it can say is that the question has to be asked before files leave China,
not after.

There is one exemption worth knowing about for brands sending material in.

> The same provisions exempt personal information that was collected abroad,
> brought into China for processing and sent back out, provided no personal
> information or important data from inside China is added along the way.
> Source: the Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-border Data Flows,
> Article 4, read on the regulator's site, September 2026.

Likeness and edits carry their own consent rules.

> China's Civil Code bars making, using or publishing a person's likeness
> without consent. A disputed license clause is read in favor of the person
> portrayed, and a license with no agreed term can be ended on reasonable
> notice.
> Source: the Civil Code, Articles 1019, 1021 and 1022, in force 1 January 2021,
> read through Chinese legal commentary, September 2026.

> Under China's deep synthesis provisions, in force since 10 January 2023, a
> provider offering face or voice editing must prompt the user to inform the
> person being edited and obtain that person's separate consent.
> Source: the Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis Internet
> Information Services, Article 14, issued by the Cyberspace Administration of
> China, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the Ministry of
> Public Security, read on the regulator's site, September 2026.

> China's rules on labeling AI-generated content, in force since 1 September
> 2025, call for a visible label on generated images and video, an implicit
> label in the file's metadata, and a visible label that stays on exported or
> downloaded copies.
> Source: the Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content, issued 14
> March 2025 by the Cyberspace Administration of China and three other
> national bodies, read on the regulator's site, September 2026.

This page does not allocate that labeling duty between a brand, a studio and a tool
vendor. Ask each partner in writing how delivered files are labeled.

## Who owns the work you commissioned

This is the one to settle before anything is shot, because the default does not favor
the buyer.

> When work is commissioned in China, the contract decides who owns the
> copyright. If the contract is not explicit, or there is no contract, the
> copyright belongs to the party that made the work, not the party that paid for
> it. Transferring the economic rights takes a written contract.
> Source: the Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China as amended 11
> November 2020, in force 1 June 2021, Articles 19 and 27, read on the Ministry
> of Commerce's law database, September 2026.

Paying for the work does not make it yours. A written assignment does.

> Work an employee creates on assignment is an employment work. Outside the
> cases the law lists, the employee holds the copyright and the employer holds a
> priority right to use it within its business scope.
> Source: the Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 18,
> read on the Ministry of Commerce's law database, September 2026.

Two further instruments frame the contract itself.

> Parties to a contract with a foreign element may choose the law that governs
> it. Without a choice, the law of the habitual residence of the party whose
> performance best characterizes the contract applies, or the law most closely
> connected with it.
> Source: the Law on the Application of Laws to Foreign-related Civil
> Relations, Article 41, read through the Supreme People's Court gazette text
> and legal commentary, September 2026.

> China joined the New York Convention on enforcing foreign arbitral awards by a
> decision of its national legislature's standing committee on 2 December 1986.
> The convention took effect for China on 22 April 1987, with reciprocity and
> commercial reservations.
> Source: the Supreme People's Court notice on implementing the convention,
> carried in the court's gazette and by China's international economic and trade
> arbitration commission, read September 2026.

## Payment

One published rule is often misread as applying to foreign buyers.

> When money leaves China for services, a single payment above the equivalent
> of 50,000 US dollars needs a tax filing with the local tax authority before it
> is remitted, with listed exceptions. The rule governs outbound payments; it
> does not apply to a foreign brand paying into China.
> Source: State Taxation Administration and State Administration of Foreign
> Exchange Announcement 2013 No. 40, issued 9 July 2013, as republished by the
> foreign exchange regulator's Tianjin branch in April 2024, read September
> 2026.

It governs money going out of China, not a brand abroad paying a studio. How a studio
receives foreign currency is a separate question this page does not cover.

## Working hours and the calendar

> China runs on a single time zone, UTC+8. A nine-to-six working day in Shanghai
> overlaps a nine-to-six day in London by about one hour in winter and two in
> summer, in Paris or Berlin by two and three, and in Sydney by about seven
> hours on standard time and six on daylight time. It overlaps Los Angeles by
> one hour in the northern winter and not at all in summer. It never overlaps a
> New York working day.
> Source: published civil time offsets, arithmetic shown so the reader can check
> it, September 2026.

For a buyer in New York, every review is overnight. For a buyer in London, there is an
hour or two. That shapes the model more than any rate does.

> China's 2026 holiday schedule stopped work for nine days over the Spring
> Festival, 15 to 23 February, for five days at Labor Day, 1 to 5 May, and for
> seven days at National Day, 1 to 7 October. Weekend days were worked in
> exchange: 14 and 28 February, 9 May, 20 September and 10 October.
> Source: the State Council General Office notice on 2026 holiday arrangements,
> issued 4 November 2025.

> The following year's schedule is set by a separate notice; the 2026 notice
> was issued on 4 November 2025.
> Source: the State Council General Office notice on 2026 holiday arrangements,
> September 2026.

## How do I run a pilot before committing?

No benchmark exists for pilot length or success, so what follows is a template to fill
in, not a standard.

| Stage | What to settle | What you measure, on your own terms |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | One SKU or one small campaign, the deliverables, the engagement model | Whether the brief was understood first time |
| Rights | A written copyright assignment and every consent the shoot needs | Whether the paperwork arrived before capture |
| Samples | Temporary entry route, carnet or guarantee, and the re-export date | Whether samples moved on the date planned |
| Data | Whether any file contains people, and the export route if so | Whether the route was settled before files moved |
| Review | Who reviews, when, across the time difference | How many overnight cycles each round took |
| Handover | Final files, source files, provenance record, retention terms | Whether the handover matched what was agreed |

Two published studio policies are relevant to the scoping and handover rows.

> hubStudio sends a binding proposal within 48 hours of receiving a brief.
> Source: hubStudio's pricing page, read 10 September 2026, stated as published
> studio policy.

> hubStudio purges source files from working environments 90 days after a
> project closes, unless the client asks for them to be kept.
> Source: hubStudio's ecommerce design service page, read 10 September 2026,
> stated as published studio policy.

Those are one studio's policies, printed so the questions they raise can be put to any
supplier: how fast a binding scope arrives, and when source files are deleted.

## Questions about offshore production in China

**What is offshore creative production in China?**

Producing campaign and commerce assets through a studio based in China, by shooting,
generating or both. The creative work is familiar. What differs is the operational
layer: moving product samples through customs, handling files that contain people under
Chinese data law, securing ownership in writing, and working across a time zone that
never overlaps New York.

**Does offshore production really save 40 percent?**

The figure circulates without a respondent count, an absolute cost, a baseline, a
currency or a unit of work, so it cannot be checked. It also does not say whether it
counts coordination, overnight review, onboarding, rework, sample shipping, rights
clearance or data paperwork. National wage figures will not support a savings ratio
either.

**What engagement models are available?**

Three: a dedicated team, where you direct people and own the queue; a project, where
you sign off scope and deliverables; and a platform, where you operate a tool and own
output quality. They differ in what you manage rather than in any published price, so
choose by where your own team's attention can go.

**How does data and IP work with a China studio?**

Under Chinese copyright law, if a commissioning contract is silent, the maker owns the
work, so ownership needs a written assignment. Files containing people can fall under
the personal information law, and sensitive data has no volume floor before a standard
contract or certification applies. This is production practice, not legal advice.

**How do I run a pilot before committing?**

Treat it as a template rather than a benchmark, since none is published. Scope one SKU
or small campaign, settle the written rights assignment and consents, the sample entry
and re-export route, any data export route, the review schedule across the time
difference, and the handover terms, then measure each against what was agreed.
