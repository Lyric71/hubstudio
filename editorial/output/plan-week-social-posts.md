# How to plan and schedule a week of social media posts in one afternoon

The calendar is the quick part. The afternoon goes on making the pictures,
rewriting one idea for five networks and waiting for someone to say yes.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## How do you plan a week of social media posts?

Work in four blocks. Plan the week from one campaign brief, make each post
and its visual, adapt every post for the other networks, then send the week
for approval and load the schedule. In hubStudio all four happen in one
place, and each network's own limits are checked before anything goes out.

Here is the afternoon, block by block.

| Block | What you do | Where in hubStudio | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan | Write the week's brief, gather its files | Campaigns | Free |
| Make | Draft each post, render or pick its pictures and clips | The network modules, Shorts autopilot | Writing it yourself is free; AI drafts and renders show their price before you run |
| Adapt | Turn each post into versions for the other networks | Re-purpose for other networks | Manually is free until you press Draft; Draft at once bills each draft like one AI draft |
| Approve and schedule | Get sign-off, then queue every post | Send for validation, the publishing step | Validation and publishing to LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook and TikTok are free; each X post is priced before you send |

Every charge comes out of a prepaid balance held in real currency, and the
price is on screen before the run.

<!-- SECTION: The week on one screen -->

## What does a week of posts look like on one screen?

Say a small brand launches a waterproof jacket on Monday. One brief, six
posts, five networks.

| Day | Network | Idea | Visual | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | LinkedIn | Why we built it: the founder's note | One image, rendered in the post | Validated, scheduled 09:00 |
| Monday | Instagram | The jacket in real rain | Carousel of five, from the shoot in the campaign | Scheduled 18:00 |
| Tuesday | TikTok (Beta) | The designer on the taped seams | Clip cut by Shorts autopilot from the launch interview | Waiting for validation |
| Wednesday | X | Three things the fabric does, as a thread | Two pictures from the campaign | Draft |
| Thursday | Facebook | A customer's question, answered | One image, cropped to a portrait post | Draft |
| Friday | Instagram | Last day of launch week | A Story picture | Draft |

The grid leaves two things open: how often to post, and when.

The networks' own pages, read for this guide, give no number for either.

> "You don't need to post daily, but creating a content schedule and
> sticking to it will help you build momentum."
> Source: TikTok for Business, creative strategy for small businesses,
> updated September 2026, the network's own guidance, with no figure
> attached.

> Meta's planner suggests posting times from when your followers were most
> active on Facebook and Instagram over the past 7 days.
> Source: Meta Business Help Center, creating posts in Meta Business Suite,
> read October 11, 2026, a description of the network's own tool.

Pick a cadence your team can keep for a month, and let your own audience
data set the hours. The holiday content calendar 2026 shows how far ahead
the big retail dates need their assets.

## Plan: how do you build the week from one campaign?

Open Campaigns and click New campaign. Give it a name and a brief: the goal,
the audience, the key message, the dates. Then add the files the week will
draw on, from the product shots to last month's best clip.

A campaign points at files in the Assets Library and copies nothing, so one
picture can sit in several campaigns. While a campaign is picked in the
Campaign choice of a studio or a post, everything that page saves joins it.
By the end of the afternoon, the whole week sits under one name. The app's
Campaigns page shows the rest of what a campaign holds.

```prompt Campaign brief
Launch week for our new rain jacket, Monday to Friday. Audience:
commuters and weekend hikers who already own one rain jacket and don't
trust it. Key message: taped seams, packs into its own pocket. Tone: plain,
a little dry. Every post points to the product page. Avoid
"revolutionary" and anything about the planet we can't prove.
```

The brief pays off twice. A LinkedIn, Facebook or X post can be drafted from
the whole campaign: pick it under Skills and material, in Context folder from
the Assets Library, then Draft with AI. hubStudio reads the brief, the list
of files (each name and type, and the prompt behind a render) and the text of
up to 20 documents. It never looks at the pictures themselves. Say in the brief
what they show.

<!-- SECTION: Make -->

## Make: how do you write the posts and make the pictures?

Each network has its own module, and a post moves through it in steps,
from the brief or the visual to publishing.

![The LinkedIn module on a new post: the band with All posts, New post and the four step tiles, and the brief with Format, Emoticons, Language, the model picker on Balanced, Draft with AI and Write it myself](/Images/help/linkedin-brief.webp "A LinkedIn post starts from its brief: pick the format, then Draft with AI or Write it myself.")

On LinkedIn, Facebook and X you start from words. Pick the format (text
only, an image or a carousel), the language and the model, write the brief,
then press Draft with AI. Or press Write it myself, which opens the editor
with no AI call and nothing billed.

```prompt Post brief, LinkedIn
Founder's note for launch day. Two short paragraphs on why we spent a year
on seams nobody sees. First line has to work before "see more". End on the
product page, no hashtags in the body, three at the end.
```

Instagram and TikTok start from the visual. On Instagram you pick One image,
Carousel or Reel first; TikTok takes a video.

![The Instagram module on a new post: the band with the three step tiles, and the picture or the video step with One image, Carousel and Reel, Render with AI, Pick from the library and Upload from your computer, with the Skills and material strip unfolded beside it](/Images/help/instagram-picture.webp "On Instagram the visual comes first: render it, pick it from the library or upload it.")

The pictures come three ways: Render with AI inside the post, Pick from the
library, or Upload from your computer. A rendered carousel runs 2 to 8
slides. The price sits on the render button before you press it. A picture
takes a minute or two (a clip, a few minutes). Start the next post while
it works.

Two tools help with the visuals. The Image editor, which is free, crops
a picture to the network's format and checks it before you save. Shorts
autopilot cuts one long interview or talk into vertical clips, captioned,
with a caption for each network, ready to pick in the TikTok or Instagram
module.

## Adapt: how do you turn one post into posts for five networks?

Finish the strongest post first. Then, at the foot of its publishing step,
open Re-purpose for other networks, tick the networks and pick a way:

- **Manually.** Each network opens in its own tab with this post as the
  brief. Nothing is drafted until you press Draft there.
- **Draft at once.** hubStudio drafts every network's version and opens each
  in a tab. Each draft is billed like one Draft with AI.
- **Publish automatically.** The same drafts, sent on the accounts you tick.
  YouTube can't be ticked here.

A network that can't take the post is grayed out: Instagram needs a picture,
TikTok and YouTube a video. Each draft is shaped by that network's own format
skill, picked by default, which carries its posting rules on length, hashtags
and tone, so each version is rewritten rather than pasted.

The limits still differ from one network to the next.

| Network | Caption limit | What a post carries from hubStudio | How it goes out |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 3,000 characters | Text, one image or a carousel of 2 to 8; no video yet | Now or scheduled, profiles and company pages |
| Instagram | 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, 20 @ tags | One image, a carousel or a Reel; no text-only post | Now or scheduled, professional accounts: feed, Story, Reel |
| Facebook | None stated on Meta's pages; the editor shows the room left | Up to 10 pictures, or one clip of up to 20 minutes | Now or scheduled, pages |
| TikTok (Beta) | 2,200 characters | A video | Now or scheduled |
| X | 280 characters a post, emoji counting 2; longer copy goes as a thread | Text, one image or 2 to 4 pictures; no video yet | Now or scheduled; each post priced before you send |
| YouTube | Title 100, description 5,000 | A video | Prepared in hubStudio, published by you in YouTube Studio |

> Instagram captions take up to 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags and 20 @ tags;
> TikTok video captions up to 2,200; LinkedIn posts up to 3,000; X posts up
> to 280, with emoji counting as two.
> Source: Instagram and TikTok developer references, LinkedIn Help and X's
> developer documentation, read October 11, 2026, each network's own page.

Every limit on this page was read on the network's own page on October 11,
2026. Networks change them with little notice. Check again before a big
batch.

Two networks also cap how much one account can post in a day through an
app.

> Instagram's developer guide allows 100 API-published posts in a 24-hour
> moving period, a carousel counting as one. The same guide's carousel
> section says 50, and the quota endpoint reports a total of 50.
> Source: Instagram Platform developer documentation, Content Publishing
> guide and content publishing limit reference, read October 11, 2026, the
> platform's own API pages.

> TikTok caps posting through apps at "typically around 15 posts per day"
> per creator account, shared across every app that posts for it.
> Source: TikTok for Developers, Content Sharing Guidelines, updated August
> 4, 2026, the platform's own developer rules.

Both caps count per account, and a brand week sits far below them. An account
that posts at volume should plan to the lower Instagram figure. The
Instagram post sizes for 2026 guide covers the picture shapes.

## Approve and schedule: how do you get sign-off and load the queue?

Approval is the slow part. Send everything at once. On each post's
publishing step, Send for validation names a teammate, or one of the client's
people when the post is made for a client. The validator gets an email,
then validates or sends it back with a comment.

While it waits, the post is locked: nothing on it can be changed, deleted or
published until they decide. Sent back, it returns to draft, and the next
version stays on the same thread. A post tagged Made for a client also shows
in that client's Client space.

Once a post is approved, stay on the Publish automatically tab, tick the
accounts and click Schedule. Pick a day and a time, or a chip: In an hour, Tonight, 18:00,
Tomorrow, 09:00 or Monday, 09:00. Then Schedule it.

- The time is read in your own time zone, set in User Settings.
- A post can go out at least a couple of minutes ahead and up to a year away.
- One post can go to up to 20 accounts, or 20 pages on Facebook.
- hubStudio checks the queue every five minutes, so 09:00 means 09:00 to
  09:05.
- A temporary problem on the network's side is retried, three attempts in
  all. A content problem is not, and whoever queued the post gets an email
  saying what the network said.

YouTube is the exception. YouTube keeps every upload from an app it hasn't
audited private, so hubStudio prepares the video, its title and its
description, and Publish interactively hands them to YouTube Studio. You
pick Public or Schedule there.

The publish page of the app sets out what each network takes.

<!-- SECTION: Checklist -->

## What should you check before the week goes out?

Run this list once, at the end of the afternoon.

- Every post carries one idea from the campaign brief, in its network's own
  words.
- Captions sit inside each network's limit, and X copy that runs long is set
  to go as a thread.
- Every picture is in the network's shape. Instagram posts need one, and
  TikTok and YouTube posts need their video.
- Every post is validated, or waiting with a named validator and a date.
- Scheduled times read right in your own time zone.
- Check the daily caps if one account carries many posts.
- YouTube uploads are in your own calendar, since hubStudio doesn't send
  them.
- Next week starts faster if every file the week used sits in the campaign.

If the team would rather hand over the making, the social media design
service is the studio producing the same week to a brief. The LinkedIn
platform page covers what the studio makes for LinkedIn, and the how-to
guides carry the other workflows.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a week of social media posts?

Start from one campaign brief and its files. Lay the week out as a grid of
day, network, idea, visual and status, then work in four blocks: make the
posts and their pictures, adapt each one for the other networks, send them
for approval, and schedule them. Doing each block for the whole week at once
is what makes it fit in an afternoon.

### How far in advance can I schedule social posts?

In hubStudio, a post can be scheduled from a couple of minutes ahead to a
year away, read in your own time zone. The queue is checked every five
minutes, so a post set for 09:00 goes out by 09:05. Scheduling works on
LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok (Beta) and X. YouTube videos are
scheduled by you in YouTube Studio.

### How do I turn one post into posts for every network?

Finish one post, then open Re-purpose for other networks and tick the others.
Manually opens each network with the post as its brief; Draft at once writes
every version for you; Publish automatically drafts and sends them. Each
version follows its network's own format rules, and a network that can't
take the post, such as Instagram without a picture, is grayed out.

### How many posts a day can I publish on Instagram?

It depends which of Meta's pages you read. Instagram's developer guide allows
100 posts through an app in a rolling 24 hours, a carousel counting as one,
while the same guide's carousel section and its quota endpoint say 50. Plan
to 50 per account. A normal week of brand posts never comes close.

### Can a client approve posts before they are scheduled?

Yes. On the publishing step, Send for validation can name one of the
client's people when the post is made for that client. They get an email,
open the post, and validate it or send it back with a comment. Until they
decide, the post is locked and can't be published, changed or deleted.

### Can I schedule YouTube videos from the same place?

Not inside hubStudio. YouTube keeps uploads from apps it hasn't audited
private, so hubStudio prepares the video, a title and a description written
to YouTube's rules, then Publish interactively opens YouTube Studio with them
ready. You pick Public or Schedule there. The other five networks are
scheduled from hubStudio itself.
