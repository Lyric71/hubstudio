# How to make a white-background packshot with AI

An AI product photo on a white background can pass Amazon, Google and Shopify
with one file. Start from a photo of the real product, not from a prompt, and
build to the narrow band where the three rules overlap.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## How do you make an AI product photo with a white background that passes?

Edit a real photo of the product rather than generating one from words. In
hubStudio, upload it to the Edit an image job, ask for a pure white background,
render at 4K, then crop it square in the Image editor and save a 2048-pixel
JPEG. Check the white, the fill and the metadata before upload.

The main image is the one picture every listing has to get right. Amazon says
every product needs at least one compliant main image, and without one it "may
temporarily remove the product listing from search." Google says an image that
misses its requirements "won't appear on Google surfaces." Extra angles and
lifestyle shots are optional on both. The main image isn't.

<!-- SECTION: The rules -->

## What do Amazon, Google and Shopify require of the main image?

Each platform publishes its own rules, and none of the three pages carries a
date. So the date that counts is the day they were read: October 8, 2026, for
Amazon's US store, Google Merchant Center and Shopify's help center.

| Rule | Amazon (MAIN image) | Google Merchant Center (image_link) | Shopify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | Pure white, RGB 255, 255, 255 | No color set on the page | No rule; your theme decides |
| Product fill | 85 percent of the image | 75 to 90 percent (best practice) | No rule |
| Minimum size | 500 px on the longest side; 1,000 or more turns on zoom | 500 x 500 px, for all products from January 31, 2027; 1500 x 1500 recommended | None stated; 2048 x 2048 recommended for square |
| Maximum | 10,000 px on the longest side | 64 megapixels and 16 MB | 5000 x 5000 px or 25 megapixels, under 20 MB |
| Formats | JPEG (recommended), TIFF, PNG, non-animated GIF | JPEG and WebP recommended; PNG, GIF, BMP, TIFF | PNG best, then JPEG; WebP, TIFF and others accepted |
| Banned | Props not included, packaging unless it's a feature, any part of a mannequin, more than one unit, the product shown twice, placeholders, reviews, claims, Amazon logos and badges | Promotional text, price, watermarks, added logos, barcodes, borders, placeholders, mockups, products not sold together | Nothing stated on the media page |
| AI disclosure | Only for photorealistic people made entirely by AI | Metadata on every image made with generative AI | Nothing stated |

> Amazon's main image needs "a pure white background (RGB color values: 255,
> 255, 255)" and the product shown "as 85% of the image," whole, once and as a
> single unit. Read from Amazon's own rule text for the US store.
> Source: Amazon Seller Central, Product image guide, US store, page undated,
> read October 8, 2026. https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1881

> Google asks for a product that "takes up no less than 75%, but not more than
> 90%, of the full image," at 500 by 500 pixels or more, with 1500 by 1500
> recommended, and no file over 64 megapixels or 16MB. Read from Google's own
> help page for the main product image.
> Source: Google Merchant Center Help, Image link [image_link], page undated,
> read October 8, 2026. https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350

> Shopify takes product images up to 5000 by 5000 pixels or 25 megapixels,
> under 20 MB, and says "a size of 2048 x 2048 px usually displays best" for
> square product images. Read from Shopify's own help center.
> Source: Shopify Help Center, Product media types, page undated, read October
> 8, 2026. https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-media-types

Shopify sets almost nothing on its own. Its Google & YouTube channel, though,
"automatically syncs your products" to Google Merchant Center, so a store that
sells on Google inherits Google's rules for the same image.

<!-- SECTION: Where the rules differ -->

## Where do the three rules pull apart?

In four places, and each one changes how you make the file.

**Background.** Google's Merchant Center page for the main image sets no
background color at all. The line about "a solid white or transparent
background" sits on Google's Manufacturer Center page, as a best practice for
brands that submit product data. White is still the safe choice everywhere.
It's Amazon's rule, though, not Google's.

**Fill.** Amazon asks for 85 percent. Google wants 75 to 90 percent and warns
that it may test subtle crops of its own. Neither page says whether the
percentage is measured on the width, the height or the area. An 85 percent
frame sits inside both; a product cropped edge to edge falls outside Google's
window, and a small one floating in white misses both.

**Size floor.** It moves on a known date.

> Google enforces a 500 by 500 pixel minimum for every product from January 31,
> 2027. Until then the floor is 100 by 100 for most products and 250 by 250 for
> clothing, and images under 500 by 500 draw warnings. Read from Google's own
> help page on small images.
> Source: Google Merchant Center Help, How to fix: Image too small, page
> undated, read October 8, 2026. https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12159030

**AI disclosure.** This rule lives in the file, not in the picture, so no
amount of looking at the image will tell you whether it passes.

> "All images created using generative AI must contain meta data indicating that
> the image was AI-generated," for example the IPTC DigitalSourceType tag, and
> that tag must not be removed. One of the listed values covers "a composite
> that includes synthetic elements." Read from Google's own help page.
> Source: Google Merchant Center Help, Image link [image_link], page undated,
> read October 8, 2026. https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350

That composite value is the closest fit for a real product photo on an AI-made
background. Amazon's rule is narrower: it asks for the keyword
contains-synthetic-performer only when an image shows photorealistic people
generated entirely by AI. A packshot with no people needs no Amazon tag.

<!-- SECTION: One file for all three -->

## What file clears all three marketplaces?

Read the three pages together and one master file falls out of the overlap. It's
our reading of the rules, not a spec any of the platforms publishes.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Square, 1:1 | Shopify asks for one aspect ratio across main images and suggests a square size |
| Size | 2048 x 2048 px | Over Amazon's 1,000-pixel zoom line and Google's 1500 recommendation; Shopify's own square figure |
| Background | Pure white, 255, 255, 255 | Amazon's rule, accepted by the other two |
| Product fill | About 85 percent | Amazon's figure, inside Google's 75 to 90 window |
| Format | JPEG at the highest quality, RGB | Recommended by Amazon and Google, accepted by Shopify |
| Weight | Under 16 MB | Google's cap, the lowest of the three |
| Metadata | The AI tag kept | Google's rule |

Amazon adds one warning that matters for AI work: "Do not artificially enlarge
small images." Render at the size you need instead of stretching a small file
afterward. The
Amazon platform page and the Shopify platform page cover the rest of each
listing, from the gallery to A+ and store imagery.

## How do you make the packshot in the hubStudio app?

The app's create page walks through the image studio and the engines. For this
job the route runs from a real photo to a saved JPEG. The Image editor is free;
the renders, and the Improve with AI rewrite if you use it, are charged to your
prepaid balance at the price shown first.

- **Photograph the real unit.** One clear, evenly lit photo of the exact
  product and variant you sell, whole, from the front. This is what keeps the
  result honest. Amazon wants "the real scale, quantity, and color" and Google
  wants "the exact item being sold." A product generated from words alone is a
  mockup of something nobody shipped, and Google lists mockups as grounds for
  disapproval.
- **Add the skill.** In Skills, open the Catalog, read E-commerce packshot with
  View details, then click Add to my skills. From then on it shapes every Improve
  with AI rewrite of your image prompts.
- **Open the image studio on Edit an image.** Pick a ChatGPT Image engine: they
  are the ones with the Background setting. Drop the photo in the box. PNG, JPEG
  or WebP all work, and the app resizes the upload to 1,536 pixels on the long
  side before it goes out.
- **Write the change.** Paste the prompt below or write your own, and click
  Improve with AI if you want it tuned to the engine.
- **Set the options.** Shape to Square, Resolution to 4K, and Background to
  Opaque for a white render. Pick Transparent with PNG instead if you want a
  cutout you can reuse on other scenes, and render it on ChatGPT Image 2.5
  Flare or Sunburst: OpenAI still lists transparency on ChatGPT Image 2 as a
  preview. Ask for three or four images in one run.
  The price shows next to the button before anything renders, and a failed run
  isn't charged.
- **Crop and save.** Click Edit on the best result to open the Image editor. In
  Crop, pick Square and drag the box until the product spans about 85 percent.
  In Save, pick JPG, keep the quality at the top, and use the 2048 px quick pick.
  A transparent PNG saved as JPG turns white where it was transparent.
- **Keep the record.** Every render stays in History with its prompt and engine.
  If someone else signs off listings, send the final to them in Validation.

If you'd rather hand over the catalog, our studio shoots and builds marketplace
sets through the ecommerce design service. For prompt craft beyond this one job,
the other How-to guides go engine by engine.

## What prompt keeps the AI inside the rules?

Start with what must not change. The first sentence protects the product; the
rest describes the frame the three rules leave you.

```prompt
Keep the product exactly as it is in the photo: shape, color, label, printed
text, proportions and every detail. Replace the background with pure white,
RGB 255, 255, 255, edge to edge, with no gradient, no floor line, no vignette
and no cast shadow. Front view, centered, the whole product in frame, one
unit, nothing else in the picture. Even, soft studio light from the front
left. No props, no added reflections, no added text, no logo, no border.
Square frame, with the product filling about 85 percent of it.
```

The label printed on the product is fine to keep. Google's ban on logos covers
ones "added in post-production," and it allows them when they're a physical
part of the product. What you can't do is let the engine redraw that label into
something close but wrong.

<!-- SECTION: The checks -->

## How do you check the file before upload?

Open the final file at full size and run it against this list. One fail sends
it back.

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Corners and edges | A color picker reads 255, 255, 255 | Off-white values, a gradient, a gray floor line |
| The product | Same shape, color, label and text as the real unit | A redrawn label, changed proportions, extra parts |
| Product edge | Clean where product meets white | A halo, fringing or a soft outline |
| Fill | Whole product at about 85 percent | Edges touching the frame, or a small product lost in white |
| Count | One unit, shown once, with included accessories only | Two angles, props, packaging that isn't a feature |
| Overlays | None | Text, badges, a watermark, a border, a logo you added |
| File | 2048 x 2048 JPEG, RGB, under 16 MB | Under 1,000 px, WebP sent to Amazon, CMYK |
| Metadata, for Google | The file still says it was made with AI | The tag lost in a re-save |

For the metadata line, drop the final file on the Image anonymizer: it lists
what the file carries, including the tags AI engines write. Read the list and
close the page without downloading the clean copy. For a file bound for Google,
skip Download clean copy in the image studio too, since it removes those
markers. If a re-save has already stripped the tag, add it back with an
IPTC-compatible metadata editor before upload.

## FAQ

### Can I use an AI-generated main image on Amazon?

Amazon's product image guide doesn't ban AI. It asks for "a realistic,
professional-quality image" that shows the real scale, quantity and color of the
product. An AI edit of a real photo can meet that. A product invented from a
prompt can't, because it isn't the product you ship, and Amazon also bars
placeholders.

### Does Google Shopping require a white background?

Not on the Merchant Center page for the main image, read October 8, 2026. That
page bans promotional overlays, borders, watermarks and placeholders, and asks
for 75 to 90 percent fill. The white or transparent background line sits on
Google's Manufacturer Center page as a best practice. White remains the safe
choice.

### What size should a white-background product photo be?

2048 by 2048 pixels covers all three. It clears Amazon's 1,000-pixel zoom line,
Google's 1500 by 1500 recommendation and Shopify's suggestion for square images,
and it sits far under every maximum. Google's 500 by 500 floor applies to all
products from January 31, 2027.

### How much of the frame should the product fill?

About 85 percent. That's Amazon's figure, and it sits inside Google's 75 to 90
percent window. Neither platform says whether it measures the width, the height
or the area, so pick one method and use it across the catalog. Shopify sets no
fill rule.

### Do I have to label an AI product photo?

For Google, yes: every image made with generative AI must carry metadata saying
so, and the tag must not be stripped. For Amazon, only when the image shows
photorealistic people generated entirely by AI, tagged
contains-synthetic-performer. A packshot with no people needs no Amazon tag.
Shopify's media page sets no rule.

### Can I turn a transparent PNG into a white-background image?

Yes. Render with a transparent background on ChatGPT Image 2.5 Flare or
Sunburst in hubStudio, open the result in the Image editor and save it as JPG: the
transparent areas turn white. Sample the corners afterward to confirm 255, 255,
255, and keep the PNG as a cutout for lifestyle scenes.

### What about Tmall and JD white-background images?

They follow rules of their own that Tmall and JD keep behind a seller login.
The Tmall white-background image rules insight counts what published sources
agree on, and the JD image requirements piece sets JD beside Tmall. Confirm any
number in your own seller backend before you build a batch.
