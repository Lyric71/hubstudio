# The transcreation process for marketing, run as a production line

Transcreation goes wrong at the handoffs: new words laid over the same visual, or a
brief that never said what was allowed to change. Run it as a line and each handoff
gets an owner.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## What is the transcreation process for marketing?

It is a line of work run from a signed-off brief, not a per-word order. The source
campaign is audited for the target market, copy options come with back translations,
the visual is adapted to match, and the set is reviewed and checked against local
rules. hubStudio writes copy in-region and adapts the visual in the same line.

The professional definition starts in the same place.

> The Institute of Translation and Interpreting defines transcreation of
> advertising and marketing content as a professional practice that combines
> translation, copywriting and intercultural consultancy, and that usually, but
> not always, involves adaptation.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Guidelines drafted from a member survey run October
> 2024 to April 2025 and focus groups with academics, transcreators and
> transcreation companies from January to September 2025.

> As a process, the same code describes transcreation as taking an agreed brief,
> including the source copy and other brand and company documents, and producing
> at least one option built to maximize appeal to and impact on the target
> audience. It classes transcreation as a translation-related activity, not a
> type of translation.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines, no data behind the definition.

The code covers advertising and marketing content only. It leaves out literary texts,
non-promotional audiovisual work and video game localization, and it says plainly
that it is not training material.

<!-- SECTION: definitions -->

## How is transcreation different from translation and localization?

Translation answers to the source. Transcreation answers to the audience. The
instruments behind each term are different kinds of document, and the table says
which.

No standards-body or association definition of localization could be read for this
page. China's translators' association publishes a localization terminology standard,
but its definitions sit behind a member login. The localization row is this page's
own working definition, and it is labeled that way.

| Term | Defined by | What it covers | What kind of source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Translation | ISO 17100:2015; Institute of Translation and Interpreting | The processes and resources a quality translation service needs; a result that conveys the meaning and nuances of the original | International standard for the service, association guidance for the result |
| Post-editing | ISO 18587:2017 | Full, human post-editing of machine translation output, and the competences of post-editors | International standard, adopted identically in China |
| Transcreation | Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of Practice, August 2026 | Translation, copywriting and intercultural consultancy combined, for advertising and marketing | Association guidelines |
| Localization | This page's working definition | Adapting a campaign's language, formats and conventions for one market | No published definition could be read |

> A good translation "accurately conveys the meaning and nuances of the original
> text," according to the Institute of Translation and Interpreting, which also
> notes that the translation and interpreting industry is not regulated.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting guidance article, published
> September 2023, updated August 2026. Association guidance, no survey behind
> it.

That last clause matters for a buyer. With no regulator, the standards and the codes
are the only shared yardstick.

## Which standards apply to a transcreation line?

Three international standards touch the work, and none of the ones read for this page
names transcreation in its scope.

ISO 17100:2015, published in April 2015 and amended in 2017, is the translation
services standard. It sets requirements for the core processes, resources and other
aspects needed to deliver a quality translation service. Post-editing of machine
translation sits in a separate standard, ISO 18587:2017, published in April 2017,
which covers full, human post-editing and the competences of post-editors. Scope and
dates for both were read on a standards catalog record in September 2026.

> China adopted both standards nationally. GB/T 19363.1-2022 adopts ISO
> 17100:2015 and replaces a 2008 edition first issued in 2003, and GB/T
> 40036-2021 adopts ISO 18587:2017 as an identical standard.
> Source: the forewords of GB/T 19363.1-2022 and GB/T 40036-2021, as reproduced
> by the Translators Association of China, read September 2026. Standard
> forewords only; implementation dates not read.

The review stage has its own reference. ISO 5060:2024, published in February 2024,
gives guidance on evaluating human translation, post-edited machine translation and
unedited machine translation output, using error categories and penalty scoring. It
was read on one catalog record.

The people doing the work have a published baseline too.

> The Translators Association of China lists the basic competences of
> translators and interpreters as foreign language ability, translation or
> interpreting ability, language processing, domain knowledge, research,
> information retrieval, cultural ability and technical ability, with each
> assessed on documentary evidence.
> Source: Translators Association of China group standard T/TAC 2-2017, basic
> competence requirements for interpreters and translators, foreword read
> September 2026.

For transcreation itself, the instrument is the Institute of Translation and
Interpreting's code. It calls itself guidelines, and says that for companies it serves
as a set of industry standards. It adds attributes a translator's list does not carry:
copywriting skills, awareness of both markets, brand and marketing knowledge, and the
ability to explain choices through back translations and rationales.

A buyer researching the subject rarely meets any of this on the pages that rank.

> Across four phrasings a buyer would type, 28 of 31 results were published by
> sellers of translation or localization software or services. One came from a
> professional association, and none from a standards body.
> Source: search result review conducted 10 September 2026, four queries, every
> result classified by publisher type.

## What are the stages of a transcreation production line?

The code sets out the association's model.

> The Institute of Translation and Interpreting's code sets out a six-step
> transcreation workflow: agree a brief, gather and analyze information,
> produce the options, collect client feedback and amend, finalize and sign
> off, and keep a record of the work where the contract allows.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Workflow drawn from practitioner handbooks and
> contributor suggestions; the code notes that real practice varies.

The code treats transcreation as copy work. A campaign also has images, layouts and
rules. The table below is this page's production view, mapped to the code where the
code covers a stage. Visual adaptation and the compliance check are production
practice, not steps in the code, and the whole table describes practice, not a
measurement.

| Stage | Who owns it | What it hands on | Where meaning slips | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brief | Client and transcreator or agency; the client signs off | Master copy with visuals, audience, tone, formats, restrictions, options, rounds, budget, deadline | Nobody agrees how much may change | Code, first step |
| Source analysis | Transcreator or agency | A cross-cultural audit, and a view of how text, sound and visuals depend on each other | A visual that will not work locally goes unflagged | Code, second step |
| Adaptation | Client and transcreator, at the brief meeting | A decision for each element: keep it, tweak it or re-create it | Keeping the same visuals is assumed, not decided | Code, brief meeting |
| Copy | Transcreator | At least two options, each with a back translation and a rationale | The client cannot see what changed or why | Code, third step |
| Visual adaptation | Designer or studio | Layouts, on-image text and imagery rebuilt around the chosen option | The target copy does not fit the space the source left | Production practice |
| Review | Client comments; transcreator amends | Agreed feedback rounds, plus any agency quality check on its own grid | Copy and visual comments arrive on separate threads | Code, fourth step; ISO 5060:2024 for evaluation |
| Compliance check | Producer, with the client's legal reviewer | Copy and visuals checked against each market's advertising and labeling rules | A claim that was legal at home ships abroad | Production practice |

The code's last two steps close the line after the check: final sign-off by the
client, then a record of the work where the agreement and any NDA allow it.

The roles come from the code, not from an ISO standard. They are the client, the
client's in-region representative, the transcreator and, where an agency is
involved, its internal quality assurance.

> The code has the transcreator supply a back translation and a rationale for
> every option submitted, recommends always offering at least two options, one
> bolder and one safer, and records two rounds of client feedback as common
> practice.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines.

The back translation is the handoff tool. It lets a reviewer who does not read the
target language see what moved.

## Where does meaning get lost between the copy and the visual?

At the join, and the code names the misunderstanding that causes it.

> The Institute of Translation and Interpreting's code reports that most
> clients think transcreation means translating a few words while keeping the
> same visuals, and names visual and space constraints in the source copy as a
> standing challenge: an original visual that may not appeal to the local
> market, or a space that is not realistic for the target language.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Qualitative finding from member survey and focus group
> work; no share or count published.

> Transcreation "involves varying degrees of adaptation or even a complete
> rewrite."
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines.

A complete rewrite rarely fits a layout drawn for the original line. No method-stated
measurement of how much meaning is lost at that join turned up in research for this
page. The failure is documented as a pattern, not a rate.

China's translators' association already treats the two as one object. Its quality
assessment specification for localization judges the translation and the
target-language typesetting together.

The fix is organizational. List the visual in the brief as an input. Measure the space
for each format before copy is written. Put the designer in front of the options before
the client chooses. On marketplace work, hubStudio does both halves in one place.

> "We write the Chinese copy in-region, with copywriters who know how Chinese
> shoppers read a detail page, and we adapt the visual system to match."
> Source: hubStudio ecommerce design service page, read 10 September 2026.
> First-party statement of practice, not a measured figure.

## What does the compliance check catch?

Claims and labels that travel badly. This section describes production practice, not
legal advice.

A superlative is the classic case. A line that says "best" in the source market can
land on a banned term in China.

> China's Advertising Law bans "national level," "highest grade," "best" and
> any term of the same or similar meaning in advertising, and sets a fine of
> 200,000 to 1,000,000 RMB on the advertiser for a breach, with the same band
> on the agency and the publisher.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China, Articles 9(3) and
> 57, 2021 amendment. Statutory text, no sample.

If any image in the adapted set is generated, the labeling rules follow it into
every copy that ships.

> China's Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content, issued 14
> March 2025 by four central bodies and effective 1 September 2025, require a
> user-perceivable label on generated images and video and a second, machine
> label written into the file metadata carrying the synthetic attribute, the
> provider identifier and a content number, and require that exported and
> downloaded copies still carry the visible label.
> Source: Cyberspace Administration of China announcement, 14 March 2025. The
> regulator's own published instrument, effective 1 September 2025.

That is why the check sits after visual adaptation and before sign-off. It has to see
the copy and the image together.

## What goes in a transcreation brief?

Everything the line needs to decide before anyone writes.

> "A clear and detailed brief is the point of departure. No transcreation can be
> started without one."
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines.

> Under that code the client supplies brand information, campaign objectives,
> the master copy including its visuals, a target audience profile, preferred
> tone and register, the final formats and any restrictions. The transcreator
> runs a cross-cultural audit of the campaign, and both sides agree the room for
> creativity, the number of options, the number of feedback rounds, the budget
> and the deadline, with the client's in-region representative in the room.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines, no data.

The template below is built from the code's first step and its example brief, and
credited to the code. Rows marked production practice are this page's additions for a
line that adapts the visual as well as the copy.

| Brief field | What to write | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Product or service | Features, benefits, points of parity and points of difference | Code, example brief |
| Brand | Mission, vision, purpose, tone of voice and personality | Code, example brief |
| Campaign purpose | The objective, and the response the reader should have | Code; Institute guidance article |
| Target audience | Gender and sexuality, age, education, profession, ethnicity and race, location, communities, values and beliefs | Code, example brief |
| Master copy | The source copy with its visuals attached, never the text alone | Code, first step |
| Tone and register | The preferred tone and register in the target language | Code, first step |
| Media and formats | Print, digital billboard, subtitles, social banners and captions, TV or online video, and the final file formats | Code, example brief |
| Restrictions | Anything that may not change | Code, first step |
| Cross-cultural audit | Each issue flagged, and how it will be handled | Code, first step |
| Room for creativity | For each element: keep, tweak or re-create | Code, brief meeting |
| Options | At least two, one bolder and one safer, each with a back translation and a rationale | Code |
| Feedback rounds | How many, and that they sit inside the budget | Code |
| Visual inventory | Every image, layout and line of on-image text that carries copy | Production practice |
| Space per format | The room the target language has in each placement | Production practice |
| Compliance by market | The advertising, disclosure and labeling rules that apply | Production practice |
| AI use and data | Whether AI drafting is allowed, and how client data is kept out of model training | Production practice, from the code's note on data protection |
| Budget, deadline, sign-off | The budget, the date, the in-region representative and who signs off | Code, first step |

## How is transcreation priced?

By time or by scope, not by the word. That is the association's rule, and it is where
buyers and practitioners most often talk past each other.

> The Institute of Translation and Interpreting's code says transcreation is
> priced like copywriting and usually above traditional translation. It is paid
> by the project as a flat fee, by the hour, sometimes on retainer, or by the
> day, and "transcreation is never paid per word."
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines, no rate figures published.

> Where the work runs on a flat fee, the code has the parties fix the number of
> versions or a clear list of deliverables up front, including the back
> translation and rationale for each version and the number of feedback
> rounds. The same code reports clients still asking for per-word quotes.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. Association guidelines drawn from member survey and
> focus group work, no counts published.

> "The cost of transcreation is the cost of the time it takes to come up with
> the final copy."
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting guidance article, published
> November 2023, updated August 2026. Association guidance, no rate data.

The units differ because they count different things.

| Pricing unit | What it counts | Used for | Published by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per source word | Words in the source text | Translation | Translation category rate cards, September 2026 |
| Per word, discounted by match rate | New text against text already held in translation memory | Localization | China's translators' association, quotation specification |
| Flat project fee | A fixed number of versions or deliverables, with back translations, rationales and feedback rounds | Transcreation | Institute of Translation and Interpreting code |
| Hourly, sometimes on retainer | Time | Transcreation | Institute of Translation and Interpreting code |
| Daily | Time | Transcreation | Institute of Translation and Interpreting code |

The only band this page carries is for translation, and it comes without a method.

> Published rate cards in the translation category put professional English to
> Simplified Chinese at roughly $0.08 to $0.18 per source word, and price
> transcreation per hour or per project instead, because source word count does
> not track the creative work.
> Source: published pricing pages collected across the category, September 2026,
> no company named. Rate cards only, no survey and no method published behind
> the band.

> The Translators Association of China's localization quotation specification,
> first released in October 2013, carries an appendix tying unit price to the
> translation memory match rate, the structure behind per-word pricing that
> discounts repeated text.
> Source: Translators Association of China, localization services quotation
> specification ZYF 001-2013, appendix titles read September 2026. Full text
> member-only.

Match-rate discounts reward repetition. Transcreation is paid for the opposite.

hubStudio's pricing page lists product copy and transcreation under its asset-based
buying model, and the same page says how the work is staffed.

> "Transcreation is handled by people who write natively in the target language,
> not translated into it."
> Source: hubStudio pricing page, read 10 September 2026. First-party statement
> of practice, not a measured figure.

What an adaptation costs once the re-shoot and re-layout are counted is covered in the
cost to localize a campaign for China article. The campaign adaptation cost per market
article sets out the ladder of adaptation jobs, from a copy swap to a rebuild.

## Can AI be used in transcreation?

The two published views point different ways, and they answer different questions.

> GenAI use in translation reached 34 percent of respondents among 50 global
> brands, with another 23 percent planning to adopt it.
> Source: global advertiser trade body content production research, March 2025.
> Survey of senior marketing procurement respondents at 50 global brands across
> more than ten sectors.

> The Institute of Translation and Interpreting's code takes the position that
> generative AI is not useful for transcreation work, citing missing human
> skills, confidentiality, unreliability, intellectual property risk and a lack
> of originality, and says it has already taken a share of the transcreation
> market.
> Source: Institute of Translation and Interpreting, Transcreation Code of
> Practice, August 2026. A position statement from an association of
> practitioners; no usage or quality data published behind it.

Neither settles the matter. The survey measures adoption in translation, not the
quality of transcreated output. The code is a position, written by an association
whose members do the work, with no usage or quality data behind it.

Where machine output is edited by people, ISO 18587:2017 sets the requirements for
full, human post-editing. And the brief can settle the practical questions: whether
AI drafting is allowed on the job, and how client data is handled. The code asks
agencies running their own AI platform to brief clients on data protection,
non-disclosure and non-retention, so client material is not used to train it.

The AI content production page describes the production work these briefs feed, and
the brands solutions page covers multi-market transcreation for brand teams.

## Questions about the transcreation process

**What is the difference between translation and transcreation?**

Translation carries the meaning and nuances of the original text, and ISO 17100:2015
sets requirements for the service. Transcreation, as the Institute of Translation and
Interpreting's August 2026 code defines it for advertising and marketing, combines
translation, copywriting and intercultural consultancy, and usually involves
adaptation. The code classes it as a translation-related activity, not a type of
translation.

**How does a transcreation process work?**

It starts from a signed-off brief. The Institute's code sets six steps: agree the
brief, gather and analyze information, produce options, collect feedback and amend,
finalize and sign off, and keep a record. A production line adds visual adaptation and
a compliance check, so the copy and the image leave together. Two feedback rounds are
common practice.

**Is there a standard for transcreation?**

None of the standards read for this page names transcreation in its scope. ISO
17100:2015 covers translation services, ISO 18587:2017 covers human post-editing of
machine translation, and ISO 5060:2024 covers evaluating translation output. The
Institute of Translation and Interpreting's Transcreation Code of Practice, August
2026, addresses it directly, as association guidelines.

**How is transcreation priced?**

By the project, the hour or the day, according to the Institute of Translation and
Interpreting's code, which says transcreation is never paid per word and is usually
priced above traditional translation. Published translation rate cards collected in
September 2026 put professional English to Simplified Chinese at roughly $0.08 to
$0.18 per source word, with no method behind the band.

**Can AI do transcreation?**

The published views point different ways. The Institute of Translation and
Interpreting's code takes the position that generative AI is not useful for
transcreation work. A global advertiser trade body survey from March 2025 found 34
percent of respondents at 50 brands using it in translation, with 23 percent more
planning to. Settle AI use and data handling in the brief.
