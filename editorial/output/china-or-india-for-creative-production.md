# China or India for creative production: an honest comparison

Search this question and you get two kinds of page. Software-outsourcing
comparisons written fifteen to twenty years ago, and offshore creative
production articles that treat India as the default and never evaluate China at
all. Neither compares the two regions on what a production buyer actually
decides on.

This page does, from government statistics and named instruments, and it starts
with where India wins. hubStudio operates studios in China and in the
Philippines, so it has a side. Conceding first is the only way the rest of it
is worth reading.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## Is China or India better for creative production?

Neither, as a general answer. The question resolves on four specifics: whether
your work is English-first, whether it has to reach a European working day,
whether the product physically exists near where it is made, and whether it
runs on Chinese platforms.

India wins the first two on the evidence below. China wins the second two, and
the margin on category proximity is not close. If your answer set splits across
both columns, that is a real result and worth knowing before anyone quotes.

<!-- SECTION: India -->

## Where does India win on creative outsourcing?

Five places, and they are the reasons India became the default.

**English, by a measurable margin.**

> India scores 484 and ranks 74th; China scores 464 and ranks 86th. Both sit in
> the index's low band and below the 488 global average.
> Source: the 2025 edition of a published English proficiency index covering 123
> countries and regions, scored from the results of 2.2 million adults who took
> the publisher's free online English test, a self-selected sample.

Twelve places apart, on a self-selected sample of people who chose to take a
free online English test. That is a real gap and a weak instrument, and both
things are true at once. Note also where both countries land: the low band. The
honest version of India's advantage is that it is better, not that it is good
by the index's own standard.

**The European working day, which is arithmetic rather than opinion.**

> India Standard Time runs at UTC+5:30 and China Standard Time at UTC+8, one
> zone for the whole country. A nine-to-six day in India overlaps a nine-to-six
> London day by about 3.5 hours in winter and 4.5 in summer. The same day in
> China overlaps it by about one hour in winter and two in summer. Neither
> overlaps a nine-to-six New York day at all.
> Source: published civil time offsets, arithmetic shown so the reader can check
> it, September 2026.

Three to four times the daily overlap with London. For work that needs live
review rather than handover, that difference decides the week. And the last
sentence matters just as much: a US buyer choosing between these two regions is
choosing between two overnight relationships, not between one live one and one
overnight one.

**A lighter cross-border data regime.** Covered in full further down, because it
deserves more than a bullet.

**Incumbency in English-language production.** The SERP itself is the evidence.
Every page that ranks for offshore creative production treats India as the
starting point. Two decades of that produces working practices, contract
templates and account managers who have done it before, and none of that shows
up in a statistic.

**A large arts-heavy graduate base.**

> India had 45.0 million students enrolled in higher education in 2023-24, a
> gross enrollment ratio of about 30 percent, with 59,533 of 64,756 registered
> institutions responding. Arts is the largest undergraduate grouping at 32.1
> percent.
> Source: India's Ministry of Education, All India Survey on Higher Education
> 2023-24, released 30 August 2026, a web-based return completed by each
> institution at over 90 percent participation.

Arts as the single largest undergraduate grouping is a genuine structural
advantage for creative work, and it is published by India's own education
ministry rather than inferred.

## What this comparison cannot honestly do

Four things, and every page that pretends otherwise is making them up. Naming
them is not throat-clearing. It is most of what separates this page from the
ones above it.

**Nobody can size the creative talent pool.**

> Neither release publishes a design or creative-arts graduate count, so the
> creative talent pool cannot be sized from public statistics on either side.
> Source: both government releases read in full, September 2026.

Two national education ministries, tens of millions of students each, and no
published count of design graduates in either. Any article giving you one has
either found a private dataset it is not naming or made it up.

**Nobody can compare the wages.** Both countries publish an earnings figure. The
figures are not comparable, and the reason is worth spelling out because the
comparison is so tempting.

> China's average annual wage in culture, sport and entertainment was 126,040
> yuan in urban non-private units in 2024, down 1.0 percent nominally, and
> 61,669 yuan in urban private units, up 3.8 percent. The all-industry averages
> were 124,110 and 69,476 yuan.
> Source: China's National Bureau of Statistics, 2024 average wages of employed
> persons in urban units, published 16 May 2025, gross pre-tax wage bill divided
> by average employment, by industry.

> India's average monthly earnings for regular wage and salaried employees were
> 20,702 rupees in 2023-24, across all industries with no creative breakout.
> Source: India's Periodic Labour Force Survey annual report for July 2023 to
> June 2024, a rotational-panel household survey of self-reported earnings.
> This figure is not comparable with the Chinese one: monthly against annual,
> all salaried workers against one named industry, household survey against
> establishment survey.

Monthly against annual. All salaried workers against one named industry. A
household survey of what people say they earn against an establishment survey
dividing a wage bill by headcount. Any multiple built from those two numbers is
fabricated, however confidently it is printed, and you will see it printed.

**Nobody publishes physical studio capacity.** No sound-stage count, no
studio-floor area, for either country. Screen counts and film certification
counts exist and are not substitutes for them.

**Nobody publishes manufacturing share by category.** The aggregate exists. The
split for electronics, beauty, fashion or appliances does not, in any source
this page would cite.

Those four gaps are why the sections below argue from structure and from
instruments rather than from a scoreboard.

## How big is the talent pool on each side?

One thing can be compared here, and it is worth doing carefully because it is
the closest either country comes to an answer.

> China had 48.46 million people in higher education of all kinds in 2024, a
> gross enrollment ratio of 60.80 percent, and produced 10.5938 million
> undergraduate and junior-college graduates that year.
> Source: China's Ministry of Education, 2024 National Education Development
> Statistical Communique, published 11 June 2025, a compulsory annual return
> from every approved institution.

Set that beside India's 45.0 million enrolled at a gross enrollment ratio of
about 30 percent, and two different pictures emerge from numbers that look
similar.

The enrollment totals are close, within about 8 percent of each other. The
enrollment ratios are not: roughly 61 percent against roughly 30 percent. That
ratio is the share of the relevant age cohort in higher education, so China is
running at about twice India's participation rate against a population of
broadly comparable size. Read the other way, India has far more headroom, since
a 30 percent ratio means most of the cohort is not enrolled yet.

One thing that looks comparable here is not. China's release publishes an
annual graduate count, 10.5938 million. India's does not publish an equivalent
figure, so the two output flows cannot be set against each other, only the
enrollment stocks and the ratios.

Two cautions before anyone builds a plan on that. The instruments differ: the
Chinese figure is a compulsory annual return from every approved institution,
the Indian one a web-based survey at over 90 percent participation, which is
high but not compulsory. And neither number tells you anything about design
graduates specifically, which is the gap named above and the one that actually
matters here.

Where India's release does help is composition. Arts being the largest
undergraduate grouping at 32.1 percent is a published fact about the shape of
the pipeline, and China's communique publishes no equivalent breakdown that
would let the same statement be made or refuted on the other side. So on
composition, India has evidence and China has none, which is a point for India
however you read it.

<!-- SECTION: China -->

## What is China better at for creative production?

Three things, and the first is the one that actually moves budgets.

**Category proximity to where the product is made.**

> India held 2.8 percent of global manufacturing and China 28.8 percent.
> Source: India's Economic Survey 2024-25, tabled 31 January 2025, a
> share-of-global-output figure published by the government of the country it
> flatters less.

One instrument carrying both numbers, published by India's own government,
which is why it is the figure worth citing. Ten times the share, and note that
this is aggregate manufacturing, not a category split, because no source
publishes the split.

What that aggregate means operationally is simple. If the product is made in
Guangdong or Zhejiang, then the sample, the packaging variant and the color
correction against a physical unit are a domestic courier away rather than an
international shipment and a customs form. A reshoot that takes two days in one
region takes two weeks in the other, and no rate card captures that.

**Production infrastructure at scale.**

> China produced 612 feature films and 873 films of all kinds in 2024, sold 1.01
> billion cinema admissions, and ended the year with 90,968 screens after adding
> 4,658.
> Source: the China Film Administration's own annual count, published 2 January
> 2025.

India's certification body publishes a count too, and it is important not to
misuse it.

> India's film certification body certified 17,431 films in 2023-24 and 71,963
> over five years. That count includes short films, promos, songs and trailers,
> so it does not sit against China's 612 features.
> Source: figures given to the Rajya Sabha by India's Ministry of Information
> and Broadcasting, financial year 2023-24.

Those two figures are counting different things, and setting 17,431 against 612
would be dishonest arithmetic. What the Chinese numbers do support is narrower
and still useful: an industrial base adding nearly five thousand screens in a
single year is one with crew depth, equipment rental and post capacity behind
it. The screens are a proxy for the industry, not for studio floors, which
nobody publishes.

**Native platform fluency, which is location-bound.**

Douyin, RedNote and Tmall are China-domiciled platforms
whose seller backends, rule centers and review queues operate in Chinese. The
specification that matters is not the one in the English-language help article.
It is the one in the seller backend, which changes without an English
announcement, and which a team outside the market cannot see.

This is the part of the comparison that is structural rather than a matter of
degree. A team can learn a platform's published specs from anywhere. It cannot
watch a review queue reject an asset at eleven at night and adjust the next
batch before morning unless it is inside the market and inside the language.

The practical shape of that gap shows up in three places. Rule changes arrive
in the backend before they arrive in any English documentation, and sometimes
they never arrive in English at all. Rejection reasons come back as Chinese
strings written for sellers, not as error codes written for developers, so
interpreting them is a language task before it is a technical one. And the
review queues themselves run on Chinese business hours, which means the loop
between submission, rejection and resubmission either closes the same day or
loses one to the calendar.

None of that is an argument that the work cannot be done from elsewhere. Plenty
of it is, and badly is not the same as impossible. It is an argument that the
cost of doing it from elsewhere is paid in schedule rather than in rate, which
is why it never appears in the comparison that a buyer is usually shown.

## What neither region solves

Worth saying plainly, because a regional decision gets asked to carry more than
it can.

**The US working day.** Neither region overlaps a nine-to-six New York day.
Choosing between them on time zones only makes sense for European hours, and
for American ones both are overnight relationships that live or die on how good
the handover document is.

**Origination versus adaptation.** Neither region is inherently better at
making the master asset rather than versioning someone else's. That is a
question about the individual studio's capability and equipment, not about the
country it sits in, and no national statistic will answer it. Ask it directly
in the review instead.

**The coordination overhead.** Both regions add a layer of brief-writing,
review scheduling and specification discipline that a local supplier does not
require. That cost is real, it does not appear on any rate card, and it is
roughly the same on both sides.

## How do IP and data rules differ?

Directly, then, since dodging this is the standard move.

India has the lighter regime for moving personal data out of the country, and
it is not close.

> India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act was enacted in 2023 and its
> implementing Rules were issued on 14 November 2025, with core obligations
> phased in over eighteen months. Transfers out of India are permitted unless
> the central government restricts a named destination, with localization only
> for categories specified for Significant Data Fiduciaries.
> Source: the instruments by name, read through independent published legal and
> policy analyses, September 2026.

A negative list. Transfer is permitted by default and restricted by exception.

> China's Personal Information Protection Law has applied since 1 November 2021.
> The Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-border Data Flows, effective
> 22 March 2024, require a regulator security assessment before exporting any
> important data, any personal information held by a critical information
> infrastructure operator, non-sensitive personal information on more than one
> million people in a calendar year, or sensitive personal information on more
> than ten thousand. A clearance lasts three years.
> Source: the instruments by name, read through independent published legal
> analyses, September 2026.

Named thresholds with a regulator in front of them. On personal data export,
India is the lighter regime, and a buyer whose work involves moving customer
data across a border should weigh that seriously.

Now the part that gets left out. Most creative production does not export
personal data at all. Product photography, packaging renders, campaign
adaptation and ecommerce imagery generate files about objects, not about
people. The thresholds above are counted in people, and a shoot of five hundred
skincare units contains none.

Where it does bite is talent. A shoot with recognizable faces, a creator
campaign, a database of user-generated content, a model release archive: those
are personal information, and that is the point at which the Chinese
thresholds start to matter and the assessment timeline enters your schedule.
The honest framing is not "China is hard for data." It is that the answer
depends on whether people are in your files, and most product work has nobody
in it.

| | China | India |
|---|---|---|
| Default posture on personal data export | Assessment required above named thresholds | Permitted unless the destination is restricted |
| Thresholds | 1 million non-sensitive, 10,000 sensitive, any important data, any CIIO transfer | Localization only for specified categories, for Significant Data Fiduciaries |
| Clearance validity | Three years | Not applicable in the same form |
| Bites hardest on | Talent, creator campaigns, user data | Fewer creative production cases |
| Irrelevant to | Product-only files with no personal information | Same |

## Which region suits which work

Run your own brief through this rather than taking a verdict.

| If this is true of the work | Points to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| English-first copy, live review with a London day | India | 3.5 to 4.5 hours of daily overlap against about one to two |
| The product physically exists and is made in Asia | China | 28.8 percent of global manufacturing against 2.8 percent |
| Runs on Douyin, RedNote, Tmall or WeChat | China | Backends, rule centers and review queues operate in Chinese |
| High-volume ecommerce content against a physical catalog | China | Sample logistics are domestic, and reshoots are days not weeks |
| Moves customer personal data across a border | India | Transfer permitted unless the destination is restricted |
| Product-only files, no people in them | Either | The data thresholds are counted in people and yours has none |
| Needs a US working-day overlap | Neither | Neither region overlaps a nine-to-six New York day at all |

Two rows there deserve reading twice. The last one, because buyers often carry
an assumption that one of these regions solves a US time-zone problem, and
neither does. And the product-only row, because it removes the objection most
often raised against China from most of the work most brands actually buy.

## How this page was built

Every figure above comes from a government release or a named legal instrument,
read in September 2026, with the publishing body and the collection method in
the source line. Where one instrument carries both countries' numbers, that
instrument was preferred, which is why the manufacturing share comes from
India's own Economic Survey rather than from two separate sources.

Four comparisons were attempted and abandoned because the public data does not
support them: design graduate counts, a wage ratio, physical studio capacity,
and manufacturing share by category. They are named in the body rather than
quietly skipped, because their absence is the most useful thing this page can
tell you about every other page on the subject.

No company in either country is named anywhere here, and no figure is
attributed to one. The comparison is regional, which is the only level at which
public statistics can support it. Reviewed September 2026.

## Questions buyers ask about offshoring creative work

**Is China or India better for creative production?**

It depends on four things: whether the work is English-first, whether it needs a
European working-day overlap, whether the product physically exists near where
it is manufactured, and whether it runs on Chinese platforms. India wins the
first two on published evidence. China wins the last two, with category
proximity the widest margin.

**Where does India win on creative outsourcing?**

English proficiency, by 484 to 464 on a 2025 index of 123 countries. Working-day
overlap with London, about 3.5 to 4.5 hours against roughly one to two. A
lighter cross-border data regime, where transfer is permitted unless a
destination is restricted. Two decades of incumbency in English-language
production. And arts as its largest undergraduate grouping, at 32.1 percent.

**What is China better at for creative production?**

Proximity to manufacturing, at 28.8 percent of global output against 2.8
percent, which makes samples and reshoots domestic rather than international. An
industrial production base that added 4,658 cinema screens in 2024. And native
fluency on Douyin, RedNote and Tmall, whose backends and review queues operate
in Chinese and cannot be worked from outside the market.

**How do IP and data rules differ?**

India permits personal data transfer out unless the government restricts a named
destination. China requires a regulator security assessment above published
thresholds, including one million non-sensitive or ten thousand sensitive
records, with clearances lasting three years. Both regimes count in people, so
product-only files with nobody in them fall outside the thresholds entirely.

**Which region suits high-volume ecommerce content?**

China, when the catalog physically exists and is manufactured in Asia. Sample
movement is domestic, a reshoot runs in days rather than weeks, and the platform
specifications that actually govern acceptance live in Chinese-language seller
backends. India suits high-volume English-language work where nothing has to be
photographed against a physical unit.
