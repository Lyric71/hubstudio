## R1. What has to be true (mapped before any lookup, 2026-10-18)

1. There is no federal statute or FTC rule that requires a label on
   AI-generated content in commercial ads.
2. Section 5 bans deceptive acts; AI assets fall under it like any asset.
3. Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023: dates, endorser
   definition, honest-opinion and actual-consumer rules, guidance status.
4. Consumer Reviews and Testimonials rule, 16 CFR Part 465: dates, AI fake
   reviews, civil penalty.
5. FTC enforcement on AI claims, category level, dated; status of the AI
   review-tool order.
6. Open FTC rulemaking relevant to AI impersonation.
7. New York synthetic performer statute.
8. New York post-mortem digital replica amendment.
9. California AI Transparency Act as amended, who it binds, dates, penalty.
10. Other state rules touching ads.
11. Federal developments bearing on state AI laws; TAKE IT DOWN; NO FAKES.
12. Platform labels: Meta, YouTube and Google Ads, TikTok, LinkedIn.
13. hubStudio app facts on History and Validation.

Outcome: 1 to 13 all cleared at primary sources, with the corrections and
cuts listed under "Do not publish". Two items surfaced that R1 did not list
and that the outline now carries: California SB 1050 (a second synthetic
performer statute, in force 1 January 2027) and Hawaii Act 247 (consent for
realistic AI imitations in ads). One surfaced and was kept out of the body as
not law: Pennsylvania SB 806.

**The bar:** law-firm alerts run 800 to 2,200 words, no tables, one law each,
exact citations. Vendor guides run 2,800 to 8,500 words with one to six
tables and weak sourcing. The best multi-layer page runs about 2,800 words
with five tables and leaves out California. To beat it: about 2,400 to 3,000
words, three or more tables, every row tied to a primary text, current to
October 2026 (SB 1000, SB 1050, Hawaii Act 247, Meta's June 2026 ad
detection, Google's July 2026 ad label).

**The gap, in one sentence:** no ranking page puts the three layers side by
side from primary texts and says which party each one binds and which brand
assets trigger it.
