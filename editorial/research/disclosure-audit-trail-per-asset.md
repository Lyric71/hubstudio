Two points the writer must not blur. Article 50 splits the duty: the
machine-readable marking duty sits on the **provider** of the generative
system, and the deepfake disclosure duty sits on the **deployer** who
publishes. A studio producing assets for a client is normally neither the
provider of the model nor, after handover, the deployer who publishes. What the
studio holds is the evidence the deployer needs on the day it discloses. Say
that. Do not say the AI Act requires a studio to keep a file.

Second: EUR-Lex would not render for this session, three URL forms, three empty
responses. The Article 50 wording above comes from the European Commission's
own FAQ and fact page, which are the Commission speaking about its own
Regulation. Cite them by name and date. Do not present any sentence as a
quotation from the Official Journal, and do not cite a law firm's rendering.

**Two nuances the writer must carry, or the section is wrong.**

The California AI Transparency Act does not bind a production studio. Its
duties fall on a covered provider, defined by a million monthly users. It is
cited here because it is the clearest statutory statement in the United States
of what a provenance record contains, four named fields, and because a studio's
record has to line up with what the tool it used embeds. Write it as a field
list from a statute, never as an obligation on the reader.

New York's duty is triggered by actual knowledge. That makes the per-asset
record the thing that settles the question, in both directions. Say what the
statute says and stop there.

One conflict, published as a conflict. A search summary claimed AB 1836 does
not take effect until 1 January 2026. The legislature's own bill record shows
it chaptered 5 September 2024 with no separate operative date, which puts it on
California's default 1 January 2025 commencement. Use the chaptering date and
the chapter number, which are primary, and do not print an effective date for
AB 1836 without checking the code section's own history note.

### Retention: what each instrument actually says

| Instrument | Retention duty stated | Length | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAC Labeling Measures, Article 9 | Yes | Not less than six months | Only where a provider hands over content without an explicit label at the user's request, after the user agreement sets out the user's obligation and liability. Logs of that transaction |
| CAC Labeling Measures, every other article | No retention period stated | n/a | n/a |
| GB 45438-2025 | None found in either published reading | n/a | The standard specifies label form and metadata fields, not archive duration |
| EU AI Act Article 50 | None, in the article or in the Commission's Article 50 FAQ | n/a | n/a |

The six-month figure belongs to Article 9 of the Chinese Measures and to
nothing else on this page. Do not carry it across to the EU or to any US state
statute, and do not present it as a general provenance retention standard.

**hubStudio's 90 day figure is policy.** It is a studio retention policy, not a
legal requirement, and no instrument researched here sets 90 days for anything.
Every sentence carrying it must say "hubStudio policy" or "our policy" in the
same sentence. Where the article puts the policy next to the Chinese six month
duty, it must also say the six months is the longer of the two and comes from
the instrument, so a project touching that clause holds the file longer.

## The record schema, field by field, traceable to a published standard

Each row names where the field comes from. Rows marked "practice" have no
standard behind them and must be presented as production practice, not as a
requirement.

| Field | What it holds | Traceable to |
|---|---|---|
| Asset ID | One stable identifier for this asset, carried in the file and in the record | China Measures Article 5, content ID; GB 45438-2025 ProduceID; California BPC 22757.3(b), "a unique identifier" |
| Producer name or code | Who generated it, as a name or a registered code | China Measures Article 5, service provider name or code; GB 45438-2025 ContentProducer; California BPC 22757.3(b), "the name of the covered provider" |
| Model name and version | The generative system and its version at the moment of creation | California BPC 22757.3(b), "the name and version number of the GenAI system that created or altered the content"; C2PA actions assertion, softwareAgent object with name and version |
| Created or altered timestamp | Date and time of creation, or of the alteration | California BPC 22757.3(b), "the time and date of the content's creation or alteration"; C2PA actions assertion, when |
| Content attribute, or source type | Whether the asset is generated, generated then edited, or captured then modified | China Measures Article 5, content attribute information; IPTC Digital Source Type NewsCodes: trainedAlgorithmicMedia, compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia, algorithmicMedia, digitalCapture; C2PA actions assertion, digitalSourceType |
| Label flag | Whether a label is present, and which kind | GB 45438-2025 Label field |
| Action history | The ordered edit chain, opened or created first, then each action with its agent | C2PA c2pa.actions.v2: action, when, softwareAgent, parameters, description. | Inputs and ingredients | Reference images, source plates, base assets, each with a stated relationship to the output | C2PA ingredient assertion: instance_id, relationship, title, format, label. Actions point at them through parameters.ingredientIds |

| Rights status of inputs | Whether each input was licensed, owned, or a person's likeness with consent on file | CAWG training and data mining assertion on the mining and training permission side; California Labor Code 927 and Civil Code 3344.1 for the consent evidence a digital replica needs. | Distributor name or code, and distribution content ID | Added by the platform, not by the studio, but the studio's record has to survive alongside it | China Measures Article 6; GB 45438-2025 ContentPropagator and PropagateID |
| Human edits, named | Which person changed what, after generation | practice. C2PA carries the actions and identity assertions exist, but no instrument researched here requires a named human editor |
| Approver and approval date | Who signed the asset off for release | practice. No standard or instrument researched here names this field |
| Destination markets | Where the asset is scheduled to run, which decides which regime applies | practice. No standard names it. It is the field that makes every other row answerable |
| Retention date | When the record is due for review or deletion | practice for the 90 day default, instrument-driven at six months where China Measures Article 9 applies |

Notes on the standards, so the writer does not overreach.

The IPTC Digital Source Type scheme is a controlled vocabulary, scheme URI
http://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype/, last modified 23 October 2024,
defined as indicating "from which source a digital image was created". Its
terms are values, not a schema. trainedAlgorithmicMedia is defined as "digital
media created algorithmically using an Artificial Intelligence model trained on
captured content". compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia is defined as
"augmentation, correction or enhancement using a Generative AI model, such as
with inpainting or outpainting operations". That distinction is the one a
retoucher gets wrong, and it is worth a paragraph of its own.

C2PA is a specification, not a law. No instrument researched here requires
C2PA. It is cited as the field vocabulary a record can borrow so the same row
exports cleanly. Version 2.4 is the version read.

GB 45438-2025 field names are triangulated from two published readings, not
from the standard's own text, which sits behind the national register's viewer.
Carry the softer attribution wherever those five field names appear.
