# SERP map: AI disclosure in US advertising (FTC, state laws, platform labels)

Run date: 2026-10-18. Tool: WebSearch (US results). Each query returned nine results, not ten; all nine are recorded in the order returned. Pages were judged by WebFetch where the server allowed it; "snippet only" marks a page that refused the fetch (403/429) or rendered no body, judged from its search title and snippet. Word counts and table counts are estimates from the fetch. Domains only: the article never names a competitor.

Flag key used in the tables:

- **(a)** claims an FTC rule that requires AI labels in ads
- **(b)** prints enforcement-growth percentages without a source
- **(c)** invents a "double disclosure rule" or similar
- **(d)** lays out federal, state and platform layers together, tied to primary texts
- **V** AI-tool or compliance vendor selling AI creation, disclosure, detection or compliance tooling

## Flag summary

- **(a) FTC rule requiring AI labels in ads:** ppl.studio ("finalized rules around AI Transparency in Advertising"); digitalapplied.com frames such a rule as expected from leaked drafts. humanadsai.com and thestacc.com get there through the "double disclosure" claim. Pages that correctly say no such rule exists: adexchanger.com, billo.app, everything-pr.com, aifashionlaw.com, creativo.ai, shadowaipolicy.com.
- **(b) Unsourced enforcement-growth percentages:** humanadsai.com ("40% in 2025"), thestacc.com ("40% in 2025", "340% since 2021"). The Q1 search summary repeated the 40% line, so it is spreading.
- **(c) Invented "double disclosure rule":** humanadsai.com and thestacc.com, both stated as an FTC requirement. Not the same thing: everything-pr.com (stacks a platform label, an ad-level tag and the NY in-ad disclosure as three separate duties) and aifashionlaw.com (reasons two duties for synthetic influencers from Section 5) do not claim an FTC rule.
- **(d) Federal, state and platform layers together, tied to primary texts:** no page fully qualifies. everything-pr.com comes closest (FTC position, NY GBL 396-b, six platforms, linked sources) but leaves California out. thestacc.com covers all three layers without one primary link and with invented claims. shadowaipolicy.com cites the federal and state texts (unlinked) with no platform layer.
- **AI-tool vendors selling creation, disclosure, detection or compliance:** humanadsai.com, thestacc.com, ppl.studio, adsgen.ai, creativo.ai, aikons.ai, clip-short.com, pixo.video, influencerforge.app, billo.app, cohley.com (UGC), shadowaipolicy.com and onetrust.com (compliance tooling). metricool.com and storrito.com are social-scheduling SaaS, not AI vendors.
- **Other accuracy hazards seen:** orrick.com and jonesday.com still give SB 942's original 2026-01-01 date, before AB 853 moved it; the Q1 search summary said California "mandates invisible metadata on all AI-created content", when SB 942/AB 853 binds covered GenAI providers (then large platforms and capture devices), not advertisers; storrito.com says a missing TikTok label draws an immediate strike while other results say the label is neutral; metricool.com cites "$50,000+ per violation" for FTC deception with no basis.

- Q4 (NY law): law-firm alerts own it (7 of 9), plus the governor's release.
- Q5 (platform labels): vendor how-tos and 2024 news; no law at all on most pages.
- Q6 (California act): law firms, legislative trackers and bill text; every page is about AI providers, none tells a brand what the act means for its ads.

## The bar

The best-ranked pages split in two. Law-firm alerts (cooley.com, mcdermottlaw.com, debevoise.com, insideprivacy.com, morganlewis.com) run 800 to 2,200 words, carry zero tables, cite the statute exactly and cover one law each. Vendor guides (thestacc.com, humanadsai.com, billo.app, clip-short.com, everything-pr.com) run 2,800 to 8,500 words, carry one to six tables, and cover more layers with weaker sourcing. Typical length across both: about 1,200 to 4,500 words; the best multi-layer page (everything-pr.com) is about 2,800 words with five tables and linked platform and government sources. Depth to beat: exact citations (GBL 396-b, 16 CFR 255 and 465, SB 942 as amended by AB 853, SB 1050), current dates, and a working table that maps each layer to who it binds.

## The gap

No ranking page puts the FTC position (no AI-label rule; Section 5, the Endorsement Guides and the fake-review rule), the state laws (New York in force, California's synthetic-performer law from 2027 and its provider-only Transparency Act) and the Meta, TikTok, YouTube and Google Ads labels on one page tied to primary texts, saying for each layer whether it binds the brand, the AI provider or the platform.
