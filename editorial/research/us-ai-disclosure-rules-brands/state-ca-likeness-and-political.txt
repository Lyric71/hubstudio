AB 2602 (2024), Stats. 2024, Ch. 259, adds Labor Code section 927. Approved and filed 2024-09-17.
Code history note (verbatim): "Added by Stats. 2024, Ch. 259, Sec. 1. (AB 2602) Effective January 1, 2025."
Operative text (verbatim): "927. (a) A provision in an agreement between an individual and any other person for the performance of personal or professional services is unenforceable only as it relates to a new performance, fixed on or after January 1, 2025, by a digital replica of the individual if the provision meets all of the following conditions: (1) The provision allows for the creation and use of a digital replica of the individual's voice or likeness in place of work the individual would otherwise have performed in person. (2) (A) Except as provided in subparagraph (B), the provision does not include a reasonably specific description of the intended uses of the digital replica. ... (3) The individual was not represented in any of the following manners: (A) By legal counsel ... (B) By a labor union ..."
Trigger for brands: a talent contract that lets you make a digital replica in place of in-person work, without a reasonably specific description of the uses and without counsel or union representation, is unenforceable on that point.

AB 1836 (2024), Stats. 2024, Ch. 258, amends Civil Code section 3344.1. Approved and filed 2024-09-17.
Code history note (verbatim): "Amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 258, Sec. 2. (AB 1836) Effective January 1, 2025."
Operative text (verbatim): "a person who produces, distributes, or makes available the digital replica of a deceased personality's voice or likeness in an expressive audiovisual work or sound recording without prior consent from a person specified in subdivision (c) shall be liable to any injured party in an amount equal to the greater of ten thousand dollars ($10,000) or the actual damages suffered by a person controlling the rights to the deceased personality's likeness."
Existing ad rule in the same section (verbatim): "a person who uses a deceased personality's name, voice, signature, photograph, or likeness, in any manner, on or in products, merchandise, or goods, or for purposes of advertising or selling, or soliciting purchases of, products, merchandise, goods, or services, without prior consent from the person or persons specified in subdivision (c), shall be liable for any damages sustained ... the greater of seven hundred fifty dollars ($750) or the actual damages"

AB 2355 (2024), Stats. 2024, Ch. 260, adds Government Code section 84514 (Political Reform Act). Approved and filed 2024-09-17.
Code history note (verbatim): "Added by Stats. 2024, Ch. 260, Sec. 8. (AB 2355) Effective January 1, 2025."
Operative text (verbatim): "84514. (a) (1) If a committee, as defined in Section 82013, creates, originally publishes, or originally distributes a qualified political advertisement, the qualified political advertisement shall include, in a clear and conspicuous manner, the following disclosure: "Ad generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence.""
Definition (verbatim): ""Qualified political advertisement" means an advertisement that contains any image, audio, or video that is generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence."
Carve-out (verbatim): "Any image, audio, video, or other media is not "generated or substantially altered using artificial intelligence" if the media is immaterially altered by artificial intelligence, including a cosmetic adjustment, color edit, cropped image, or resized image."
Enforcement (verbatim): the Fair Political Practices Commission may "Seek injunctive relief to compel compliance" or pursue administrative or civil remedies; "A violation of subdivision (a) shall not constitute a misdemeanor".
Who it binds: political committees, not commercial advertisers.

AB 502 (2025-26), Stats. 2026, Ch. 333, URGENCY, amends Elections Code 20012 (the AB 2839 section). Approved and filed 2026-09-19.
Bill record (verbatim): "CHAPTERED 2026-09-19 APPROVED 2026-09-19 FILED 2026-09-19" ... "AB 502 CHP 2026 CHP 0 333"
Code history note (verbatim): "Amended by Stats. 2026, Ch. 333, Sec. 1. (AB 502) Effective September 19, 2026."
Digest (verbatim): "The bill would specify that the prohibition applies within the 30 days before an election in California with regard to candidates for any federal, state, or local elected office and elected officials and, with regard to elections officials and voting machines and related equipment, beginning 30 days before an election through 38 days after the election." ... "This bill would also exempt an advertisement or other election communication from these prohibitions if a reasonable person would understand that the content was satire or parody."
Disclosure wording kept (verbatim): ""This ____ has been manipulated."" and "For visual media that is video, the disclosure shall appear for the duration of the video".
NOTE: NCSL's page says the legislature amended the law "in September 2025"; the official record shows chaptering on 19 September 2026. Use the official date.
