1. ORGANIC AND ADS: LinkedIn Help "Content credentials"
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984
Page date: "Last updated: 2 weeks ago" (relative; about 2026-10-04 counting back from 2026-10-18)
Read: 2026-10-18

Verbatim:
"To ensure that LinkedIn stays safe, trusted, and professional, we are adopting the C2PA standard to help members stay more informed about the content they see on the platform. Note: We're gradually making this feature available, and you might not have access to it at this time."

"Members can view the Content Credentials on all image and video content that has C2PA information attached."

"In select countries, when you upload media that does not already have valid authenticity information, you will have the option to self-attest that the media was created using AI. If self-attestation is selected, LinkedIn will generate and securely store authenticity information for the original image or video. The original media itself is never modified."

"Although it's not yet possible to identify and label all AI-generated and modified content, we're continuing to learn and assess feedback to capture more content and credentials over time."

"Image and video content that is cryptographically signed using C2PA Content Credentials will be noted with the C2PA icon"

"Clicking on this label will display the content credential and available metadata, such as: Assertion about the media - This is information on whether AI was used to generate or edit any part of the content. ... App or device used ... Created by ... Issued by ... Issued on"
(The original uses en dashes after each field name; rendered here as " - ".)

"If you post image or video content that contains C2PA credentials, we will indicate its presence with the C2PA icon as pictured below. This will allow users to click the icon to view the available metadata. The icon will be visible to all viewers of your content."

"Please note, for Sponsored Content single image and video ads, the C2PA icon will only appear on single image or video ads when the image or video is uploaded. This applies to content created in Campaign Manager, as well as content sponsored from your Company Page."

CHANGE vs 2026-10-08: the page now describes an optional self-attestation ("in select countries") for media without C2PA data. The page does not list the countries, so it cannot be stated that US members have the option. The page calls the mark "the C2PA icon"; it does not use the words "CR icon".

What the page does NOT say: it sets no duty to disclose AI use; self-attestation is described as an "option". LinkedIn's label is provenance display (C2PA), not a mandatory AI label.

URL: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies
Page date: undated, read 2026-10-18
Read: 2026-10-18

Verbatim:
"Do not share synthetic or manipulated media that depicts a person saying something they did not say or doing something they did not do without clearly disclosing the fake or altered nature of the material."

"Do not share content or endorse someone or something in exchange for personal benefit (including personal or family relationships, monetary payment, free products or services, or other value), unless you have included a clear and conspicuous notice of the personal benefit you receive and have otherwise complied with our Advertising Policies. 3. ADS: LinkedIn Advertising Policies
URL: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/ads-policy
Page date: "Last revised on November 18, 2025"
Read: 2026-10-18

Finding: the policy text contains no provision on AI-generated, synthetic or manipulated content (searched for "AI", "artificial", "synthetic", "generated", "manipulated": no match). Ads are bound to the community policies:
"Professional Community Policies. Ads must comply with the LinkedIn Professional Community Policies."

"Fraud and Deception. Ads must not be fraudulent or deceptive. Your product or service must accurately match the content of your ad. Any claims in your ad must have factual support. ... Do not imply you or your product are affiliated with or endorsed by others without their permission."

Political ads:
"Political. Political ads are prohibited, including ads advocating for or against a particular candidate, party, ballot proposition, law, regulation, or otherwise intended to influence an election outcome; ads fundraising for or by political candidates, parties, political action committees or similar organizations, or ballot propositions; and ads exploiting a sensitive political issue even if the advertiser has no explicit political agenda."

Consequence for the article: on LinkedIn, an AI-made ad needs disclosure only through the community policy rule on synthetic media that depicts a person saying or doing something they did not; there is no ad-specific AI label rule, and political ads are banned outright.
