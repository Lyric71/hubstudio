1. Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence"
Signed: December 11, 2025. Published: 90 FR 58499-58501 (FR Doc. 2025-23092), December 16, 2025.
URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/16/2025-23092/ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence
Raw text read: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/16/2025-23092.txt
Also at: https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/eo-14365.pdf (not separately read)
Section 7, verbatim:
"Sec. 7. Preemption of State Laws Mandating Deceptive Conduct in AI Models. Within 90 days of the date of this order, the Chairman of the Federal Trade Commission shall, in consultation with the Special Advisor for AI and Crypto, issue a policy statement on the application of the Federal Trade Commission Act's prohibition on unfair and deceptive acts or practices under 15 U.S.C. 45 to AI models. That policy statement must explain the circumstances under which State laws that require alterations to the truthful outputs of AI models are preempted by the Federal Trade Commission Act's prohibition on engaging in deceptive acts or practices affecting commerce."
Section 6, verbatim (FCC, not FTC): "the Chairman of the Federal Communications Commission shall ... initiate a proceeding to determine whether to adopt a Federal reporting and disclosure standard for AI models that preempts conflicting State laws."
Section 8(b), verbatim: the legislative recommendation "shall not propose preempting otherwise lawful State AI laws relating to: (i) child safety protections; ..."
Other sections (headings verbatim): "Sec. 3. AI Litigation Task Force." "Sec. 4. Evaluation of State AI Laws." "Sec. 5. Restrictions on State Funding."

2. FTC response: PROPOSED policy statement (not final as of 2026-10-18)
Press release: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy , July 1, 2026, vote 2-0
Verbatim (WebFetch extraction): "In a December executive order, President Trump directed the FTC to issue a policy statement addressing the legal implications of state laws that require alteration of the 'truthful outputs of AI models.'"
FR: "Policy Statement Concerning the Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence Systems", 91 FR 41638-41642 (FR Doc. 2026-13628), July 7, 2026, File No. P264200, docket FTC-2026-0859
Raw text read: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/07/07/2026-13628.txt
Verbatim: "ACTION: Proposed policy statement; request for comments." "DATES: Comments must be received on or before Friday, July 31, 2026."
Verbatim: "Consumers have no basis to believe that AI systems aim to produce outputs that are distorted by undisclosed ideological objectives."
Verbatim: "an AI company might be tempted to alter or steer the output of its systems contrary to consumers' reasonable expectations for various reasons, including attempted compliance with a State law, such as Colorado's recently revised Artificial Intelligence Act. But steering an AI system in this manner may deceive consumers in violation of section 5 of the FTC Act."
Verbatim: "Although the FTC Act does not expressly preempt State law, State law is impliedly preempted to the extent it conflicts with a Federal regulatory scheme."
STATUS: the FTC policy statements index (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/policy-statements) lists it as "Federal Trade Commission's Proposed Policy Statement Concerning The Suppression Of Accuracy In Artificial Intelligence Systems" (July 1, 2026); no final version listed; no later FTC FR document on it (federalregister.gov API search, 2026-10-18). Note it was issued about 6.5 months after the order, past the 90-day window in Sec. 7.
SCOPE NOTE: the proposed statement concerns AI system outputs (models), not advertisers' labeling of AI-generated ads. It says nothing that pre-empts state ad-disclosure laws as such; do not present it as doing so.

3. TAKE IT DOWN Act (scope only)
Public Law 119-12, S. 146, approved May 19, 2025 (congress.gov API: latest action 2025-05-19 "Became Public Law No: 119-12.")
Text read: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm
Verbatim title: "An Act To require covered platforms to remove nonconsensual intimate visual depictions, and for other purposes."
Verbatim Sec. 3(a)(1)(A): "Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment of this Act, a covered platform shall establish a process ..."
Verbatim Sec. 3(a)(3): "a covered platform shall, as soon as possible, but not later than 48 hours after receiving such request-- (A) remove the intimate visual depiction; ..."
Verbatim Sec. 3(b)(1): "A failure to reasonably comply with the notice and takedown obligations under subsection (a) shall be treated as a violation of a rule defining an unfair or a deceptive act or practice under section 18(a)(1)(B) of the Federal Trade Commission Act"
FTC blog, May 19, 2026: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida
Verbatim (WebFetch extraction): "Today, the Federal Trade Commission begins enforcing Section 3 of the Act"; covers "nonconsensual intimate imagery, including intimate real photos and videos as well as 'digital forgeries'"; "potential civil penalties of $53,088 per violation."
SCOPE: nonconsensual intimate imagery and platforms' takedown duty. Not an advertising or AI-labeling law.
