## Constraint banner, read before drafting

1. **Search access was degraded, and this file says so where it matters.** The
   session's web search tool budget was exhausted before this brief started
   (five refusals). Every query below was run by fetching a search engine page
   directly. Baidu served a captcha after three requests and on every retry,
   Brave returned HTTP 429 after two, Shenma returned blank pages, 360 Search
   and Sogou redirected to anti-bot pages, DuckDuckGo served a captcha, and
   Toutiao search returned truncated pages on 13 of 44 requests. Taobao-owned
   hosts (daxue.taobao.com, jianghu.taobao.com) cannot be fetched by the
   research tool at all. That is a tool limit, not a platform finding, and
   nothing below treats it as one.
2. **Every date carries its platform and its year.** Tmall's New Year goods
   festival opened on 17 January in 2024, 6 January in 2025 and 4 January in
   2026 (spot sale). A date without its year is wrong the following year.
3. **No company except the platforms reaches the page by name.** Tmall, Taobao,
   JD, Douyin and Alibaba may be named. Chinese outlets appear here for the
   evidence trail only; on the page they are "Chinese technology press",
   "Chinese trade reporting of the platform's rules" and so on, with the date.
   The agency and shopping-agent pages in the SERP map are never named,
   described or alluded to.
4. **The plan is not a platform requirement.** No platform rule found asks for
   a decoration refresh, a creative asset, a livestream or a short video (see
   the absence row). The month-by-month plan and the reuse table are production
   practice and derived arithmetic, labeled as such on the page.
5. **No GMV, growth, rebate or discount-percentage figure** reaches the page.
   None is needed and none checked carries a method.
6. **No hubStudio rate.** None is needed.

## The bar

The two English pages that address Tmall for a brand run about 3,500 to 4,000
words, carry a festival list with no years or no list at all, and carry no
table of dated events, no refresh guidance and no plan. The Chinese ranking
pages are short consumer guides that print one event's dates with no platform
source, or cross-platform marketing calendars that are not Tmall-specific.

The brief's 1,800 words clear the field if the page carries: an annual table in
which every date has its platform, its year and its source type; a statement
of what the reported rules ask of a store (and do not ask); a month-by-month
plan labeled production practice; a reuse-versus-rebuild table labeled derived;
and a refresh checklist. Two tables clear it. The dated annual table wins it,
because no ranking page in either language prints a year next to a Tmall date.

**The gap, in one sentence.** No page ranking for these queries in English or
Chinese prints Tmall's promotion dates with their year and a platform source,
and none says what the reported merchant rules actually ask of a store, so a
flagship's content year gets planned from undated festival lists and consumer
guides that reprint last year's dates.

**H1 consequence.** The working H1, "Running a Tmall flagship's content, month
by month", holds. The dated annual table and the finding that reported rules
ask for scores, listing deadlines and pricing but no creative carry the piece.

## R6. Where sources conflict, and why

1. **Tmall New Year goods festival 2025: 6 January against 17 January.** An
   ecommerce trade report of 7 January 2025, consistent with Tmall's own Weibo
   post of 2 January 2025 (seen only as a search snippet), gives an 8 p.m.
   opening on 6 January. Consumer guides, a Q and A page and a seller-service
   blog, dated December 2024 to March 2026, give 17 to 31 January. The 2024
   festival ran 17 to 31 January, so the guides most likely carried 2024's
   window forward, the same failure brief 30 found for 618. Publish 6 January,
   say guides printed 17 to 31 January. The 2025 end date is not reported;
   the one guide giving 20 January is cut, although it is consistent with the
   "19 days" duration the 2026 report attributes to 2025.
2. **Tmall New Year goods festival 2026: 1 and 4 January to 11 February,
   against 19 to 31 January.** Technology press on 4 and 6 January 2026
   reported the platform's timetable (presale from 1 January, spot sale 4
   January to 11 February). Consumer guides from mid-January 2026 printed 19 to
   31 January, and one local news account describes 19 January as the moment
   "the Taobao and Tmall festival opened" with platform subsidies and
   cross-store discounts. The likeliest reading is a Taobao-wide discount burst
   inside the platform's longer window, but no report confirms that. Publish
   the reported timetable and the conflict; do not publish 19 January as an
   opening.
3. **Taobao Double 12 2025 close: 12 December against 14 December.** The rules
   as reported (26 November 2025) give 11:59:59 p.m. on 12 December, matched by
   a 5 December guide. A sponsored brand-campaign article (3 December) and a
   guide claiming official confirmation (11 December) give 14 December; the 5
   December guide also lists 13 to 15 December as a return period, which may
   account for the later date. The rules win: 12 December.
4. **Tmall March promotion 2025: hours and structure.** Three guides agree on
   27 February but disagree on 00:00, 2 p.m. warm-up or 8 p.m., and on a 5:59
   p.m. or 7:59 p.m. deposit close. A fourth gives a different 1 to 11 March
   structure. No rule reprint was reached. Cut from the table.
5. **The September 99 sale.** Guides give 1 September warm-up with 9 to 30
   September, or a 5 to 6 September warm-up; an evening-paper account says the
   99 red-envelope campaign became a "Super 88" campaign from 8 to 11 September
   2025. None cites the platform. Cut. The page may say only that no dated
   platform reporting of the 2024 or 2025 September sale was found.
6. **The 2026 New Year goods festival duration.** Reported as 41 days; 1
   January to 11 February inclusive is 42 calendar days, and the spot window
   alone is 39. Publish the dates, not the count.
