**The bar:** length not measured page by page. The three stage-timeline pages
read in full each carry one table of day ranges per stage and no method. Search
summaries show per-content-type totals elsewhere. To clear it: a stage table
that separates work time from waiting time and marks every cell sourced,
first-party or production practice; a review-round arithmetic table; a
checklist; and the absence stated with its count. The brief's 1,900 words is
enough.

**The gap, in one sentence:** Every result on four buyer phrasings is published
by a company selling production, software, tools or training, and the pages
that break turnaround into stages print day ranges with no sample or method;
none separates the time work takes from the time it waits for a decision, and
none shows how review rounds multiply the calendar.

**H1 consequence:** the H1 cannot be a number, because no method-stated number
exists. It has to carry the primary query and promise the stage split. The
working H1 does not contain `content production turnaround time`, and its "days
not weeks" phrase reads as the turnaround promise the brief forbids. Proposed:
"Content production turnaround time, stage by stage". Flagged, see Notes.

### Turnaround checklist material

Every line is production practice (P) unless marked. None carries a figure.

- Name one approver per deliverable and one person who consolidates comments.
- Write the number of rounds into the scope, and define a round as one
  consolidated set of comments, not a thread.
- Book review slots in both calendars before kickoff, and put the feedback
  turnaround in working days in the scope.
- Check working-hour overlap between the reviewer and the studio, then pick
  asynchronous or call-based review (S for the overlap hours).
- Confirm the sample ship date and whether samples cross a border.
- Check public holidays in the production country for the whole window (S,
  2026 notice).
- Decide at the brief whether a trained model is needed; if it is, its window
  sits before generation, not beside it (F for the window).
- Ask where each quoted window starts: brief, signature, sample receipt or
  shoot (S, observation).
- Ask whether a quoted schedule includes your review time (S, observation).
- Ask the price and the daily volume cap of the faster retouch tier (S, seller
  terms).
- Lock formats and markets before the first review.
- Put a date on final approval, not only on final delivery.

## Review-round arithmetic (derived)

**The model.** Review time added to one deliverable = rounds x (working days
waiting for consolidated feedback + working days of revision). Work and wait are
separate terms. Faster production shrinks only the second.

**Illustrative inputs, not measurements.** No method-stated source gives any of
these values. They are round numbers chosen so the reader can substitute their
own, and the page must say so.

| Scenario | Rounds | Wait for feedback, per round | Revision, per round | Review time added |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Next-day feedback | 3 | 1 day | 1 day | 6 working days |
| Feedback in three days | 3 | 3 days | 1 day | 12 working days |
| Weekly review meeting | 3 | 5 days | 1 day | 18 working days |
| Weekly meeting, one extra round | 4 | 5 days | 1 day | 24 working days |
| Weekly meeting, revision twice as fast | 3 | 5 days | 0.5 day | 16.5 working days |

Checks: 3 x (1 + 1) = 6; 3 x (3 + 1) = 12; 3 x (5 + 1) = 18; 4 x (5 + 1) = 24;
3 x (5 + 0.5) = 16.5. Halving revision time in the weekly case saves 1.5 working
days. Moving from a weekly meeting to next-day feedback saves 12. One extra round
at the weekly pace costs 6.

**Arithmetic on hubStudio's published project labels** (pricing page lines 51,
56 and 156). First review inside two weeks of kickoff and full delivery inside
four weeks. At the outer edge of both, and at five working days a week, 10
working days separate first review from delivery. Three rounds on finalized work
inside that window leaves 10 / 3 = about 3.3 working days per round for feedback
and revision together. Derived, not a measured cycle and not a commitment. It
also shows why the three to six week training window cannot sit inside the four
week label at the top of its range (R6).

**Time zones, reasoning only.** With no working-hour overlap between New York and
Shanghai (sourced arithmetic), a round that needs a live conversation waits for a
call booked outside one side's day, while comments sent at the close of a New
York day arrive at the start of the Shanghai day. The page may state that
mechanism. It may not attach a number of days to it.
