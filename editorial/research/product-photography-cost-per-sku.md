## R1. What has to be true before any number is looked up

Written before the lookups. Anything that could not be established was cut from
the outline rather than softened in the copy.

1. A SKU is not a unit of photography. Before any per-SKU figure can be honest,
   the piece has to state how many images a SKU is being priced at, and say that
   the number is the reader's input, not a market constant.
2. Published per-image capture prices exist on live rate cards and can be read
   directly, one card at a time.
3. Published per-image retouch prices exist on live rate cards and sit one to
   two orders of magnitude below capture. If that gap is real, retouch is not
   production and the piece can say so with arithmetic instead of assertion.
4. A real volume mechanism exists on published cards and is computable. Either a
   stepped per-image ladder, a membership that buys a percentage off, or a
   credit block whose unit price falls with block size. Without one of these the
   volume angle collapses into the same day-rate framing the SERP already has.
5. That mechanism has a breakeven volume derivable from published inputs by
   division, with the arithmetic shown on the page.
6. Fixed cost lines exist on published cards and can be named: a per-booking
   studio fee, a non-member order minimum, a monthly platform fee, an annual
   membership fee.
7. Chinese studio rate cards publish their own executing rates per image and, if
   R4 pays off, a batch discount ladder the English-language SERP does not have.
8. The 70/30 generated-to-shot mix is a hubStudio delivery figure. It runs only
   with its method stated: what was counted, over what sample, across what
   period.
9. No hubStudio rate, monthly figure or per-item price appears anywhere.

Cut before writing, for want of a source: any images-per-SKU market average, any
reshoot rate, any sample-logistics cost figure, any absolute annual catalog
total, and any claim about where the generated-to-shot ratio moves that is not
first party.

## The unit. What one SKU is, before any number

This is the whole gap, so it is the first section of the article.

A SKU is a unit of inventory. It is not a unit of photography. Every card read
for this file prices an image, an hour, a booking or a credit. Not one of them
prices a SKU. Every ranking page prices a SKU anyway, and none of them says how
many images it put inside one.

| What the card actually prices | Cards that use it | What has to be supplied before a per-SKU number exists |
|---|---|---|
| One image, by complexity | Both US studio cards read for this file | Images per SKU, and the complexity mix across the catalog |
| One hour of a human input | One US studio card, for styling and models | Hours per SKU, which falls as setups are shared across a run |
| One booking | One US studio card, a fixed studio fee | SKUs per booking, which is the entire volume effect |
| One credit | One US studio card, blocks of 25, 50 and 100 | Credits per SKU, which the card does not define |
| One edited image, by operation | Both retouch cards | Operations per image, and images per SKU |
| One style, with a minimum image count attached | The Chinese apparel card, five-style minimum, 6 to 10 images a style | Whether a style equals a SKU, which for apparel it usually does not |

**Definition the article should adopt and state plainly.** One SKU is priced as
N finished images at a stated complexity, plus its share of the fixed lines, plus
whatever human hours the shot type requires. The article states N as the reader's
input, prints the curve at a stated N, and says openly that no source publishes a
market average for N. That single sentence beats every page on the SERP, because
every one of them hides N inside a range.

## Worked arithmetic, shown step by step

Every number below is derived by division or multiplication from a published
input already in the claims table. No new figure is introduced here. The
article shows the arithmetic on the page, because showing it is the differentiator
against a SERP that shows none.

### A. The membership breakeven on a published percentage ladder

Inputs, both from one published studio card read 2026-09-10: a standard product
image at $50, and three annual memberships at $480, $2,340 and $7,500 a year
buying 10, 20 and 30 percent off product work.

Step 1, convert each percentage into money on one image.
10 percent of $50 is $5.00. 20 percent is $10.00. 30 percent is $15.00.

Step 2, divide the annual fee by the saving per image.
$480 / $5.00 = 96 images a year.
$2,340 / $10.00 = 234 images a year.
$7,500 / $15.00 = 500 images a year.

Step 3, restate in SKUs at a stated six images a SKU.
96 / 6 = 16 SKUs. 234 / 6 = 39 SKUs. 500 / 6 = about 84 SKUs.

Below 16 SKUs a year, on this card, the discount costs more than it saves. Above
about 84 SKUs the deepest published tier is the cheapest. That is a real, dated,
computable breakpoint, and no page on the SERP prints one.

### B. The published curve, capture only, at the brief's five volumes

Same card, standard image at $50, six images a SKU, cheapest published tier
selected at each volume, annual fee included in the total.

| SKUs | Images | Cheapest published route | Total capture | Effective per image | Capture per SKU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 300 | 10 percent tier at $480 | $13,980 | $46.60 | $279.60 |
| 200 | 1,200 | 30 percent tier at $7,500 | $49,500 | $41.25 | $247.50 |
| 500 | 3,000 | 30 percent tier at $7,500 | $112,500 | $37.50 | $225.00 |
| 2,000 | 12,000 | 30 percent tier at $7,500 | $427,500 | $35.63 | $213.75 |
| 5,000 | 30,000 | 30 percent tier at $7,500 | $1,057,500 | $35.25 | $211.50 |

Worked for the 50-SKU row: 300 images at $45 each is $13,500, plus the $480
membership, is $13,980, divided by 300 images is $46.60, times six images a SKU
is $279.60. Every other row is the same three operations.

**The finding, and it is the article's spine.** The published curve falls hard
from 50 to 500 SKUs and then stops. From 500 SKUs to 5,000 SKUs, a tenfold
increase in volume, the effective per-image price moves from $37.50 to $35.25,
about 6 percent. The published discount ladder tops out at 30 percent off list
and the annual fee is fully amortized by roughly 500 images. Past that point,
buying more photography does not make photography cheaper.

### C. The fixed booking fee, and why a small run is not a small price

Inputs from the second published studio card read 2026-09-10: $39 a photo and a
$149 studio fee per booking.

Six images from one booking: (149 + 6 x 39) / 6 = 383 / 6 = $63.83 an image.
Sixty images from one booking: (149 + 60 x 39) / 60 = 2,489 / 60 = $41.48.
Six hundred images from one booking: (149 + 600 x 39) / 600 = 23,549 / 600 = $39.25.

The fixed fee is 62 percent of the unit price at one SKU per booking and under
1 percent at a hundred SKUs per booking. The list price never changed.

### D. Credit blocks fall too, and by how much

Same card as A: $1,500 for 25 credits, $2,500 for 50, $4,500 for 100.
$1,500 / 25 = $60.00 a credit. $2,500 / 50 = $50.00. $4,500 / 100 = $45.00.
A fourfold increase in block size buys 25 percent off the unit. The same ceiling
as the membership ladder, reached the same way.

### E. Retouch is not production, in one division

Published retouch floor $0.25 to $1.20 an image by operation, against published
capture of $39 to $50 an image, both collected September 2026.

$0.95 / $39 = 2.4 percent. $0.95 / $50 = 1.9 percent.
At the bottom of the band, $0.25 / $50 = 0.5 percent.
At the top, $1.20 / $39 = 3.1 percent.

Retouch runs at roughly 1 to 3 percent of the capture line. A quote that leads
with a per-image editing price is pricing between one and three percent of the
job. That sentence is the piece's sharpest single claim and it is pure
arithmetic on two published bands.

### F. The retouch platform fee has its own volume floor

Input: one published retouch card at $0.95 an image plus a $95 monthly platform
fee, which also refuses anyone under 1,000 images a year.

$95 / 100 images a month = $0.95 an image, doubling the unit price to $1.90.
$95 / 1,000 images a month = $0.095, a 10 percent uplift to about $1.05.
$95 / 4,000 images a month = about $0.02, a 2.5 percent uplift.

The route is not merely more expensive at low volume, the card declines the
order. That is a published access condition, not an estimate.

### G. The same ceiling appears in the Chinese market

Input: a Chinese-language route comparison read 2026-09-10, baidi tu at 30 to 80
RMB an image, above 100 images 10 to 20 percent off, above 500 images 20 to 30
percent off.

Under 100 images: 30 to 80 RMB.
Above 100 images: 24 to 72 RMB.
Above 500 images: 21 to 64 RMB.

Two markets, two independently published volume mechanisms, and both stop at
about 30 percent off list. That agreement is worth publishing as a structural
observation about how photography is sold, and it is worth labeling as two
single-source ladders that happen to agree rather than as a measured market
average.

## Fixed against variable, by cost line

Every line below is named on a card read for this file. No line carries an
invented figure.

| Cost line | Fixed or variable | Where it appears on a published card | What changes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capture, per image | Variable | $39 to $50 standard on two US cards, 30 to 80 RMB baidi tu on the Chinese comparison | Complexity uplifts to $75, $100, $150, $190 on one card |
| Retouch, per image or per operation | Variable | $0.25 to $1.20 by operation on two cards, or included in the image price on one studio card | Which operation, and turnaround. Price falls as turnaround lengthens |
| Styling and models, per hour | Variable, but lumpy | $79 to $159 an hour by model type, $149 an hour styling, $129 ghost mannequin styling | Shared across a run, so it behaves like a fixed cost inside one booking |
| Studio fee, per booking | Fixed per booking | $149 a booking on one card | SKUs per booking. This is the volume effect in its purest form |
| Order minimum | Fixed floor | $300 for non-members on one card, five outfits or twenty images on model work, five styles on the Chinese apparel card | Membership, which removes or lowers it |
| Membership or platform fee | Fixed per period | $480, $2,340 or $7,500 a year on one card, $13 or $49 a month on another, $95 a month on a retouch card | Annual image volume against the fee |
| Rush turnaround | Variable uplift | 6 and 12 hour options priced above the 24-hour default on one retouch card, jiaji at 30 percent on the Chinese comparison | Deadline discipline, nothing else |
| Sample logistics, reshoots, approval rounds | Not priced on any card read | Absent from every card | Nothing. No card read for this file prices them at all, which is itself the finding |

The last row is the point of the section. Eight cards were read across two
markets and not one of them prices getting the product to the studio, sending it
back, or reshooting a SKU that failed approval. Those costs are real and they sit
outside every published price on the SERP.
