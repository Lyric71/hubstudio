## Cleared for use

> WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.2.2, Captions (Prerecorded), Level A:
> "Captions are provided for all prerecorded audio content in synchronized
> media, except when the media is a media alternative for text and is clearly
> labeled as such." Source: W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2,
> W3C Recommendation, December 2024. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

> W3C's explanation of the criterion says captions "not only include
> dialogue, but identify who is speaking and include non-speech information
> conveyed through sound, including meaningful sound effects," and lists both
> open captions and closed captions as ways to meet it. Source: W3C,
> Understanding SC 1.2.2, updated August 2026.
> https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html

> YouTube says its automatic captions "might misrepresent the spoken content
> due to mispronunciations, accents, dialects, or background noise," and
> tells creators to "always review automatic captions." Source: YouTube Help,
> Use automatic captioning, read October 2026.
> https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554

> YouTube accepts SubRip (.srt) caption files in their basic form only: "No
> style info (markup) is recognized. The file must be in plain UTF-8."
> Source: YouTube Help, Supported subtitle and closed caption files, read
> October 2026. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734698

> LinkedIn adds closed captions to a video only from the desktop, and only
> from "an associated SRT (SubRip Subtitle) file attached to the video before
> it can be posted." Source: LinkedIn Help, Add closed captions to videos on
> LinkedIn, page last updated about five years before October 2026.
> https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552177

TikTok's launch post (September 2021) for auto captions: creators tap the
auto captions icon, can edit the text once generated, and viewers can set
captions to off. Usable as a dated sentence, not a blockquote figure.
