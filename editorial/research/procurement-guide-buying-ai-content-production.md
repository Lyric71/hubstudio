**The gap, in one sentence:** Across five buyer phrasings, 37 of 43 results are
published by sellers of software, tools or services and none by a government,
trade body or standards body, so a buyer asking how to procure AI content
production is handed tools for writing content or answering RFPs, and no page
supplies the paperwork: RFP sections, statement of work units, a weighting
method tied to public procurement rules, and AI-specific contract clauses.

**H1 consequence.** Two queries return the supply side (tools to answer RFPs,
tools to write content) and one reads "production" as software deployment or
manufacturing. The working H1 holds, but its first 100 words must pin
"content production" to commissioned creative assets (images, video,
adaptations) bought from a production partner, not AI writing software, or
the page inherits the tool intent.

## R6. Where sources conflict

1. **Survey spend base, September 2025.** The trade body's page says 97 billion
   dollars; trade press says "close to 100 billion". Same survey, rounding.
   Publish the trade body's 97 billion.
2. **EU model clauses date.** The Commission's community page dates the update
   5 March 2025; the commentary's document control table reads 12 February 2025
   and calls itself a dynamic working document. Document date against
   publication date. Publish March 2025 as the publication.
3. **UK creative services framework date.** Both trade bodies' own pages say
   27 November 2025; legal trade press and the drafting law firm say
   3 December 2025. Publish November 2025.
4. **How to express price against quality.** Not a numeric conflict, two
   instruments with two methods. The US federal rule states the relationship in
   three verbal tiers (significantly more important, approximately equal,
   significantly less important). UK guidance allows percentages, ranking or
   another description, with no hierarchy. Both require disclosure before bids.
   Publish both methods side by side; publish no percentage split as a norm.

### Drafted unit definitions, production practice (not sourced definitions)

| Unit | Drafted definition | Why it matters in the SOW |
|---|---|---|
| Master | The originated asset approved against the brief, from which variants derive | Prices origination separately from multiplication |
| Asset | One finished, delivered file at one specification (format, dimensions, duration, language) | The countable deliverable; results, not hours |
| Variant | A change to an approved master that keeps its concept and message: resize, crop, cut-down, copy swap | Mirrors the union line on versions; cheap per unit |
| Adaptation | A variant for another market that changes language, legal lines, packshot or cast, without changing concept or message | If concept, message or a new principal changes, it is a new master and priced as one |
| Revision round | One consolidated set of written feedback from the buyer's named approver on a delivered draft, answered by one new delivery | Stops scattered comments counting as unlimited rounds |
| Change request | Any change to the approved brief, specification or scope after sign-off | Kept outside revision rounds and priced separately |
| Usable second | A second of generated or captured footage accepted at final review into a delivered cut | Published generation prices bill attempts, so the SOW should buy accepted seconds |
| Usable shot | A shot accepted into a delivered cut | Same logic for stills and edits |
| Acceptance | Written sign-off against the specification, within a stated review window | Triggers payment and rights transfer |

The number of included revision rounds is a buyer's choice. Do not print a
typical number: none is published with a method, and the studio's own policy
must not appear.
