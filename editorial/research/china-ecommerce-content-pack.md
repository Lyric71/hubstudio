## R4. Chinese-language web first

Searched in Chinese before English, per R4. Search terms are given here in
romanized form, with the English rendering in the romanization table at the
foot of this file: `tianmao shangpin zhutu baidi tu chicun guifan`,
`jingdong shangpin tupian guifan zhutu chicun xiangqingye kuandu guize`,
`rule.jd.com shangpin xinxi fabu guifan tupian guize`.

What the Chinese web gave that the English web did not: the deliverable
vocabulary itself. Chinese design references name the asset types a listing is
built from as separate, budgeted line items (main image set, white-background
image, long detail page, mobile detail page, size and specification chart,
store decoration banners, short video). English pages describe the same job as
one undifferentiated block called "product content" or "listing images". That
vocabulary is the article, and it is verifiable as a taxonomy even where the
pixel numbers attached to it are not.

What the Chinese web did not give: a primary source. Every Chinese page that
carried a number was a design agency blog, a tool vendor content page or a
seller-education site restating platform rules at second hand. Not one linked
to a reachable platform rule page. This is the same wall that blocked brief 01.

**The bar:** the ranking pages run roughly 700 to 1,500 words, carry zero or
one table, and answer either a Western marketplace or a logistics question.
Nothing on any of the five queries carries a per-SKU manifest, a naming
convention or a handover clause list. The bar to beat is 1,600 words with a
manifest table, a file-structure block and a copyable quote-comparison

**The gap, in one sentence:** Not one page on any buyer phrasing publishes an

**H1 consequence:** the ranking pages all miss the manifest, so the manifest is
the H1, not the platforms.

## R1. What has to be true before looking anything up

Listed before searching. Each row is resolved in the claims table below.

1. A China ecommerce SKU needs more than one class of image asset, and those
   classes have names a buyer can be quoted against.
2. Tmall and JD ask for overlapping but not identical asset sets, so a
   two-platform launch is not one set used twice.
3. There is a white-background image requirement on both platforms, distinct
   from the styled main images.
4. Detail pages are a separate deliverable from main images, and the mobile
   detail page is separate again.
5. Exact pixel dimensions, file size caps and fill ratios exist as platform
   rules, and are the thing quotes silently differ on.
6. Those exact numbers are published somewhere reachable without a seller
   login.
7. A complete handover has contents beyond the flattened exports: layered
   masters, linked assets, fonts, metadata, provenance and a retention term.
8. Retention of production files is a policy set by the producer, not a legal
   requirement, and the term differs between suppliers.
9. hubStudio publishes a retention term of its own that can be stated as
   policy.
10. Naming conventions are the mechanism by which a manifest becomes
    auditable, and are cheap to specify and expensive to retrofit.

Items 5 and 6 are where this brief either holds or breaks. It holds
structurally and breaks numerically. The article must be written to that shape:
the manifest is the deliverable, the pixel table is not.

## Cleared for use

Ready to paste. Each blockquote is followed by its source line.

**1. The rule centres are public URLs with gated content.** This is the
strongest thing this piece has, because it is a primary observation nobody
else has published and it explains why three quotes differ.

> The two platform rule centres a buyer would check are published at public
> web addresses and serve no rule text without a seller session. On 10
> September 2026 the Tmall rule centre redirected twice and returned no rule
> body, and two different JD merchant rule pages returned the rule-centre
> heading and nothing else.
> Source: direct unauthenticated fetches of rule.tmall.com and rule.jd.com,
> 10 September 2026, four requests across two platforms, redirect chain and
> response body recorded.

**2. The published specs disagree, so publish the disagreement.**

> Published Chinese specification summaries do not agree on the Tmall main
> image limits. The file size cap appears as 500KB on some pages and 3MB on
> others, and the product fill ratio appears as 60 percent on some and 70 to
> 80 percent on others. None of them distinguishes cross-border Tmall Global
> from domestic Tmall, and none accounts for category variation.
> Source: published Chinese design and tool references collected September
> 2026, values read side by side and reported as a range rather than averaged,
> no publisher named.

**3. The taxonomy holds even where the numbers do not.**

> Every Chinese production reference checked builds a listing from the same
> named parts: a main image set with a pure-white-background image inside it,
> a long scrolling detail page, a separate mobile detail page, a size and
> specification chart, store decoration banners, and short video. The parts
> are stable. The dimensions attached to them are not.
> Source: eight independent Chinese design, tool and seller-education
> references collected September 2026, compared on their deliverable lists
> only, with every number they carried set aside.

**4. Retention is policy, not law.** Stated as policy, per the brief.

> Retention of production files is a policy each supplier sets, not a legal

> A search across five buyer phrasings in English and Chinese returned no page

## Do not publish

### The hard block for this brief

| Item | Reason it is blocked |
|---|---|
| **Any exact Tmall or JD pixel dimension, file size cap, dpi value, ratio or fill percentage, stated as a platform requirement** | R3. Not one of these numbers was read at a primary source. Both rule centres serve public URLs and gate the content, so every circulating number traces to a vendor content page with no rule link and no method. Brief 01 is blocked on exactly this. Publishing one of these as fact is the single most damaging thing this system can do. The article may say that the numbers exist, that they differ between platforms, that they differ between cross-border and domestic, and that they are the line quotes silently vary on. It may not print one. |
| **Any per-SKU asset count presented as a platform requirement** | No platform publishes a required count reachable without a seller login. Counts in circulation are production convention, not rules. If the article gives a manifest with counts, the counts must be presented as a scoping template the reader can hold three quotes against, never as what a platform demands. |
| **The numbers already on hubStudio's own ecommerce design service page** | The ledger flags them as needing re-verification against the backend before they are repeated. Repeating them inside a piece about how nobody can verify these numbers would be self-refuting. Reference the service page by name, do not lift its figures. |
| **The 70/30 generated-to-shot mix** | The ledger records that the sample of engagements and the period are still missing. R5 says a figure with no stated method does not get published. The brief's slot requirement is satisfied instead by the 90-day retention policy, which is published, first-party and stated as policy. |
