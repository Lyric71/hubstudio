## Cleared for use

> Amazon's product image guide requires a pure white background on the main
> image and lets only a limited number of product types use a lifestyle image
> there; scenes of the product in use go in the additional image slots, coded
> PT01 to PT99.
> Source: Amazon Seller Central, Product image guide, US store, page undated,
> read October 8, 2026.

> Amazon asks sellers to add the keyword contains-synthetic-performer to the
> dc:subject (XMP) field when an image shows photorealistic people entirely
> generated by AI, and not when the image shows no people.
> Source: Amazon Seller Central, Product image guide, US store, page undated,
> read October 8, 2026.

> Google Merchant Center takes up to five lifestyle images per product, at
> least 600 by 600 pixels, with no promotional text, watermark, logo overlay or
> border, and requires images made with generative AI to keep metadata saying
> so, such as the IPTC DigitalSourceType tag.
> Source: Google Merchant Center Help, lifestyle image link attribute, page
> undated, read October 8, 2026.

> OpenAI lists text rendering among its image model's limitations and says the
> model "may occasionally struggle to maintain visual consistency for recurring
> characters or brand elements across multiple generations."
> Source: OpenAI image generation guide, the maker's own documentation, page
> undated, read October 8, 2026.

> OpenAI's guide says the model "uses the mask as guidance, but may not follow
> its exact shape with complete precision."
> Source: OpenAI image generation guide, page undated, read October 8, 2026.

> Google's image generation documentation tells users that to preserve
> critical details such as a logo during an edit, they should "describe them in
> great detail along with your edit request."
> Source: Gemini API image generation documentation, the maker's own page,
> last updated October 6, 2026.
