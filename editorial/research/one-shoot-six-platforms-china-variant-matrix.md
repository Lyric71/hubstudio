**Rules that bind every cell.**

1. Pools are never mixed. "12 of 18 (T1)" stays 12 of 18 and names T1. Counts
   from different files are never added together, even where two files agree
   on a value. "RedNote and Tmall both publish 1:1" is allowed; "32 sources
   publish 1:1" is not.
2. A primary row stays scoped exactly as its file scopes it: the WeChat Channels
   help-centre article, the Mini Program framework reference, the Weibo
   help-centre article and date, the Douyin upload API route. A primary row is
   never stretched to a surface its page does not cover.
3. A modal value is never described as verified, official, required or read
   from a platform.
4. Every cross-platform statement in "Derived intersections" is arithmetic on
   the cited values, not a platform rule. Each names its inputs. Where a
   derivation needs an assumption no source states (for example, that a crop is
   centered), the assumption is printed.
5. The brief's own rule applies on top: "Nothing enters this table that is not
   already verified on a spec page." Under deviation 7 that reads as: nothing
   enters the matrix that is not already a counted, labeled row in one of the
   nine files. Anything the brief asks for that the cluster does not carry is
   cut, not researched fresh (see "Do not publish").

**Why no new web research went into the matrix.** Two gaps surfaced: Douyin
Shop product listing images, and per-platform copy lengths. Both would need new
spec research, and the brief bars any cell that is not already on a spec page.
They are recorded as absences, not filled.

The page carrying this matrix opens with the disclaimer block from `../SPEC.md`.
Because the matrix spans marketplace and social platforms, the writer uses the
marketplace first paragraph for Tmall and JD and follows the readable-page rule
for WeChat, Weibo and Douyin, naming each readable page and its scope in one
paragraph after the block. NN is not a single number here: the block must say
the figures come from eight separate collections of 12 to 24 sources each,

## The bar

No ranking page in either language publishes a slot matrix. The English pages
are platform listicles of roughly 1,500 to 3,000 words with no production
table, or pages about something else. The Chinese pages are single tables of
pixel presets, 800 to 2,500 words, one table each, no source count, and they
treat a white-background image, a detail page and a TopView cut as if each were
a crop of the same file.

The bar to beat inside the brief's 1,600 words: a platform-down matrix split
across two five-column tables with a source key in every cell, a cell-type table
(re-crop, re-export, re-edit, native capture), a shoot-day checklist, the
derived composition boxes with their inputs, and a derived slot count labeled
as arithmetic. Two tables clears the field. The cell-type table wins it, because
no page anywhere separates a crop from a capture.

**The gap, in one sentence:** see the header table.

**H1 consequence.** The ranking pages answer "which sizes". The page answers
"which frames", and the count is what falls out of that.
