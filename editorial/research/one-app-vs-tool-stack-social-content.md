## R1. What has to be true (mapped before looking anything up)

1. That a social post passes through distinct jobs (make the picture, edit
   it, store it, get it approved, schedule and publish it, adapt it for a
   second network), and that in a stack of single-purpose tools each job
   change is a tool change. This is a description of a workflow, not a
   figure: it is argued from the jobs, never from a measured count.
2. Where the hand-offs happen in a stack: export, rename, re-upload,
   re-approve. Counted on the traced workflow itself (arithmetic on the
   table, labeled as such), never presented as a survey figure.
3. Where each of those jobs happens in the hubStudio app, and whether the file
   is carried or re-uploaded. App facts from hubstudio-positioning.md and the
   help center only: create-an-image.md, history.md, assets-library.md,
   campaigns.md, validation.md, linkedin.md (as the model of the network
   modules), balance-and-payments.md, your-team.md.
4. The version and approval record in the app: versions on one Validation
   thread, nothing on it can be deleted, a post waiting for approval is
   locked. From validation.md and linkedin.md.
5. The cost structure of the app: prepaid balance in real currency, no
   subscription, no seat fees, price shown before every run, failed run not
   charged, balance does not expire, editors and publishing free except X
   posts, storage and downloads a small charge. From positioning, never an
   amount.
6. The cost structure of a stack: per-seat or per-plan subscriptions per tool.
   Stated as a structure only. No category price band is needed and none is
   printed (the brief says "cost structure, not prices").
7. A martech utilization figure or a tool-count figure, only from a published
   survey with sample and method, attributed to the category and the date.
   Cut if none clears the bar.
8. A context-switching or app-toggling figure, same bar.
9. What the app does not do: no API, no DAM or PIM connectors, no brand kits,
   no SSO, no mobile app. From positioning ("Never claim for the app").
10. When a stack still wins: one specialist task done all day by one expert,
    or a team that depends on a connector the app does not have. Argued, no
    figure.
11. The networks the app publishes to and the limits worth naming: LinkedIn,
    Instagram, Facebook, TikTok (Beta), X; YouTube prepared in the app and
    published by hand. Up to 20 accounts per post, queue with retries,
    re-purpose for other networks. From positioning and linkedin.md.

## The hand-off trace (the page's own count)

Assumptions, printed on the page: one LinkedIn post with one picture, one
revision round, then the same post adapted for Instagram; a five-tool stack
(image generator, image editor, shared file store, approval tool, scheduler),
the copy written in an ordinary document. Counted moves: each export
(download), each upload, each rename, each paste of the copy into another
tool.

| Step | Stack: what moves | Moves | One app: where it happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Make the picture | Generate, download | 1 export | Image studio, or Render with AI in the post; saved to History and the Assets Library on render |
| Crop and retouch | Upload to the editor, export | 1 upload, 1 export | Image editor in the browser, Save and use it in the post |
| File it | Upload to the file store, rename to convention | 1 upload, 1 rename | Already in the Assets Library; the Campaign choice files it as it is made |
| Send for approval | Upload the picture, paste the copy | 1 upload, 1 paste | Send for validation from the post: copy and pictures go together, nothing uploaded again |
| Revision round | Upload to the editor, export, upload to the approval tool, upload to the file store | 3 uploads, 1 export | Edit in the post, a new version on the same thread; the post is locked while it waits |
| Schedule | Upload the approved picture, paste the copy | 1 upload, 1 paste | Publish now or Schedule from the same post |
| Second network | Upload to the editor, export the new crop, upload to the scheduler, paste the adapted copy | 2 uploads, 1 export, 1 paste | Re-purpose for other networks |

Total for the stack: 4 exports, 9 uploads, 1 rename, 3 pastes, 17 moves. Two
approvals are given, and neither is given on the object that is finally
scheduled: the reviewer approves a file and a text in one tool, and the post
is assembled again in another. In the app: no export, upload, rename or paste
between tools on this trace. A source photo uploaded once (a product shot to
edit) would add one upload to both ways of working.
