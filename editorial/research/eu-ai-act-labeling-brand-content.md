## Sourcing note: how the instrument text was read

EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202 with an empty body on three URL forms on 2026-10-08
(OJ:L_202601744, CELEX:32026R1744, CELEX:32024R1689), the same failure logged
by briefs 24 and 27. The AI Act text was therefore read on the European
Commission's AI Act Service Desk (ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu), which
publishes the consolidated text and flags the Omnibus amendments ("AMENDED",
"NEW", consolidated version dated 27 July 2026). That is the Commission
publishing its own instrument, so it is treated as primary. The Commission's
guidelines (C(2026) 5054) reproduce Article 50 in full and match the Service
Desk wording, which is the cross-check.

The EU texts use British spelling ("labelling", "programme", "colour"). The
house checker fails British spellings in publishable copy, so blockquotes on
the page are close paraphrases in American spelling, attributed to the
instrument, not presented as verbatim quotations. Short fragments in quotation
marks are kept only where they contain no British spelling.

platform labels and shows where the two triggers disagree for a brand asset.

## Cleared for use

Attribution forms as they will run on the page (American spelling, close
paraphrase unless in quotation marks).

> Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Providers of generative AI systems
> already on the market before that date have until 2 December 2026 to meet the
> machine-readable marking duty in Article 50(2), under Article 111(4), added
> by the AI Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026.
> Source: AI Act consolidated text on the European Commission's AI Act Service
> Desk, version of 27 July 2026, and the Commission's AI Act policy page, both
> read 8 October 2026.

> The Commission had proposed six months for the marking grace period, the
> Parliament wanted three, and the agreement of 7 May 2026 landed on 2 December
> 2026. Source: European Parliamentary Research Service briefing PE 782.651,
> June 2026.

> A company that merely commissions an advertising agency, without deciding
> or controlling whether and how the agency uses AI, is not a deployer. Where
> the deployer is a company, an advertising company for instance, its staff are
> not separate deployers, and it stays the deployer when it brings in
> freelancers under its control. Source: European Commission guidelines on
> Article 50, C(2026) 5054, 20 July 2026, paragraph 14.

> An AI image of a product in an advertisement or on packaging is a deep fake
> when it can mislead about the product's appearance, characteristics or use. A
> real car shown against an AI-generated background is not, as long as the ad
> does not mislead about the car. Source: European Commission guidelines on
> Article 50, 20 July 2026, examples under paragraph 116.

> Deployers cannot rely on the provider's machine-readable marking to meet the
> disclosure duty, because a person cannot perceive it without a tool. Source:
> European Commission guidelines on Article 50, 20 July 2026, paragraph 117.

> Where a very large platform gives deployers a labeling tool that produces a
> clear and distinguishable disclosure, they can rely on it within that
> platform. The responsibility stays with the deployer. Source: European
> Commission guidelines on Article 50, 20 July 2026, paragraph 126.

> Information tucked into a manual, under layers of menus or in terms of use
> is not clear and distinguishable. Source: European Commission guidelines on
> Article 50, 20 July 2026, paragraph 142.

> Non-compliance with Article 50 can draw fines of up to 15 million euros or 3
> percent of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, and for small and
> mid-sized companies whichever is lower. Source: AI Act Article 99(4) and
> 99(6), consolidated text of 27 July 2026, and the Commission guidelines,
> paragraph 152.

> The final code of practice was published on 10 June 2026, judged adequate by
> the Commission and the AI Board, and signed by about 190 companies and
> organizations by the end of July 2026. Source: European Commission, code of
> practice page and news item, 31 July 2026; the count is the Commission's
> tally of its own signatory register.

> The label's main element is the capital letters "AI", placed where nothing
> covers it (the code's example is the top right corner), shown at the start of
> a video and again after breaks. Source: Code of Practice on Transparency of
> AI-Generated Content, Section 2, Measures 1.1 and 1.2, 10 June 2026.

> Meta requires a label on photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio
> that was digitally generated or altered, and does not require one on images.
> Source: Instagram Help Center, "Label AI content on Instagram," read 8
> October 2026.

> TikTok requires creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited
> content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people, and says unlabeled
