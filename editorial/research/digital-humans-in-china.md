**The bar:** the ranking set is price tables per named tool, one to three
tables each, dated 2025 to 2026, plus two 2022 media features. None separates a
subscription clone from a bespoke capture from a 3D character; none carries a
consent chain; the only "rules" content is five unsourced red lines. The bar to
beat: 3,000 words, a decision table by product type, a build and run cost table
that publishes the spread by type with dates, a rules table with instrument,
article, issuing body and effective date, and the consent chain.

**The gap, in one sentence:** Chinese-language pages price digital humans only
as seller-written tool tables that name vendors and put a 398 RMB subscription
clone next to a 300,000 RMB bespoke 3D build without saying they are different
products, and not one sets build and run cost beside the consent chain and the
livestream and labeling rules that decide whether the thing can go on air.

**H1 consequence:** the working H1 stands. "Build cost, run cost and labeling
rules" is exactly the pairing the ranking set never makes. The page's spine is
the product-type split (licensed real-person replica, custom digital human,
generic avatar), because the price conflict only resolves once the products are
separated. English SERP unmapped, so this verdict is provisional on the English
side.

## R6. Where sources conflict

**Build cost for a presenter cloned from a real person spans about two orders
of magnitude, and the spread is a product difference, not noise.** 398 RMB
(seller list price, undated) and 1,680 RMB (single-use card relayed November
2025) price a likeness clone inside a self-serve video tool: the buyer uploads
20 minutes of footage and gets an avatar that talks to a script. 8,000 to
30,000 RMB (intermediary table, April 2026) prices a bespoke 2D capture. 49,999
RMB and up (relayed November 2025) prices a high-end managed service. Publish
three bands with their dates and what each buys. Never a midpoint.

**Photoreal 3D conflicts by a factor of up to three at the floor, and by an
order of magnitude against 2022.** 20,000 to 100,000 RMB a character (July 2026,
seller-affiliated review) against 80,000 to 300,000 RMB and up (April 2026,
intermediary). 300,000 to 500,000 RMB average for high fidelity and a 2 to 3
million RMB ceiling for photoreal (practitioners, August 2022). Why they
conflict: the 2026 pages price a character asset, the 2022 quotes price a
project including rigging, motion capture and operation; tooling changed between
2022 and 2026; and every figure is published by a party selling one of the
routes. Publish the 2026 pair as the current spread and the 2022 figures only
with the year in the same sentence, if at all.

**Voice clone: 9.9 RMB to 9,998 RMB.** A consumer voice preset inside a tool
against an advanced clone relayed for one platform. Same product word, different
product.

**A search summary and the page disagree.** The search summary gave 7,980 RMB
for a likeness clone on the card whose page reads 398 RMB. The page wins; the
summary figure is logged as do not publish.

**Livestream run cost has no conflict because it has no data.** One reference
case and zero licence prices. That is the R6 finding for run cost.

**Livestream measures, Article 17 wording differs between the two government
reproductions.** The Hunan text reads "livestream marketers or room operators
who are natural persons", the Beijing text "livestream marketers who are natural
persons". Irrelevant to this page (age rule); noted so a later brief does not
quote it from the wrong copy.
