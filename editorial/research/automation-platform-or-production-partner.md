## Decision axes the article needs

The brief asks for three axes: volume, variation count, and the share of work
that is originated rather than adapted.

**There is no sourced crossover point on any of the three.** This was searched
directly. The only numbers in circulation, for example a threshold of about 500
assets a month, come from sellers of automation tooling, carry no sample and no
method, and are logged in **Do not publish**. The article must reason
structurally and say on the page that no published benchmark sets the line.

The structural reasoning is fully sourced, and each axis rests on a claim above.

| Axis | What moves it | Sourced basis | What the article may say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | Number of finished assets needed per period | Claim B: production budget share fell to 19 percent from 24 percent while nearly two thirds expect to increase social output | High volume argues for the multiply layer, which is usually already owned |
| Variation count | Number of versions from one master | Claims A and C: the layer assembles supplied components; weak masters wear out under repetition, strong ones do not | Variation multiplies whatever the master already is, in either direction |
| Originated share | Portion of work with no existing master | Claim A: an undefined component is a failure state with a fallback, not a generation request | Any originated share above zero needs an origination layer, whatever the volume is |
| Ownership | Whether the automation layer is already in place | Claim F: 66 percent of brands run an in-house agency, most already implementing AI production | Usually the platform decision is already made, which reframes the question |
| Cost basis | Seat and period against asset and deliverable | Claim E: published seat pricing runs roughly $12 to $400 per user per month, enterprise tiers unpublished | The two layers are priced on different units and do not net against each other |

The honest conclusion the article should carry: the crossover is not a
published number, it is a property of the buyer's own mix. If the originated
share is zero, the multiply layer is enough. Above zero, no amount of volume
removes the need for origination, because the layer that multiplies is defined
by a standard that requires the component to be supplied first.
