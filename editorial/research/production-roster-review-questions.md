**The bar:** the ranking set runs 1,800 to 3,500 words. Table count is zero on
almost every page, one on a single page, so two or three aligned tables clears
the field outright. Not one page in the five SERPs carries a dated, sourced
figure with a stated sample and method. Two page types split the whole field:
the hire-time vendor listicle of ten to forty ungrouped questions, and the
consultancy essay on roster size. A 2,800-word piece with a grouped fourteen
question set, a before-and-after table, a copyable scoring table and four dated
trade-body figures beats every page listed above on depth, structure and
evidence at the same time.

**The gap, in one sentence:** Every ranking page either vets one vendor at hire
time or advises procurement on how many agencies to keep, so no page gives the
buyer a grouped, scoreable question set for reviewing an existing production
roster at the moment production supply centralizes.

Secondary finding worth carrying into the brief: the primary query is polluted
by software and manufacturing results for "production readiness review." The H1
and the first hundred words must make "production" mean advertising production
immediately, or the piece inherits the wrong intent.

### R6. Triangulation notes

- **ANA 2017 figures.** The ANA's own press release at
  `ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2017-production-transparency` and its numeric
  mirror at `ana.net/content/show/id/45531` both returned HTTP 403 to automated
  fetch on 2026-09-09, so the primary page could not be read. Three independent
  trade outlets carry the two figures in near-identical wording on 8 and 9
  August 2017. Two of the three were fetched and read in full. The figures are
  therefore triangulated, not primary. **The supporting survey's respondent
  count is not published in any source found.** The 30 executives and 12
  organizations belong to the task force behind the study, not to the survey
  sample, and one outlet explicitly says it cannot tell whether they are the
  same population. Any use of these figures must state that the respondent count
  was not published. Do not convert "fewer than half" or "over 60 percent" into
  a headcount.
- **Date conflict on the ANA release.** One outlet dates it 8 August 2017, two
  date it 9 August 2017. Publish the month, "August 2017", not the day.
- **WFA production figures.** Confirmed on the trade body's own page and
  independently in secondary coverage, same numbers, same sample description.
  Primary.
- **One WFA parse conflict.** An automated read of the trade body page returned
  "66 percent increase in outsourced on-site teams" while the same page and the
  secondary coverage both say two thirds of brands changed their agency model in
  the last four years and that on-site outsourcing "rose significantly" with no
  percentage. Treat the 66 percent as a misparse of the two-thirds figure. Use
  "two thirds changed their agency model." Do not publish a percentage for the
  growth of on-site teams.
- **Market count conflict.** The trade body page says 500+ brand leaders in 16
  markets for the 2026 media budgets survey; a secondary summary says 17. The
  range is 16 to 17 and the reason is unresolved, so the market count is not
  used at all.
- **Centralization pattern.** Established by dated trade reporting from
  2026-02-05 that counts four separate reorganizations across 2025 and early
  2026, corroborated by separate dated coverage of one of them and by a
  corporate announcement. Independently, the advertiser trade body's March 2025
  research names centralization as a returning model from the buyer's side. Two
  independent lines of evidence, one supply side and one buy side. The pattern
  is safe to state. The instances are not.
