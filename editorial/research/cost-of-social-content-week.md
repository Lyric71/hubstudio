## R1. What has to be true (mapped before any lookup)

1. The defined week is a specification the article sets, not a figure: ten
   posts across four networks (LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok), eight
   with still pictures and two with short videos, captions written, one
   approval round per post. It is the unit of comparison, labeled as such.
2. US in-house: median and percentile wages for the occupations that make
   social content (marketing specialists, graphic designers, public relations
   specialists, film and video editors), plus a manager's hourly wage for
   review time. BLS OEWS, May 2025.
3. US employer-cost uplift: total compensation over wages, private industry,
   latest quarter. BLS ECEC.
4. Europe in-house: earnings for comparable occupations from Eurostat SES 2022
   or a national statistics office, plus the non-wage share of labor costs
   (Eurostat labour cost levels).
5. Freelance: hourly or daily rates from published surveys with a sample and a
   method, attributed to category and date.
6. Agency: monthly social retainer tiers read on agencies' own published price
   pages on one stated date, no firm named, labeled a market claim.
7. Hidden costs: approval rounds, rework, tool seats, stock licenses, manager
   review hours. Structure, with a figure only where one is sourced.
8. App route: structure only, from `hubstudio-positioning.md` and
   `src/content/help` (balance-and-payments.md, troubleshooting.md, x.md,
   campaigns.md). No amount.
9. Studio route: the three ways to work and what the studio does, from
   `hubstudio-positioning.md`. No rate.

Cut at R1, before lookup: hours per post or per week for any route (no
published measurement with a method; the only time-per-post figures in the
SERP are seller estimates, see R2), software seat prices (named vendors only,
ledger do-not-publish row from brief 15), approval-round frequencies (ledger
do-not-publish rows from 2026-10-13: misquoted or seller-published).

**The bar:** opened pages run 800 to 6,500 words, about 3,000 typically; 0 to
2 tables on most, 4 to 8 on the deepest; ranges, a loaded multiplier of 1.4
to 2.0 asserted without source, and an FAQ. Nearly all are written by
vendors, agencies or staffing firms selling the alternative. One page names a
sample; one quotes BLS, for the wrong occupation group.

**The gap, in one sentence:** No ranking page builds the cost of one defined
unit (a week of posts) from official wage statistics with a stated method, and
none counts approval rounds, rework, tool seats, stock licenses and the
reviewer's hours.
