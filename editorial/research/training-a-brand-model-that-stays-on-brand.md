## R1. What has to be true before anything is looked up

1. An approval rate for a brand model has been measured somewhere with a
   stated approver, sample and period. **Resolved: no.** Not in the SERP, not
   on the Chinese web, not in peer-reviewed work. Publishable as an absence.
2. hubStudio's 22 to 78 percent approval movement has a written method.
   **Resolved: no.** Blocked. Cut from the outline.
3. hubStudio's sevenfold output rise and "about 60 percent lower" costs have a
   written method. **Resolved: no.** Blocked. Cut.
4. Peer-reviewed research measures how faithfully a fine-tuned image model
   reproduces a subject or style, with a stated metric and dataset.
   **Resolved: yes, adjacent only.** Subject fidelity by embedding similarity
   on a 30-subject dataset, plus a human preference study.
5. Those automated fidelity scores track human judgment. **Resolved: only
   partly.** A 2025 peer-reviewed benchmark puts their agreement at about half
   to three fifths of human-to-human agreement. This is what makes a human
   approval gate defensible rather than decorative.
6. A fine-tuned model can be trained from a small number of images.
   **Resolved: yes, for the method studied**, three to five images of a subject
   in the 2023 paper. Not a brand-library requirement.
7. Adapter-style fine-tuning leaves the base model's weights untouched and
   trains a separate small set of weights. **Resolved: yes**, by the 2021 paper
   that introduced low-rank adaptation. Matters for the ownership question.
8. Generative image models can reproduce training images, including
   photographs of people and trademarked logos. **Resolved: yes**, peer-reviewed
   2023. Supports the "never in the training set" list as precaution, not as a
   measured risk rate for brand fine-tunes.
9. China regulates training data for generative AI services. **Resolved:
   yes, with a scope limit.** The 2023 Interim Measures, Article 7, apply to
   services offered to the public in China; Article 2 excludes organizations
   that develop or apply the technology without offering it to the public.
10. Consent is required before a real person's face or voice goes into a model.
    **Resolved: reused** from brief 26 (China deep synthesis Article 14; the
    2025 union commercials agreement training clause).
11. Who owns a trained brand model. **Resolved: reused** from brief 22.
    Copyright does not settle it for prompt-driven output; the contract does.
    hubStudio's published policy is that the client owns it.
12. Three to six weeks from brief to trained model. **Resolved: first-party,
    no method.** May run only as a published range attributed to the AI

13. The five-stage pipeline (Brief, Train, Create, Perfect, Deploy).
    **Resolved: first-party description** on the AI excellence page. A

14. A published minimum number of training images for commercial fine-tuning
    services. **Resolved: not cleared.** Vendor product pages only, not read at

### What the research actually measures, and what it does not

> In peer-reviewed fine-tuning research, "staying on subject" is scored by
> machine, not by a brand team. A 2023 study fine-tuned a text-to-image model
> on three to five photos of a single subject, generated 3,000 test images
> across 30 subjects and 25 prompts, and scored fidelity as the average
> similarity between generated and real photos inside a pretrained image
> encoder. The fine-tuned model scored 0.696 on that scale, against 0.774 for
> real photos of the same subject compared with each other.
> Source: peer-reviewed computer vision conference paper, CVPR 2023, company
> research lab; automated embedding similarity on a 30-subject evaluation set.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242

> The same study asked 72 people 1,800 paired questions about which image
> "best reproduces the identity" of a reference object. Raters picked the
> fine-tuned model's image 68 percent of the time, an earlier method's 22
> percent, and could not decide 10 percent of the time. That is a preference
> between two research methods, not an approval rate for finished work.
> Source: peer-reviewed computer vision conference paper, CVPR 2023; pairwise
> forced-choice user study, rater expertise not stated.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242

> The authors list where fine-tuning breaks: rare contexts, the background
> bleeding into the subject's appearance, overfitting when a prompt resembles
> the training photos, and the plain admission that "some subjects are easier
> to learn than others."
> Source: peer-reviewed computer vision conference paper, CVPR 2023, authors'
> own limitations section.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242

> Automated consistency scores are a weak stand-in for a human reviewer. A
> 2025 benchmark had seven annotators rate generated images across 150
> reference images and 1,350 prompts, then checked how well automated scores
> tracked them. On whether the subject was preserved, the two common
> image-similarity scores reached only 50.72 percent and 60.98 percent of the
> agreement human raters showed with each other.
> Source: peer-reviewed machine learning conference paper, ICLR 2025; Pearson
> correlation with human ratings, expressed as a share of human-to-human
> agreement, each image rated by at least two annotators.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16855

> The same benchmark found a multimodal language model used as a judge came
> closer, at 83.31 percent of human-to-human agreement on subject preservation
> and 98.17 percent on prompt following, and its authors warn that those
> results "heavily rely on the advancements of multimodal large language
> models and require carefully designed system prompts."
> Source: peer-reviewed machine learning conference paper, ICLR 2025, Table 4
> and limitations; the paper's text gives 98.71 percent where its table gives
> 98.17 percent, and the table value is used here.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16855

### How a brand model is built, and what that means for ownership

> The most widely used lightweight fine-tuning approach "freezes the
> pre-trained model weights and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices
> into each layer." In practice the brand's training produces a separate, small
> set of weights that sits on top of a base model the brand does not own.
> Source: 2021 research paper introducing low-rank adaptation, company research
> lab and academic authors; method description, abstract read at source.
> https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685

Writer's note, not for a blockquote: the second sentence is the inference the
page can draw from the mechanism, and it holds only for adapter-style training.
A full fine-tune rewrites the base weights. Say "in the common adapter
approach", never "always".
