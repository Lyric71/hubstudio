## The absence that is the article. No published retry or selection rate

This section exists because the brief asked for a retry and selection rate and
the honest answer is that nobody publishes one. That absence is the finding.
Write it plainly. Do not fill it.

**What was searched.** Five queries on 2026-09-10, in English and in Chinese,
including the direct question in the words a buyer would use. Every result was
one of four things: a seller of generation tools, a seller of finished AI ads,
an API vendor reporting uptime rather than yield, or an academic paper on
detecting generated video, which measures whether a classifier can spot a
synthetic clip and says nothing about how many tries a shot takes.

**The two figures that exist at all, and how far apart they are.**

| Figure | Where it comes from | What stands behind it | Usable |
|---|---|---|---|
| About 300 seconds of generated source for each second used, on one 90-second short | A named post-production lead, interviewed in a visual-effects trade publication, April 2024 | One production, a three-person team, the practitioner's own estimate. No log, no counting instrument | Yes, as a single disclosed production on a 2024 model, never as a rate |
| About four generations per usable clip, with quality-tier bands | A tool vendor's own FAQ, updated July 2026 | The publisher's unnamed internal case work. No instrument, no sample published, no third-party check | No. Market claim only, and the vendor sells the thing the figure flatters |

Those two published numbers differ by roughly seventy-five times. Neither is a
benchmark. The article says exactly that, gives both shapes without naming
either publisher, and then does the useful thing instead: it shows what the
retry rate does to the bill at each end, so a buyer can measure their own.

**What the writer must do with this.** State that no independent benchmark
exists. State that the only practitioner disclosure found is a single 2024
production. State that the only rate published by anyone selling the tools is
their own unaudited case work. Then publish the arithmetic below, which turns
the missing number into something the reader can supply from their own logs.
Do not invent a house rate. Do not present hubStudio delivery data here: the
retake and selection figure the brief asks for is not in the source ledger, so
it carries no method, no sample and no period and cannot be published. Flag it
as `TODO: client sign-off` if the draft wants it.

## Worked arithmetic, shown step by step

All of it runs on published inputs already cleared above. The article shows the
steps, because the point is that the reader can rerun it on their own numbers.

**Step 1. What a second of generated video costs at published list rates.**

Credit route, one published card read 2026-09-10:

- $12 a month buys 625 credits. 12 / 625 = $0.0192 a credit.
- $28 a month buys 2,250 credits. 28 / 2,250 = $0.01244 a credit.
- $76 a month buys 9,500 credits. 76 / 9,500 = $0.008 a credit.
- The card states 12 credits for each second of generated video.
- 0.0192 x 12 = $0.230 a second. 0.01244 x 12 = $0.149 a second.
  0.008 x 12 = $0.096 a second.

API route, one published list read 2026-09-10: $0.05 to $0.60 a second by model
tier and resolution, audio included at the default.

Band to publish: roughly $0.05 to $0.60 a second at list, with the credit route
landing at about $0.10 to $0.23, inside that band. Two independent surfaces.

**Step 2. Thirty seconds of finished screen time, if nothing is retried.**

- 30 x $0.05 = $1.50 at the cheapest published rate.
- 30 x $0.23 = $6.90 on the mid credit tier.
- 30 x $0.60 = $18.00 at the most expensive published rate.

This is the number the dollar-an-ad math is built on, and on its own arithmetic
it is roughly right. That is worth conceding plainly in the article.

**Step 3. The same 30 seconds with a retry rate applied.**

At four generations for each usable one, 30 finished seconds need 120 generated
seconds:

- 120 x $0.05 = $6.00.
- 120 x $0.23 = $27.60.
- 120 x $0.60 = $72.00.

At the 300 to 1 ratio disclosed on one documented production, 30 finished
seconds need 9,000 generated seconds:

- 9,000 x $0.05 = $450.
- 9,000 x $0.23 = $2,070.
- 9,000 x $0.60 = $5,400.

**The finding.** Between those two published ratios the generation bill for the
same 30 seconds moves by about seventy-five times, while the price per second
moves by twelve. The retry rate, the one number nobody publishes, swings the
generation line harder than the price per second everybody advertises. That
sentence is the article.

**Step 4. One hour of a person's time, for scale.**

Using the federal wage series and the published compensation multiplier, both
US private industry:

- Film and video editors, median $75,420 a year. 75,420 / 2,080 = $36.26 an
  hour. x 1.43 = $51.85 an hour loaded.
- Camera operators, median $74,990. 74,990 / 2,080 = $36.05. x 1.43 = $51.55.
- Art directors, median $114,850. 114,850 / 2,080 = $55.22. x 1.43 = $78.97.

Read that against step 3. At the cheapest published generation rates, 120
seconds of generated footage costs about $6, less than a sixth of one loaded
editor hour in the United States. The generation line is not where the money
goes. The hours around it are. The article should say the quiet part: what
collapsed is the cost of the attempt, not the cost of the judgment.

The 2,080-hour divisor is a stated convention (40 hours for 52 weeks) and the
article must say so where it uses it. The wage medians are US, and the article
must state the geography every time it uses them.

## Where AI genuinely does cost a fraction, with the conditions attached

Cleared as reasoning from evidence already in this file, not as a new claim.
Every condition below traces to a row above.

| Condition | Why it holds, from the evidence here |
|---|---|
| The shot is generated once or twice, not hunted | The generation line is trivial at low retry counts and dominates at high ones. Step 3 shows the swing |
| No live plate, no talent, no location | Those lines are the bid form sections a generation bill never touches |
| Nothing needs a usage payment or a release | Media usage sits outside the production estimate entirely, and outside the tool bill too |
| The output is a variant of an approved master, not a new master | Versioning is bid separately by convention, so the cheap case is the version, not the original |
| The quality bar is a social feed, not a broadcast finish | The only published yield bands that exist at all fall by an order of magnitude as the bar rises, even in the seller's own telling |
| The team already knows what shot it wants | What collapsed is the cost of an attempt. Judgment did not get cheaper |

The article states these as conditions, in the buyer's words, and never as a
savings percentage. No percentage on this page stands in for a number a source
withheld.
