=== Page 3, "THE 2026 SURVEY / Introduction / A profession in transition." ===

This year's survey ran in February 2026 and pulled in 573 responses from copywriters across the UK and a handful of other markets. That makes it one of our largest datasets, and the most diverse in employment mix: freelancers still dominate at 54%, but the share of in-house and agency-employed copywriters is the highest we've seen, representing nearly a third of our respondents.

Questions in the 2026 survey covered some new ground, including credentials and formal recognition, written contracts, late invoices, payment disputes, ethical pushback, and writing for vulnerable audiences.

=== Page 4, "INTRODUCTION - IN NUMBERS / Five numbers that summarise the year" ===

573 - Responses to the 2026 survey. 54% freelance · 19% in-house · 14% agency-employed · 7% mixed · 6% agency founders.

£440 - Median freelance day rate. Up from £430 last year and £400 in 2023 - a slow, real-terms climb.

A NOTE ON METHODOLOGY: Starting with this year's survey, we report income and day rate using median averages as the headline metric, with the mean alongside. Median averages are more indicative of a typical individual's experience; mean averages are more easily skewed by a handful of high-earners. The UK Office for National Statistics uses the same approach for reporting income and the gender pay gap. Previous ProCopywriters surveys reported mean averages; both metrics are included for direct comparison.

=== Page 34, "THE 2026 SURVEY · SECTION 07 / Freelance copywriter experiences" ===

Our freelance section tells the story of 383 copywriters who describe themselves as either freelance, contracting or self-employed, and explores the specific challenges related to self-employment.

Day rates

The headline freelance day rate in 2026 is £440 (median), based on 338 freelance respondents who answered this question. The mean is £465, a drop from last year's result of £480.

A note on the day rate headline. The 2025 ProCopywriters survey quoted a mean day rate of £480. We are now leading with the median for the methodological reasons set out in the introduction; both figures are shown across the last four years below for direct comparison.
