## Constraint banner, read before drafting

Five limits apply to everything below.

1. **No competitor, vendor, agency, data firm or trade outlet reaches the
   page by name.** The SERP tables name domains because the spec's table shape
   requires it. Platforms (Tmall, Taobao, JD, Douyin) may be named, because
   their own schedules are the subject. Chinese news outlets are recorded
   below for the evidence trail; on the page, attribute to "Chinese business
   press reporting the platform's announcement" plus the date.
2. **Every milestone date carries its year.** These dates move every cycle,
   and in 2026 the Tmall 618 start moved later, not earlier. A date printed
   without a year is a wrong date next year.
3. **No production lead time is sourced.** No platform, regulator or survey
   publishes how long asset production should take before a platform
   deadline. The backward calendar is reasoning from verified platform dates
   and must be labeled as reasoning on the page. See Do not publish.
4. **No GMV, sales or "strongest growth" figure.** None found carries a
   stated method a reader could check. Cut them all.
5. **No hubStudio rate.** The slot's delivery-record number needs a method
   before it runs; see Notes for the writer.

### Vendor-published share and the verdict on the brief's SERP claim

32 results across four phrasings. 7 are off-intent. Of the 25 on-intent
results, **16 are published by a vendor, agency, SaaS tool or data firm
(64 percent)**, 1 by a platform, and the rest by news, trade press, an
encyclopedia or a personal blog.

**The brief's verdict, VACANT, is confirmed with one correction.** The
results do drift to encyclopedia entries and off-intent pages (a games
company, a music release, a corporate asset sale). They do not drift to
Western event production. The dominant page type is the China agency
explainer, which lists festival dates for consumers and never the merchant
gates behind them.

Two pages of 32 print a dated platform milestone at all: the JD corporate
blog (its own 2024 start time) and one tech-press recap (2024 presale start
dates). Zero print a merchant sign-up window, a listing deadline, or a
schedule counted backward from either. Where an agency calendar gives
"mid-May" or "late May", it gives no year, and the 2026 Tmall 618 start
(21 May) broke the pattern it describes.

**Search in Chinese found what English did not.** The Chinese business press
reprints the platforms' merchant rules the day they are issued, with exact
timestamps to the second for warm-up, deposit, final payment and spot sale.
None of that reaches an English page ranking for these queries.

## The bar

Ranking pages run roughly 800 to 2,000 words, carry zero or one table, and
cite no dated platform schedule. The brief's 1,900 words with a week-by-week
table, a failure-mode table and a checklist clears the field on structure.
The differentiator is two dated milestone tables with years, sourced to
reporting of the platform announcements, and an honest statement that the
backward calendar is reasoning because no one publishes production lead
times.

**The gap, in one sentence.** Every page ranking for these queries explains
what Singles Day and 618 are or reports how they sold, and not one prints the
platforms' own dated merchant milestones (sign-up window, listing deadline,
presale and spot-sale start, by year) or counts a production schedule
backward from them, so a brand planning assets has no dated anchor to work
from.

### R6. Where sources conflict, and why

1. **2026 Tmall 618 start: 21 May against 12 or 13 May.** The 21 May date
   comes from trade and technology press reporting the platform's 2026
   merchant rules on 29 April and 2 May, which also give the full wave
   structure and listing deadlines. The 12 and 13 May dates come from
   consumer guides and local news aggregators that cite no rule. The likely
   cause: JD's 2026 warm-up season began 13 May, and Tmall's 2025 presale
   opened 13 May, so guides written from last year's template carried the
   date forward. Publish 21 May, and say that consumer guides printed an
   earlier date. Confidence in 21 May is triangulated, not primary.
2. **2025 Tmall Double 11 spot sale: 20 October against 21 October.** The
   reprinted rules give 20:00:00 on 20 October. Consumer guides say 21
   October. The rules win; the guides appear to have carried 2024's 21
   October date forward.
3. **2025 Tmall Double 11 presale sign-up close: 30 September against 14
   October.** Both sources are vendor restatements. Neither links the rule.
   Publish only that sign-up opened on 23 September, reported, or cut it.
4. **"The start moves earlier every year."** True from 2023 to 2024 on both
   festivals and both big marketplaces. Not true after that: Tmall's Double
   11 moved one day later in 2025 (15 October against 14 October), and Tmall's
   618 moved eight days later in 2026 (21 May against 13 May). JD kept moving
   earlier on Double 11 (23 October, 14 October, 9 October) and, on 618,
   split warm-up (13 May) from the official start (30 May in 2026, 31 May in
   2025). Publish the dates by year and let the table show the direction.
