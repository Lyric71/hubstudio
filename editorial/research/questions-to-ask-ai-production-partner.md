Scope note the writer must carry onto the page in the article's own voice:
this describes production practice, not legal advice. Every regime below is
cited to the instrument, the issuing body and the effective date, never to a
law firm alert.

Reader stage is procurement. The instrument has to work on three vendors at
once, hubStudio included, and the brief is explicit that a question hubStudio
answers awkwardly stays in and gets said out loud.

**The bar:** the ranking set runs 900 to 2,000 words, list-shaped, and almost
entirely unsourced. Across all four queries, thirty ranked results, not one
carries a scoring table a buyer can copy, not one names an instrument with an
article number and an effective date, and not one asks a single question about
a physical camera, a signed release, a delivered file's metadata, or surge
capacity in a production week. The bar to beat is 2,600 words with twelve
questions, a good answer and a weak answer for each, one copyable three-vendor
scoring table, and every regulatory question cited to the instrument.

**The gap, in one sentence:** Everything ranking for this query evaluates an AI
software vendor, a development shop or an avatar platform, so nobody has
published a procurement instrument for buying creative production from a studio
that generates the work, which is why not one ranking page asks who owns a
custom-trained brand model, who signed the likeness release, who carries the
label into the delivered file, or whether the partner can put a camera in front
of the actual product.

**H1 consequence:** the ranking pages all miss that the thing being bought is
work, not software. The H1 is the question set for a production partner, and
the article's first move is to say that a software questionnaire will not score
a studio.

**SERP verdict: VACANT, confirmed.** The brief's call is correct. The
competition is not weak, it is absent: every ranking page answers a different
purchase.
