How to check and manage your product image status
Product images are essential for shoppers discovering your products across Google surfaces. Because Google Merchant Center crawls image URLs you provide in your feed rather than storing uploaded image files directly, tracking the status of your images and resolving review, crawling, or compliance issues quickly is vital to keeping your products approved and active.

Check the status of your images
After you submit the URL(s) for image attributes, Google’s automated crawlers retrieve and review your image assets. You can monitor image health and review progress across three primary areas in Merchant Center:

Understand image statuses and processing timelines
Google must fetch and evaluate every image URL before an item can serve. New images are typically crawled and approved within 3 days, during which the product remains under review. If you are updating an existing image, we temporarily serve your previously approved image for up to 30 days while the new URL processes to prevent serving disruptions; however, you should always use a unique, new URL to prompt a faster crawl and avoid a detection delay of up to 6 weeks. To prevent timeout disapprovals, ensure your servers are configured to allow immediate access from Googlebot and Googlebot-Image.

Quick tips for uploading your first images
If you are a new merchant setting up your inventory for the first time, Google accesses your product photos via the image web links (URLs) you provide in your product data. Ensure Google can read your links on the very first try by following these simple preparation rules:
Use direct, public Links
: Your URLs must link directly to the raw image file (for example, ending in .jpg or .png). Do not use links that go to a general store web page, a drive preview, or password-protected directories.
Confirm crawler access
: Google's crawlers (Googlebot and Googlebot-image) must have permission to view your website's files. Ensure your website hosting provider or security settings do not block automated traffic.
Keep links stable
: Avoid using temporary URLs that change every time your site updates. Stable links prevent unnecessary re-crawling backlogs.

Best practices
When updating product photography, follow these best practices to avoid delays and mismatched customer experiences:
Always use a new URL
: When changing an image, do not overwrite the image file while preserving the old URL. Modifying the file name (for example, product-v2.jpg) forces Googlebot to prioritize the recrawl within 24–72 hours rather than waiting up to 6 weeks. Before submitting updated image URLs in your feed, open each link in a web browser to verify that the image loads directly without errors or redirect issues.
Manage the 30-day transition window
: When you change an image URL for an existing approved product, Google continues serving the previous approved image for up to 30 days while evaluating the new asset.
Prevent variant and attribute conflicts
: Because the fallback image displays during processing, do not change associated product attributes (such as color or pattern) without verifying they still match the fallback image. If a critical visual conflict occurs, revert the attribute change or assign a brand-new item ID
