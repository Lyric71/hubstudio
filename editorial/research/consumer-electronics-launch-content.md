## Constraint banner, read before drafting

1. **Search budget.** WebSearch returned "used its web search budget (200 of 200)"
   after this agent's eighth call on 2026-09-10. Everything after that was a direct
   fetch of a known URL or a URL found inside a fetched page. Three lines of inquiry
   died there and are logged under Do not publish: Chinese enforcement cases on TV
   or phone spec claims, UK rulings on battery life or brightness claims, and a
   second independent TV shipment tracker with a stated method.
2. **Blocked primary hosts.** EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202 with an empty body (a
   JavaScript challenge) for Regulation 2017/1369, Delegated Regulation 2019/2013
   and Regulation 765/2008, in HTML and PDF, by WebFetch and by curl.
   legislation.gov.uk returned 403. ecfr.gov redirected to an unblock page, so FCC
   rules were read on the Legal Information Institute mirror of the eCFR. The Omdia
   press release host was refused by the fetch tool. The Library of Congress and
   Justia hosts of the 1965 Supreme Court decision returned 403; it was read on the
   Legal Information Institute host.
3. **Extraction method.** WebFetch returns a model-read extract of each page. Every
   quotation marked verbatim below came through that extract, except the FTC
   Deception Policy Statement, which was downloaded and converted with pdftotext and
   read line by line. Check 2 must re-read the exact wording of every quote before
   it ships, and the FCC disclosure sentence first.
4. **Chinese web first.** The CCC mark and energy label instruments were searched in
   Chinese and read on Chinese government hosts (samr.gov.cn and the Shenzhen
   market regulator, amr.sz.gov.cn). The Advertising Law was read in Chinese on the
   commerce ministry's legal database. The Chinese searches ran in the same parallel
   batch as the English SERP queries, not after them.
5. **Legal register.** Every compliance row describes production practice, not
   legal advice. The page must say so (SPEC.md structure rule 10).

   the three-vendor workflow would run "three to four months end to end". The
   results label says it "would have run three to four times longer". Months and a

## Cleared for use

Blockquote-ready, American English, no Han characters, no competitor named. The

**1. The UK dash cam ruling: an image sold as device output must be device output.**

> A UK advertising regulator upheld a complaint against a dash cam brand's web
> page after finding that a night image of readable number plates was a stock
> photo with the plates added, not footage from the cameras advertised.
> Consumers, it ruled, would read the image as a screen grab from the product, and
> it told the advertiser not to use images that exaggerate product performance.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 5 June 2024, an upheld investigation under the code's misleading advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules. https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/portable-multimedia-ltd.html

**2. Small print does not fix a misleading image.**

> The same regulator's guidance says images should not be altered in a way that
> exaggerates what the product can do, and that a qualification or small print
> such as "Not representative of actual gameplay" is generally unlikely to stop an
> ad from misleading.
> Source: UK advertising regulator guidance on misleading imagery, 11 September 2025, published advice drawing on six earlier rulings. https://www.asa.org.uk/news/a-picture-says-a-thousand-words-avoiding-misleading-imagery-in-ads.html

Writer note: the guidance names a gameplay disclaimer, not a screen disclaimer.
Applying it to "screen images simulated" is an analogy and must read as one.

**3. Performance claims need evidence before the ad runs.**

> The UK non-broadcast code requires marketers to hold documentary evidence for
> objective claims before an ad runs, and bars ads that mislead by exaggerating
> the capability or performance of a product. The broadcast code applies the same
> two rules to TV.
> Source: UK advertising codes, non-broadcast rules 3.7 and 3.11 and broadcast rules 3.9 and 3.12, code text read on the regulator's site 10 September 2026. https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html

**4. An unsubstantiated performance line, ruled.**

> A UK advertising regulator upheld a complaint against a portable cooler ad that
> claimed it could "Cool Any Room in Seconds," finding the advertiser supplied no
> evidence and the claim was unsubstantiated and misleading.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 26 November 2025, an upheld investigation of a paid online display ad under the code's misleading advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules. https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/ecom7-ltd-a25-1303251-ecom7-ltd.html

**5. Generated footage that overstates the product (ledger reuse).**

> A UK advertising regulator upheld 22 complaints against an ad for a robot toy,
> finding that footage which appeared to show real or AI-generated puppies gave
> the impression the product was highly realistic, and ruled that the advertiser
> must not misleadingly represent or exaggerate the functionality and appearance
> of its products.
> Source: UK advertising regulator ruling, 25 March 2026, an upheld investigation under the code's misleading advertising, substantiation and exaggeration rules. https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/uab-commercecore-g25-1321974-uab-commercecore.html

**6. The tool does not carry the liability (ledger reuse).**

> The same regulator's guidance holds that the advertising code is
> media-neutral: the rules apply regardless of how the content was created, and
> the advertiser remains responsible for any AI output used in its ads.
> Source: UK advertising regulator guidance, 11 June 2026, published guidance for advertisers on AI-generated content. https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ai-and-deepfakes-four-things-advertisers-need-to-know-before-they-hit-run.html

**7. The US mock-up line.**

> The US Supreme Court held that an undisclosed mock-up in a TV demonstration was
> a material deceptive practice because it told viewers they were seeing proof of
> the claim with their own eyes. Where an ad rests on the seller's word rather than
> the viewer's own perception, the Court said, an undisclosed prop is not
> prohibited.
> Source: FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive Co., 380 U.S. 374, decided 5 April 1965, the Court's opinion on a televised shaving demonstration using plexiglass in place of sandpaper. https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/380/374

**8. US: fine print may not cure a misleading visual.**

> The US Federal Trade Commission judges an ad by its overall impression,
> including its visual imagery, and says written disclosures or fine print may be
> insufficient to correct a misleading representation.
> Source: FTC Policy Statement on Deception, 14 October 1983, the Commission's published enforcement policy, read in full text. https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/410531/831014deceptionstmt.pdf

**9. US: substantiation for objective claims.**

> The US Federal Trade Commission treats an objective claim made without a
> reasonable basis as deceptive, and where an ad says "tests prove," it expects the
> advertiser to hold at least the level of proof the ad claims.
> Source: FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation, 23 November 1984, the Commission's published enforcement policy. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation

> China's Advertising Law requires information about a product's performance,

> unambiguous, and requires cited data, statistics and survey results to be true,
> accurate and sourced. An ad counts as false where performance, function,
> specification or quality information does not match reality and materially
> affects the purchase, or where it relies on fabricated or unverifiable data.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China (Guanggao Fa), 2021 amendment, Articles 8, 11 and 28, statute text read in Chinese on 10 September 2026. https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=90361
