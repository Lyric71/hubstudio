## Method statement, read this before using any number below

The Taobao Rule Center gates its rule text. That was established at primary
level for brief 28 on 2026-09-10 and is not re-run here:
`rule.tmall.com/tdetail-11423.htm` returned 302 to `rulechannel.tmall.com/tmall`,
which returned a further 302 and no rule body. No open source reaches the
official white-background image rule, in Chinese or in English. The Taobao Rule
Center is named in this file only as the thing that could not be read. It is
the source of no number below.

Under deviation 7 every value below is a **modal published value**: every
published figure found was recorded, counted by independent domain, and the
value that appears most often is the one published, with its count and, where
material, its spread.

**Two pools, kept separate so no count is double-counted.**

1. **Reused from brief 01** (`tmall-product-image-requirements.md`), an
   eighteen-domain pool. Its white-slot rows are carried forward with their
   counts unchanged and marked `reused, brief 01`. They are not re-counted here.
2. **New for this brief**, a fifteen-domain pool gathered specifically on the
   white-background image (baidi tu), fourteen Chinese-language and one
   English-language. Ten domains were read in full on 2026-09-10. Five were read
   through search-result extracts only, and a value from an extract-only domain
   is never allowed to decide a mode on its own. Several domains also sit in the
   brief 01 pool, which is why the two pools are never added together.
3. **Carried from brief 04** (`jd-image-requirements-vs-tmall.md`): the
   white-background image is JD's first main image and Tmall's fifth, five of
   five pages per platform, no contradicting page. Marked `reused, brief 04`.

**Rules that govern every row.**

1. One domain counts once, however many pages it publishes. Where a domain
   contradicts itself across its own pages, the row says so. One domain in the
   new pool (tudingai.cn) publishes three 2026 pages that disagree with each
   other on fill ratio, file weight and whether a contact shadow is tolerated.
   A second (biaojixia.com) gives RGB 255 255 255 flat on its spec page and a
   tolerance of plus or minus 3 on a tutorial page. Each is counted once, and
   each self-contradiction is printed in the row it affects.
2. A value found on one domain is `contested`, never `consensus`.
3. Every figure was chased for age. Where a value is repeated often but its
   wording traces to one early page, the row says `originates once`.
4. No number below is verified, official, required, or read from the platform.

## The bar

The English pages that rank on these four phrasings are background-removal
landing pages, Western marketplace forum threads and stock libraries. None
carries a Tmall white-slot table. None says the white image is a separate
deliverable. None states where a number came from. The one English sentence in
the whole set is a single unsourced claim on a tool page.

The bar to beat is therefore low on length and high on honesty: 1,400 words
with a white-slot spec table carrying a source count per row, an element table
(allowed or prohibited, and why), a slot-by-category table, the stated method
and a visible reviewed date. Two tables clears the field. Three with source
counts wins it, because no ranking page shows its working.

**H1 consequence.** The English SERP does not know the asset exists. The H1 is
the concept, the white-background image as its own rule set, and the white value
finding is the piece's hard edge.

## R1. What has to be true before looking anything up

Listed before searching. Each resolves in the spec table.

1. The white-background image is a distinct deliverable, not a styled main image
   placed on white.
2. It has a fixed slot, and the slot varies by category.
3. The background is specified as pure white, and a numeric value is published.
4. A tolerance below pure white is published somewhere, or its absence is
   reportable.
5. It has its own dimensions and its own file weight, distinct from the mains.
6. There is a fill ratio or subject-size rule.
7. Shadows and reflections are prohibited, and whether a contact shadow is
   tolerated is published.
8. Models, body parts, hangers, mannequins and tags are prohibited.
9. Text, watermarks, logos, borders and splicing are prohibited.
10. It surfaces somewhere specific: search, recommendation, content channels or
    activity registration.
11. A failing white image has a published consequence, and published rejection
    causes exist.
12. A real rejection notice is obtainable for illustration.

Item 4 is the high-value one and resolves as a conflict, not a consensus. One
domain publishes a tolerance of plus or minus 3 (252 to 255 per channel) on a
tutorial page while its own spec page gives 255 flat. One domain says any
near-white fails and names #F9F9F9 and 254 254 255 as failing examples. Every
other domain says pure white, in words or as 255 255 255, and states no
tolerance. No domain accepts any value below 252. The consequence for a
retoucher is derived, not counted: RGB 255 255 255 is the only background value
that no published reading rejects. Item 10
partially breaks: the pool describes recommendation and content channels, and
no domain in the pool says the white image is used in search results. Item 12
breaks outright: no rejection notice was obtained, and none is described here.

## Cleared for use

Ready to paste. Each blockquote is followed by its source line, and every
source line states the method: how many sources, which languages, the date, and
that the value is modal rather than a primary reading. No vendor, agency or
publisher is named in any block. The page opens with the disclaimer block from
`../SPEC.md`, filled with "15 independent sources" (or "18" where a reused brief
01 row leads) and "10 September 2026".

**1. The asset exists, and it is its own deliverable.**

> Tmall's white-background image is published as a deliverable in its own
> right, not as a styled main image dropped onto white. Fourteen of fifteen
> Chinese-language sources that address it give it a rule set separate from the
> other main images. Ten of fifteen place it in the fifth slot in general
> categories, as do eight of eighteen sources in a wider Tmall image collection.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources on the white-background image,
> fourteen Chinese-language and one English-language, collected 10 September
> 2026, counted by domain, with a separate eighteen-source Tmall image
> collection from the same date. Modal values with their agreement counts, not
> a reading from the platform.

**2. The white value, as published.**

> The most commonly published background for the white-background image is pure
> white. Eight of fifteen sources call for pure white, and every source that
> prints a number prints RGB 255 255 255. The number is recent: every source
> dated 2017 to 2024 describes pure white in words and prints no value, and all
> three that print the triplet are dated 2026.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language first,
> collected 10 September 2026, counted by domain and grouped by publication
> date. Modal value with its agreement count, not a reading from the platform.

**3. The sources do not agree on how close to white is close enough.**

> Thirteen of fifteen sources state no tolerance at all. One publishes a
> tolerance of plus or minus 3, counting 252 to 255 on each channel as passing,
> on a page whose own publisher gives 255 flat on its specification page. One
> says any near-white fails and describes a background at 254 254 255 as
> detectable. No source describes any value below 252 as acceptable.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, each published tolerance position counted separately. Contested
> modal position, reported as the spread rather than a single value, not a
> reading from the platform.

**4. What that means for a retoucher, stated as reasoning.**

> RGB 255 255 255 is the only background value that no published reading
> rejects. A background at 252 passes the one published tolerance and fails the
> one published zero-tolerance reading. Clipping the background to pure 255 is
> therefore the one choice the published record does not contradict. That is a
> conclusion drawn from the spread, not a platform rule.
> Source: derived from the published tolerance positions above, fifteen
> independent sources collected 10 September 2026, reasoning stated openly and
> resting on modal values rather than primary readings.

**5. The fifth slot is the general case, not every case.**

> Published category standards move the white-background image. A reproduction
> of the food standard puts it second. A 2017 tutorial puts it second for
> luggage, and apparel is published both as the fifth image (2017) and as the
> first (2026). On JD, every page naming a slot puts the white image first,
> against Tmall's fifth, five of five pages per platform with none
> contradicting.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources collected 10 September 2026,
> one source per category map, and a separate twenty-four-page JD and Tmall
> collection from the same date. Contested for each category map, consensus for
> the JD against Tmall slot, not a reading from either platform.

**6. What it forbids, where the sources agree most.**

> The prohibitions are the most consistent part of the published record. Nine
> of fifteen sources rule out text, watermarks, logos and promotional overlay.
> Seven rule out a model, six rule out shadows, six rule out spliced composites,

**7. The contact shadow is contested.**

> Whether a faint shadow directly under the product survives is not settled.
> Six of fifteen sources prohibit shadows with no exception. Two, both published
> in 2026, describe a light contact shadow as tolerated, and one of those two
> says the opposite on another of its own pages. Reflections and glare are
> named as prohibited on one source only.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, counted by domain with self-contradicting publishers noted.
> Contested modal position, not a reading from the platform.

**8. Fill guidance has changed shape, and the two shapes pull apart.**

> Published fill guidance for the white-background image comes in two
> generations. Five pages dated 2017 to 2024 say the product should fill the
> frame and leave no white margin. Three pages dated 2026 give a number instead,
> 70 to 80 percent of the frame, which leaves 20 to 30 percent white. The full
> numeric spread runs from 60 to 80 percent, and no source states where either
> version came from.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, grouped by publication date and reported as the spread rather
> than averaged. Contested for the figure, not a reading from the platform.

**9. A file weight repeated three times, from one origin.**

> The 38KB to 300KB file weight that circulates for the white-background image
> traces to a single origin. It appears on three of fifteen sources. The oldest
> is dated September 2017, and the two later pages repeat that page's size,
> format and prohibition list almost line for line. Other sources give a 3MB
> ceiling instead. Three pages restating one page are one data point, not three.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026, with each figure traced to its earliest dated appearance.
> Contested, originating once, not a reading from the platform.

**10. Where it is described as surfacing, and what failing it costs.**

> The white-background image is described as feeding recommendation and content
> channels on the Taobao app homepage. Eight of fifteen sources say so, with the
> channel names changing as the pages get newer. Six of fifteen say a listing
> without a compliant white image loses eligibility for those channels. No
> source in the collection says the image is used in search results, and none
> puts a number on the exposure lost.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese and English, collected
> 10 September 2026, counted by domain. Modal position with its agreement count,
> the absence stated with its count, not a reading from the platform.

**11. Failure causes, as a list, because nobody ranks them.**

> No published source ranks why white-background images fail. Three of fifteen

> duplicated product, and rough cutout edges. None says how often any of them
> occurs.
> Source: fifteen independent published sources, Chinese-language, collected 10
> September 2026. Stated as an unranked list with its count, not as a platform
> rejection taxonomy.
