## R1. What has to be true before anything is looked up

The piece cannot run unless each of these is either sourced or explicitly
declared unsourceable on the page:

1. India is genuinely ahead of China on English proficiency, measured by a
   published index with a stated method and a current date.
2. India has a structurally better overlap with a European working day than
   China does, and the arithmetic is checkable from published UTC offsets.
3. Both countries have higher-education systems at a scale that makes "talent
   pool" a real dimension, with a national figure for each.
4. Neither national statistics system publishes a design-graduate count, so
   "creative talent pool" cannot be quantified for either side.
5. China's proximity to manufacturing is measurable and large, with a figure
   that covers both countries from one instrument.
6. Both countries now have a named, dated personal data statute, and the two
   differ in a way a buyer can act on.
7. Creative labor cost is not comparable across the two on public statistics,
   and the article must say so rather than manufacture a ratio.
8. Film and studio infrastructure is not comparable across the two on public
   statistics either, because the two governments count different objects.

Items 4, 7 and 8 are the honest failures. They stay in the article as stated
gaps, which is the thing the ranking pages will not do.

## Where India wins, on this evidence

Stated first, because the piece fails its own premise otherwise.

1. **English.** 484 against 464, 74th against 86th, on the same index in the
   same year. Twenty points and twelve places. The honest footnote is that the
   index puts both countries in its low band and its sample is self-selected,
   so the gap is real and the absolute level is not a promise on either side.
2. **The European working day.** About 3.5 to 4.5 hours of live overlap with
   London against about 1 to 2 hours. That is not a preference, it is
   arithmetic, and it decides whether a review happens on a call or in an email
   thread overnight.
3. **Moving personal data out of the country.** India's framework permits
   outbound transfer unless the government restricts a destination. China's
   requires a regulator security assessment above named volume thresholds, or a
   standard contract, or certification. For a brand shipping personal data, or
   talent likenesses tied to identifiable people, India is the lighter regime.
4. **An established English-language production ecosystem.** Two decades of
   studios built specifically for English-language adaptation, artwork and
   retouch, in a market where the buyer's language is a working language. Every
   page on all four SERPs names India first and none evaluates China at all,
   which is itself the evidence for incumbency.
5. **Higher-education headroom in the discipline groupings creatives come
   from.** Arts is the single largest undergraduate grouping in India at 32.1
   percent of enrollment.

## Where the comparison cannot be made, and the article must say so

1. **Creative labor cost.** China publishes an industry-level annual wage for
   culture, sport and entertainment. India publishes a monthly earnings figure
   for all regular salaried workers with no creative breakout. Different unit,
   different universe, different collection method. Any ratio built from these
   two numbers is fabricated. Publish each figure attributed to its own
   instrument, and say plainly that no like-for-like creative wage comparison
   exists in public statistics.
2. **Design and creative graduate counts.** Neither national release publishes
   one. Total enrollment is comparable, 45.0 million against 48.46 million,
   though the years differ by one and the enrollment ratios differ sharply, 30
   percent against 60.80 percent. Creative graduate counts do not exist
   publicly on either side.
3. **Studio and stage capacity.** No public register of sound stages exists for
   either country. The nearest published numbers count different objects: China
   counts feature films produced and screens installed; India counts
   certifications issued, a category that includes shorts, promos and songs.
   612 against 17,431 is not a comparison, it is two different questions.
4. **Category proximity below the aggregate.** The 28.8 against 2.8 figure is
   all manufacturing. Nothing sourced here supports a per-vertical split for
   consumer electronics, beauty, fashion or appliances. The vertical table in
   the brief must be built from the aggregate plus reasoning that is labeled as
   reasoning, or cut.
