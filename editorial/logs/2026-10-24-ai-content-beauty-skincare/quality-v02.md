# AI content for beauty brands: skincare and claims

Use AI to build a campaign around the product. Keep the product’s appearance
and any result shown on skin tied to evidence, even when the image contains no
written claim.

By Cyril Drouin

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

## Where does AI content for beauty brands fit?

AI content for beauty brands can extend settings, casting, and campaign
variations, provided the finished image represents the product truthfully.
hubStudio combines generated work with product photography and human review.
Keep packaging, shades, and texture tied to approved references; use suitable
evidence for efficacy claims. A generated result is not proof.

Decide what each asset asks a buyer to believe. A bathroom scene sets a mood.
A cream disappearing into skin suggests absorption. A face with fewer visible
lines can suggest a result even without a before-and-after caption. For a
serum launch, commission the setting separately from any demonstration of what
happens after application.

| Asset | Production choice | Claim risk | Suggested sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Packshot | Photograph the current pack; mix with a generated setting if needed | Altered label, shape, quantity, or applicator | Product owner |
| Texture or shade swatch | Capture the real formula under controlled conditions | Invented finish, color, coverage, or viscosity | Product and claims reviewers |
| Ingredient visual | Generate a reviewed illustration | Implying an absent ingredient or an unsupported finished-product effect | Claims reviewer |
| Model campaign | Generate, shoot, or combine, with rights and disclosure review | Skin appearance may imply product performance | Creative and claims reviewers |
| On-skin result | Use a documented real observation appropriate to the claim | A photograph can still exaggerate or misrepresent results | Evidence owner |
| Before and after | Use comparable records backed by relevant evidence; never fabricate the result | False improvement or an unrepresentative outcome | Evidence owner and market reviewer |

The sign-off roles are recommendations, not statutory job titles. An asset
doesn’t always need a shoot, but it does need to represent the product
accurately. Review its characteristics as well as any implied effect. This
article describes production practice, not legal advice.

## What do the market rules change about an image?

Rules reviewed October 24, 2026. The market review should happen before the
creative team settles on a product demonstration. This table identifies the
rules used here; it is not an exhaustive market-clearance checklist.

| Market | Primary rule or guidance | What to check in the image |
|---|---|---|
| US | FDA intended-use definitions; FTC advertising substantiation | Does the picture imply treatment or a body-function change? Does evidence support the overall message? |
| EU | Cosmetics Regulation Article 20; common claims criteria | Do pictures imply characteristics or functions the product lacks? Does the evidence match the depicted effect? |
| UK | ASA/CAP beauty-filter guidance | Has a filter or retouch exaggerated the advertised product’s effect? |
| China | NMPA efficacy evaluation standard; local advertising guidance where applicable | Does the visual stay within the product’s supported claim and relevant registration or filing information? |

### US: decide whether the claim changes the product’s status

The FDA distinguishes cosmetics from drugs by intended use. Cleansing or
beautifying is different from treating disease or changing the body’s
structure or function. Advertising can help establish that intended use. An
animation suggesting that a cream rebuilds skin therefore deserves a
classification review, not just an art director’s approval.

> FDA’s definitions distinguish appearance-related cosmetic uses from drug
> uses involving disease or body structure and function. The method is a
> reading of its intended-use guidance, not a test of an individual product.
> Source: FDA, September 11, 2024.
> https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap

The FTC separately requires a reasonable basis for objective advertising
claims before the ad runs, including claims conveyed by implication. If the ad
communicates that testing proves a result, the support must match that
message. The team should review image, headline, and caption together.

> Objective advertising claims need prior substantiation, including implied
> claims. The method is a reading of the FTC’s policy on the reasonable-basis
> requirement and advertised levels of support.
> Source: Federal Trade Commission, November 23, 1984.
> https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation

### EU: pictures count as claims

Article 20 of the Cosmetics Regulation covers pictures as well as words. They
cannot imply characteristics or functions the product does not have. The
common criteria also connect claims to evidence and prevent a depiction of
performance from going beyond that support.

> Regulation 655/2013 sets six common criteria: legal compliance,
> truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, and informed
> decision-making. The method is a reading of its annex alongside Article 20
> of Regulation 1223/2009, which expressly includes pictures.
> Source: European Union, July 10, 2013, and November 30, 2009.
> https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32013R0655
> https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32009R1223

An ingredient’s documented properties do not automatically establish the same
effect in the finished formula. An illustrated ingredient passing through a
skin cross-section may imply more than the approved copy says. Give that
sequence to the claims reviewer before spending time on polished motion.

### UK: a filter can change the claim

The ASA’s beauty-filter rulings of February 3, 2021 addressed tanning ads in
which filters exaggerated the apparent product effect. Its subsequent guidance
does not prohibit every filter. It asks whether the alteration misleads about
what the advertised product can achieve.

> A beauty filter becomes a problem when it misleadingly exaggerates product
> efficacy. The method is a reading of CAP’s explanation of the ASA’s
> February 2021 rulings, not a blanket prohibition on retouching.
> Source: ASA/CAP, February 11, 2021.
> https://www.asa.org.uk/news/the-mis-use-of-social-media-beauty-filters-when-advertising-cosmetic-products.html

For production, preserve the source footage and record changes to skin tone or
texture. Removing a distracting object is a different decision from smoothing
the very lines a moisturizer is advertised to improve.

### China: connect the asset to the claim’s evidence

The NMPA efficacy evaluation standard calls for scientific support for
cosmetic efficacy claims. Evaluation methods depend on the claim. Certain
sensory and physical-effect claims qualify for an exemption from publishing
the evidence summary; that does not make a fabricated visual result true.

> The NMPA standard took effect May 1, 2021, with different summary-upload
> deadlines for products registered or filed at different times. The method is a reading
> of the complete notice and standard republished by the Yunnan drug
> regulator, including the summary exemptions.
> Source: NMPA notice published April 9, 2021; official republication
> December 22, 2021.
> https://mpa.yn.gov.cn/zfxxgk/zcwj/qtwj/gjwj/202112/t20211222_1224866.html

Beijing’s cosmetics advertising guidance makes the connection explicit:
efficacy and safety advertising should agree with the relevant registration or
filing information and evidence summary. That is local guidance, not a new
nationwide platform rule.

> Beijing’s guidance links efficacy and safety advertising to the product
> record and supporting summary. The method is a reading of the guidance’s
> content-accuracy provisions and its stated Beijing scope.
> Source: Beijing market and drug regulators, June 27, 2025.
> https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/zwxx/2024zcwj/202506/t20250627_4129056.html

Beauty content production in China covers the local production context in more
depth. Do not carry an approved claim into another market merely by
translating its caption.

## Do synthetic models need a separate disclosure review?

Yes. Review whether the person must be identified as synthetic separately from
whether the product message is true. The requirements depend on where the
asset appears and what kind of performer it contains.

Amazon’s official listing and A+ announcement requires applicable media
containing photorealistic AI-generated people to carry the
`contains-synthetic-performer` keyword in the XMP `dc:subject` field before
upload. The announcement excludes, among other categories, real people who
were merely altered using AI. It is not a rule to label every edited bottle.

> Amazon’s announcement specifies a metadata tag for photorealistic
> AI-generated people in listings and A+ content across its worldwide stores.
> The method is a reading of the official announcement and tagging guide.
> Source: Amazon Seller Central, July 27, 2026; guide checked October 24, 2026.
> https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/ed53566e-9add-4008-a7bd-2bca6f600fee?mons_sel_locale=en_GB
> https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GFXHCHYZRGJRBZA5?locale=en-US

New York’s rule is already in effect. Its covered ad creators must
conspicuously disclose synthetic performers when they have actual knowledge of
their use. Its definition concerns a human-like visual or audiovisual
performer who is not recognizable as an identifiable natural person;
audio-only ads and specified other uses have exclusions.

> New York’s synthetic-performer disclosure rule took effect June 9, 2026.
> The method is a reading of General Business Law 396-b and the governor’s
> dated commencement announcement.
> Source: New York State, June 9, 2026; statute checked October 24, 2026.
> https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/396-B
> https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai

California’s forthcoming rule covers prominently featured synthetic performers
and requires clear disclosure. Its definition includes voice, so do not assume
an audio ad has the same treatment as in New York.

> California’s SB 1050 adds Business and Professions Code 17610. Its
> January 1, 2027 effective date follows from the September 16, 2026 enactment
> and the constitutional default for this regular-session statute. The
> method is a reading of the chaptered law and that effective-date rule.
> Source: California Legislature, September 16, 2026; Article IV, section 8,
> in the legislature’s August 2022 handbook.
> https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1050
> https://www.senate.ca.gov/sites/senate.ca.gov/files/joint_handbook_2021-22_final.pdf

Platform metadata and a conspicuous disclosure within an ad are separate
delivery requirements. Check the applicable exclusions before selecting the
final wording. EU AI Act labeling for brand content addresses the separate
European framework. Disclosing how an image was made leaves the product claim
to be checked against its evidence.

## Which assets belong in each channel’s brief?

Decide where the image will appear before requesting variations. A model
portrait used for casting in a brand film may suggest a product result when it
sits beside an efficacy claim on a shopping page. Show the reviewer that
placement, not just the image file.

| Channel | Suggested asset set | Keep attached to the handoff |
|---|---|---|
| Product page or marketplace | Current pack, actual texture or shade references, reviewed use demonstration | Product version, evidence references, applicable synthetic-person metadata |
| Paid social | Campaign scene, product reveal, approved claim variant | Exact caption, disclosure, destination, and market approval |
| Organic social | Routine context, ingredient illustration, application education | Approved directions and claim wording; review of the implied result |
| Brand website or launch film | Campaign setting, reviewed product transformation, cutdowns | Source references, explanation of simulated elements, and cleared claims |

Amazon platform page if that marketplace is part of the launch. Insights has

## What should the reviewer receive before approving a batch?

The reviewer needs to compare the finished visual with the product and the
evidence. Start with current pack photography and identify the formula or
variant. Add the approved wording for the intended market, with the study or
evidence reference that supports it. A general assurance that someone checked
the claim leaves the reviewer unable to make that comparison.

For a swatch, include the real reference and the capture conditions. For an
on-skin result, explain the observation and any image processing. A real photo
can still be misleading if lighting, pose, makeup, or the selection of
subjects changes the impression of performance. Photography records a frame;
it does not by itself establish a product’s typical effect.

Give the claims reviewer the final crop, including its caption. Include
on-image wording too. Removing context can change what the viewer believes, so
record the approved version and return to review when an edit changes the
product effect or claim.

Check the exported file too.

Where the destination requires metadata, inspect the delivery copy; a resize
or format conversion is another point to check that the tag remains. Keep the
source reference and review record with that copy.

## Questions beauty teams ask before commissioning

### Can beauty brands use AI-generated images in ads?

Yes, subject to the rules for the market, placement, product, and message.
Generation can provide a campaign setting or an illustrative scene, but the
finished ad must not mislead about the product. Review skin appearance,
packaging, and implied results alongside the copy, then check any applicable
synthetic-performer disclosure requirement.

### Can I show AI-generated before and after skincare results?

Do not present an invented transformation as a person’s real result or as
proof of efficacy. A synthetic comparison does not establish what the formula
achieves. If the campaign needs a result demonstration, build it around
appropriate evidence and comparable real records, and have the market reviewer
assess the complete presentation before release.

### Do AI models in beauty ads need to be disclosed?

Some uses do. New York has a synthetic-performer advertising rule, California
has enacted a forthcoming rule, and Amazon requires metadata for applicable
listing and A+ media. Their scopes differ. Check where the ad reaches
consumers, whether the performer meets the definition, and which disclosure
must survive the final export and upload.

### Is AI skin retouching allowed in cosmetics advertising?

There is no universal permission for skin retouching. The UK guidance asks
whether an alteration exaggerates the advertised product’s effect; other
markets also assess truthful claims and supporting evidence. Keep the
unaltered source, explain changes to the reviewer, and avoid smoothing or
recoloring that changes the result the product is supposed to deliver.

### What beauty content can AI make well?

Useful assignments include campaign environments, reviewed ingredient
illustrations, model-led concepts, and adaptations built around approved
product photography. Treat these as creative tasks with review, not a promise
of perfect product fidelity. A shade, label, applicator, or texture that
affects purchase expectations needs checking against the real product before
the asset leaves the team.

### How do cosmetics claims rules differ in the EU and the US?

In the US, intended use helps determine whether a product is a cosmetic, a
drug, or both, while FTC rules govern advertising substantiation. EU cosmetics
rules expressly address pictures and apply common claims criteria. Neither
system treats a plausible generated image as evidence of efficacy. Check the
product’s status and the exact visual message for each market.
